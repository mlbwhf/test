## A working template — the Assumption Audit

Run this every six months for any business unit or product line. Two hours. Cross-functional team of six to eight people. One-page output.

| Step | Question | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | List every assumption your current strategy depends on. | A list of 15-30 assumptions, written one to a sticky note. |
| 2 | For each, mark whether it is verified (V), inherited (I), or aspirational (A). | Colored markings on each sticky. |
| 3 | Of the I and A assumptions, which would be most expensive to be wrong about? Rank top 5. | A prioritized list of 5 untested beliefs. |
| 4 | For each, design the smallest experiment that would test the assumption. | Five experiments, each sized to be runnable in one quarter. |
| 5 | Pick the two highest-leverage experiments. Run them. | Two scheduled experiments with owner, budget, and 90-day deadline. |

The Assumption Audit is the single most useful exercise in this chapter. It is also the most uncomfortable. Most of the assumptions you uncover will turn out to be inherited rather than verified, and that discovery, in front of your team, is humbling. That is the point.
