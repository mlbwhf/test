# Case Study: Elevate Labs

**Type:** Positive · **Status:** v1.0-draft (composite/illustrative — flag for owner review)
**Profile:** ~450-person B2B software company, design and collaboration tools

> Case studies in this directory illustrate the framework in use. Names and
> details are composites drawn from field experience and the companion
> books; they are teaching cases, not audited accounts.

## Starting position

Elevate Labs first ran the Mutation Readiness Scorecard as a team scan in
Q1: **total 34/72 — Mutation-Aware**, with a profile of Signal Detection 8,
Decision Velocity 4, Experimentation Capacity 6, AI Fluency 5, Structural
Plasticity 4, Leadership Posture 7.

The team scan spread was itself the first finding: the CEO scored the
company 12 points higher than the VP of Engineering. The gap was widest on
Decision Velocity — the CEO believed decisions were fast because *her*
decisions were fast.

## What they did (three quarters)

**Quarter 1 — Cadence and Capital.** Installed the weekly signal review
(30 minutes, Mondays, product ops as owner). Moved 15% of the product
budget from annual line items into a rolling experiment pool with
pre-agreed kill criteria per bet (risk-assessment canvas, every time).

**Quarter 2 — Configuration.** The signal review surfaced a converging
signal: three enterprise customers independently asked whether Elevate's
collaboration features would survive AI-generated design workflows. Rather
than commission a strategy deck, they stood up a 5-person team in 11 days
(their previous best was 7 weeks) in the *Process / Transform* cell of the
4×4 matrix: rebuild the review workflow assuming AI drafts the first pass.

**Quarter 3 — Conviction.** Two of the pool's six experiments hit kill
criteria and were stopped on schedule. The CEO presented one of the killed
experiments — her own pet idea — at the all-hands, including what it cost
and what it taught. LP1 ("leaders publicly change positions") moved from a
team-scan median of 2 to 4 the following quarter.

## Result

Q4 reassessment: **51/72 — Mutation-Ready** (threshold, and they'd say so
themselves). Decision Velocity 4→9, Structural Plasticity 4→8. The
AI-native review workflow shipped to beta with 40 customers; more
importantly, the *mechanism* that produced it — signal → pool → capped team
→ ship or kill — ran twice more that year without executive sponsorship.

## What made it work

1. **They fixed the weakest dimension first** (Decision Velocity), not the
   most fashionable one (AI Fluency). AI Fluency followed almost for free
   once experiments could be funded and staffed quickly.
2. **Kill criteria were public.** Stopping a project cost no one status,
   which is the only condition under which kill criteria get honored.
3. **The CEO went first on Conviction.** The framework's least delegable
   lever was pulled by the only person who could pull it.
