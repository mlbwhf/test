# Innovation Framework Scorecard

**Version 1.0-draft** · 15 questions · 5 principles · ~12 minutes
**Source:** the Innovators Framework — Part I of *Mutation Readiness: An Operating Manual for Innovation in the Age of AI*

> Machine-readable source of truth: [`innovation-framework-scorecard.yaml`](innovation-framework-scorecard.yaml)

## What this measures

The companion to the [Mutation Readiness Scorecard](mutation-readiness-scorecard.md).
Mutation Readiness measures whether your organization can sense and respond
to AI-age change; this scorecard measures whether you have the **cultural
operating system underneath that lets innovation happen at all**. Most
organizations should run this one first — an organization in Innovation
Theatre will fail at Mutation Readiness regardless of what it installs.

## How to score

Rate each question 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree); N/A
allowed. **Principle score** = sum of three answers (max 15). **Total** =
sum of five principles (max 75). If any items are N/A, normalize to a
percentage.

| Band | Score | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Innovation Theatre** | 0–30 | The language of innovation without the practices. Work is performed for management or external audiences, not lived in daily decisions. |
| **Innovation Practicing** | 31–55 | The rituals exist; the organization hasn't absorbed them into how decisions are made. The biggest gap is usually between what leadership says and what the performance system rewards. |
| **Innovation Native** | 56–75 | Innovation is identity, not department. The risk is now complacency rather than absence. |

## The questions

### Principle 1 — Fail Forward (Grit & Agility)
*Structured experiments, intelligent failure, fast recovery.*

- **Q1.1** Does your team run small, structured experiments with explicit hypotheses and pre-defined learning goals — not just "let's try it and see"?
- **Q1.2** When something fails, do you have a structured way to debrief it (Failure Harvest, post-mortem) that surfaces what the team believed before, what they believe now, and what to do next — without blame?
- **Q1.3** Is iteration and perseverance publicly recognized in your performance system, not just successful outcomes?

### Principle 2 — Open Communication & Candid Feedback
*Psychological safety combined with radical candor — either one alone collapses.*

- **Q2.1** Do team members feel safe to challenge ideas — including ideas from leadership — without political consequence?
- **Q2.2** Is feedback typically shared in real time, rather than saved for annual reviews?
- **Q2.3** When senior leaders make decisions employees disagree with, do those employees say so — and is the disagreement engaged seriously?

### Principle 3 — Challenging Mental Models
*Questioning assumptions you have been treating as facts.*

- **Q3.1** Does your team regularly question fundamental assumptions about your industry, customers, or business model — in real decisions, not workshop exercises?
- **Q3.2** When you face a hard problem, does someone on the team know how to use first-principles reasoning to break it down to underlying physical or economic facts?
- **Q3.3** Has your team explicitly retired a "sacred cow" — a practice everyone assumed was non-negotiable — in the past 12 months?

### Principle 4 — Flexible Structure / Agile Decision-Making
*Decisions close to the work; information across boundaries faster than up the chain.*

- **Q4.1** Are teams aligned to customer outcomes or value streams, rather than to internal functions?
- **Q4.2** Do teams have decision-making autonomy without escalation for most operational choices in their area?
- **Q4.3** When something needs to change structurally, does it happen within a quarter, or does it take a year of approvals?

### Principle 5 — Growth Mindset at Scale
*How the organization rewards effort and learning vs talent and outcomes.*

- **Q5.1** Does your performance review system explicitly reward how an employee helped others grow — with comparable weight to delivery metrics?
- **Q5.2** Do employees have dedicated, protected time for self-directed learning, experimentation, or curiosity-driven work?
- **Q5.3** When senior leaders fail or change their minds, do they share what they learned, publicly, more than once a year?

## Rapid mode

Five questions — the strongest single signal per principle: Q1.2, Q2.1,
Q3.3, Q4.3, Q5.1. Score out of 25. Bands: 0–10 Theatre, 11–18 Practicing,
19–25 Native.

## After scoring

The work program targets the **weakest one or two principles**, never all
five at once — five concurrent culture initiatives is the failure mode of
every culture change. Band-specific playbooks (including the
smallest-possible practice to install per principle) are in the
[Claude Code skill](../claude-skills/innovation-framework-assessment.md),
which also runs the assessment conversationally and writes a
30-60-90-day plan.

If you score above 55 — or above 40 with momentum — run the
[Mutation Readiness Scorecard](mutation-readiness-scorecard.md) next:
the cultural foundation is necessary but not sufficient for the AI age.
