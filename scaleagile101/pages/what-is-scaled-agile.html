Chapter 1

What is Scaled Agile?

Agile for one team is simple. Agile for fifty teams building one product needs a framework. Here's the plain-English version.

Scaled Agile is the practice of applying Agile principles — small batches, fast feedback, continuous improvement — across many teams working toward a shared goal. The Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe®) is the most widely adopted approach, giving large organizations a common language, set of roles, and planning rhythm so dozens or hundreds of people can deliver as one.

Why "scale" Agile at all?

A single Scrum team of 7–9 people can self-organize around a backlog and a two-week sprint. But enterprise products are built by many teams at once, and that creates problems a single team never faces: overlapping dependencies, conflicting priorities, integration surprises, and no shared view of progress. Scaling frameworks exist to solve exactly those coordination problems — without throwing away the Agile values that made teams effective in the first place.

How SAFe is organized: four levels

Team

The familiar Agile team using Scrum or Kanban to deliver in short iterations. Everything else is built on top of healthy teams.

Program (the Agile Release Train)

5–12 teams (50–125 people) organized into an Agile Release Train (ART) that plans and ships together on a fixed cadence. This is the heart of SAFe.

Large Solution

For very big products, multiple ARTs coordinate as a Solution Train to build one large solution together.

Portfolio

Connects strategy to execution — funding value streams, governing with Lean budgets, and aligning delivery to business goals.

The mindset underneath it

  • Lean thinking — maximize value, minimize waste and delay.
  • Flow — keep work moving in small batches instead of big, risky releases.
  • Alignment — everyone plans together so priorities are shared, not assumed.
  • Relentless improvement — inspect and adapt at every level, every cycle.
