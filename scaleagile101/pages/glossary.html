Scaled Agile glossary

The terms and acronyms you'll hear most often, in plain English.

Agile Release Train (ART)

A long-lived team of 5–12 Agile teams (50–125 people) that plans and delivers value together on a fixed cadence.

Program Increment (PI)

A timebox (typically 8–12 weeks) during which an ART delivers value, bookended by PI Planning and Inspect & Adapt.

PI Planning

The cadence-based event where the whole ART plans the next PI together, aligning on objectives and dependencies.

Release Train Engineer (RTE)

The servant leader who facilitates ART events and keeps the train aligned and flowing — the "chief Scrum Master" of the train.

Product Owner (PO)

Owns and prioritizes a single team's backlog so the team always builds the most valuable item next.

Product Management

Owns the ART backlog and vision — decides what the train builds across all its teams.

Iteration / Sprint

A short, fixed period (usually two weeks) in which a team plans, builds, and reviews a slice of work.

System Demo

A demo of the full, integrated system increment — the most objective measure of an ART's progress.

Inspect & Adapt (I&A)

The end-of-PI event combining a demo, quantitative review, and a structured problem-solving workshop.

WSJF

Weighted Shortest Job First — a way to prioritize work by dividing its value and urgency by its size.

Value Stream

The sequence of steps an organization uses to deliver value to a customer — ARTs are organized around them.

Lean Portfolio Management

Connecting strategy to execution with Lean budgets and lightweight governance at the portfolio level.
