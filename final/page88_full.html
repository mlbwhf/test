Five dimensions of repeatable innovation

Most innovation programs fail because they treat innovation as an event. The Innovation Framework treats it as a system — five connected dimensions that compound.

D1 · Strategy — where innovation must win

Pick the few bets that matter. Define horizon-1/2/3 ambitions, kill criteria, and the strategic theses each portfolio bet is meant to validate.

D2 · Portfolio — staged investment, not annual budgets

Treat innovation bets like a venture portfolio. Stage-gate investment, persistent funding, and the governance pattern that keeps the portfolio honest.

D3 · Process — how bets move from idea to revenue

The end-to-end pipeline from problem discovery through validated learning, MVP, scale-up, and integration into the core business.

D4 · Culture — the conditions innovation requires

Permission to test. Safety to be wrong. Talent rotation between core and explore. The culture levers leaders actually control.

D5 · Metrics — measuring innovation honestly

Innovation accounting. Leading indicators that predict revenue. Lagging indicators that prove it. The scorecard that survives a CFO conversation.

FAQ

We already run hackathons and an innovation lab. Why do we need this?

Hackathons produce ideas. Labs produce demos. Neither produces a stage-gated portfolio with kill criteria, an executive scorecard, and a handoff into the core business. The Framework connects all five dimensions so innovation becomes recurring revenue rather than a marketing line item.

What roles benefit most?

Heads of Innovation, Strategy, R&D, and Corporate Venturing. Also senior product and portfolio leaders who own the bet pipeline.

How is this different from SAFe or other agile frameworks?

SAFe scales the delivery of known work. The Innovation Framework manages the discovery and validation of unknown work — the bets that turn into next-decade revenue. The two are complementary, not competing.
