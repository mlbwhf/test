Analysis

The state of play: one default, three forces redrawing the map

Measured by raw traffic, the AI world looks unipolar: ChatGPT holds about 53.9% of global chatbot web visits and 1.1 billion monthly users, with Gemini second (~662M users) and Claude third (~245M). In Europe ChatGPT's share runs above 70% — higher than in the US itself (61.7%). But the national maps tell a different story, because three forces override the default wherever they apply.

Force one: access and regulation. China blocks Western frontier models outright, so its market is a parallel universe led by ByteDance's Doubao (382M MAU), Alibaba's Qianwen (167M, up 58-fold in a year) and DeepSeek (130M). Russia's sanctions-constrained market produced the map's strangest result: Perplexity holds ~48% share — not because Russians prefer answer engines, but because it remained accessible while rivals restricted service — alongside state-backed GigaChat and YandexGPT.

Force two: ecosystem and price. Where the query mix skews toward everyday personal tasks — and OpenAI's own data shows non-work use climbing from 59.8% to 68.9% of all messages — distribution and cost beat benchmark scores. Doubao wins China partly because it lives inside Douyin. Gemini is gaining fastest in price-sensitive markets like India because it bills in rupees over UPI, ships free tiers through AI Studio, and comes pre-installed on Android — the only frontier assistant that meets a $3-ARPU market on its own terms.

Force three: language. In markets whose languages frontier models serve poorly, local champions hold the line: Naver's HyperCLOVA X in Korea (48.9 on the KoBALT-700 Korean benchmark vs 32.4 for Alibaba's QwQ — and Naver still owns ~63% of Korean search), Fugaku-LLM in Japan's public sector, Sarvam in India's sovereign program. The pattern across all three forces: the model that wins a country is the one that fits its query mix — frontier English models win research-and-writing queries, ecosystem apps win daily-utility queries, and local models win local-language queries.

Key takeaways
  • ChatGPT is the default, not the universal. ~54% of global visits, 70%+ in Europe — but zero in China and a minority in Russia and Korea's local-language use.
  • The query mix picks the winner. As usage shifts personal (68.9% non-work), ecosystem reach and price matter more than benchmark rank.
  • Growth has moved to Asia. Korea (+43.2%), Thailand (+36.2%) and Japan (+34.1%) are growing AI usage ~2× faster than the US (+19%).

Why: the query-type evidence

The "why" behind each map line becomes visible in what people actually ask. OpenAI's own usage research divides prompts into three intents — asking (49%: seeking information and advice), doing (40%: producing output — writing, code, plans), and expressing (11%: companionship, reflection) — and finds non-work use rising from 59.8% to 68.9% of all messages by March 2026. The growth data confirms the shift of gravity: between H1 2025 and Q1 2026, AI usage grew +43.2% in South Korea, +36.2% in Thailand, +34.1% in Japan — versus +19% in the US. The next hundred million users are Asian, mobile-first, and largely personal-use — which is precisely the query profile where ecosystem, price, and language beat raw benchmark scores. That is why Gemini's international footprint (strongest in East Asia) and China's domestic apps are growing faster than the category leader.

Three deep dives

China's parallel market. China's AI-native apps reached ~499M monthly users in mid-2026 (QuestMobile). Doubao leads on distribution, not benchmarks; Qianwen's 58-fold surge shows Alibaba converting commerce reach into AI usage; and DeepSeek is the paradox — its consumer app is shrinking even as its open-weight models spread worldwide as infrastructure. Full country picture: AI in China 2026.

India, the swing state. The world's #2 ChatGPT market (198M users) is also the market where Gemini's economics bite hardest — native rupee billing over UPI, free Flash tiers, Android defaults. Whichever model wins India's price-sensitive, multilingual, mobile-first query mix will bank the largest user gain of the decade; the IndiaAI Mission's Sarvam adds a sovereign wildcard.

Korea, proof that language wins. Korea is the only open, sanctions-free, wealthy market where a local model beats the global default in daily use — because HyperCLOVA X is measurably better in Korean and ships inside Naver, which still owns ~63% of search. It is the template every sovereign-AI program (tracked here) is trying to copy.

FAQ

What is the most popular AI model in the world in 2026?

ChatGPT, with ~53.9% of global chatbot web visits and ~1.1 billion monthly users — followed by Gemini (~662M) and Claude (~245M). But it is banned from the #2 AI market (China) and a minority player in Russia and Korean-language use.

What is the most popular AI model in China?

ByteDance's Doubao, at ~382M monthly active users (May 2026), ahead of Alibaba's Qianwen (~167M) and DeepSeek (~130M). Western models are blocked.

Why does Perplexity dominate Russia?

It is a sanctions artifact: Perplexity remained accessible while competitors restricted Russian access, giving it ~48% share — alongside domestic GigaChat and YandexGPT.

Which countries prefer local AI models over ChatGPT?

China (by policy), Korea (by language quality — HyperCLOVA X), Russia (by sanctions and state backing), with Japan, India and the Gulf running sovereign models in public-sector niches.

What do people prompt AI most, and does it differ by country?

Globally, coding is still the top prompt category (29%, down from 44% as mainstream use grows), with history/society second and finance the fastest-growing. By country the differences are stark: 45.2% of India's AI use is software tasks (#1 worldwide), while high-adoption countries like the US and Singapore spread prompts across education, science, business and personal life.

Which AI model is growing fastest internationally?

Gemini has the most internationally distributed user base with particular strength in East Asia, powered by Android distribution and aggressive pricing; the fastest-growing usage countries are Korea (+43.2%), Thailand (+36.2%) and Japan (+34.1%).
