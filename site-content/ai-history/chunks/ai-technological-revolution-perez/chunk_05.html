5. Infrastructure and finance

The money looks like an installation-period frenzy, but relative to the economy it is still smaller than the railway mania

Every surge built an infrastructure, and AI's is GPU data centres plus the power to run them. Capital spending by the five largest cloud and platform companies (Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Oracle) rose from about $162 billion in 2022 to about $448 billion in 2025, by Report AI's sum of Epoch AI figures (Exhibit 3), and the big four guided to $720–740 billion for 2026. Measured against the US economy, the 2025 figure is about 1.5% of GDP (Report AI calculation), above US telecom capex at the 2000 peak (about 1.0–1.2% of GDP) but far below UK railway investment in 1847, about 7% of national income (Exhibit 4).

The financial signals Perez associates with frenzy are present. AI companies took about 46% of US venture deal value in 2024, 65% in 2025 and 83% in January–September 2026, according to PitchBook data cited by our finance pages (Exhibit 5). The 'Magnificent Seven' reached nearly 35% of the S&P 500's value by December 2025, up from about 20% in November 2022, according to the Bank for International Settlements. Supplier and circular financing appeared: Nvidia signed a letter of intent in September 2025 to invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI, later replaced by a $30 billion equity stake. Hyperscaler bond issuance reached about $194 billion in the first half of 2026.

So did the revenue gap. Big-four capex was about 60 times the combined revenue of OpenAI and Anthropic in 2024, about 23 times in 2025 and about 13 times in 2026 on guidance (Report AI calculation). The gap is closing quickly because revenue is growing quickly: the two labs' combined annualised run-rate rose from about $6.5 billion in December 2024 to about $115 billion in the third quarter of 2026. The IMF noted in October 2025 that the boom was largely equity- and cash-funded, which makes a bust less likely to be systemic; Perez's own concern is the opposite tendency, with world debt at almost four times global GDP.
