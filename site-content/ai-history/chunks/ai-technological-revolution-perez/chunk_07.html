Frequently asked

Who is Carlota Perez?

Carlota Perez is a Venezuelan-British economist and scholar of technology and socio-economic development, known for Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital: The Dynamics of Bubbles and Golden Ages (Edward Elgar, 2002). Her framework of five technological surges since 1771 is widely used by economists and investors.

What are Carlota Perez's five technological revolutions?

The Industrial Revolution (from 1771), the age of steam and railways (1829), the age of steel, electricity and heavy engineering (1875), the age of oil, the automobile and mass production (1908), and the age of information and telecommunications (1971), each dated from a 'big bang' event.

Does Carlota Perez think AI is a new technological revolution?

No. In a March 2024 Project Syndicate essay she wrote that AI 'is better understood as a key development within the still-evolving information-communications-technology (ICT) revolution, which started in the 1970s with the microprocessor', while allowing that it could lay the groundwork for a sixth revolution.

Is AI in a bubble, according to Perez's framework?

Perez's framework predicts a financial frenzy and a crash during the installation of any revolution, and in August 2025 she told the Financial Times, 'I have not seen a golden age happening without a crash.' Report AI's data shows frenzy-style signals, such as AI taking about 72% of global venture funding in January–September 2026, but relative to the economy the investment is smaller than past manias.

How big is AI investment compared with the railway mania?

UK railway investment reached about 7% of national income at the 1847 peak, according to figures Perez cites. The five largest US tech companies' capital expenditure was about $448 billion in 2025, about 1.5% of US GDP by Report AI's calculation, and not all of it was for AI.

Methodology & sources

Figures are compiled from company disclosures, regulators, research firms and reputable reporting, and cross-checked against Report AI's own index pages. Each value carries its as-of date, source and a confidence rating: HIGH where the primary source was read, MEDIUM where it rests on consistent reputable secondary reporting (most figures, because many primary pages cannot be opened from our research environment), DERIVED where it is our own arithmetic, and CONTESTED where credible sources disagree. A year means the calendar year the figure describes; 2026 values are the latest available and marked “to date”. Weekly and monthly user counts, and forecasts and outcomes, are never compared as if they were the same measure. This report applies the framework of Carlota Perez (Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital, 2002, and later papers and interviews on carlotaperez.org) to AI. Every Perez quotation was checked against the text of the source named, mostly the author's own PDFs; the Financial Times interview was read through its Irish Times syndication. The key-factor test, the three readings, the scorecard, the capex-to-GDP shares, the capex-to-revenue ratios and the big-bang-to-crash intervals are Report AI analysis and calculations (DERIVED), not Perez's classifications. Market data come from Report AI's State of AI data and the AI Bubble Tracker, with sources in the data download. These reports compile and verify secondary data with original analysis; they are not original surveys. See our editorial standards, corrections log and fact-check register.
