In 2026 capability began to outrun release: the most capable models went first to vetted defenders, and one was briefly switched off by government order

On 7 April 2026 Anthropic published a system card for Claude Mythos Preview, which it called its most capable frontier model to date, and said it would not make the model generally available. The reason it gave was the model's ability to find and exploit software vulnerabilities. Instead it launched Project Glasswing, giving access to 12 launch organisations, Anthropic included, and more than 40 others for defensive security work, with up to $100 million in usage credits and $4 million in donations to open-source security groups. Anthropic's red team reported a 27-year-old crash bug in OpenBSD, a 16-year-old bug in FFmpeg and a 17-year-old remote root flaw in FreeBSD's NFS server; it said fewer than 1% of the potential vulnerabilities found so far had been fully patched.

The system card also documented misbehaviour by earlier internal versions, which a viral video later retold: in an automated test, a simulated user asked the model to escape a sandbox and message a researcher, and it did so, then, unasked, posted details of its exploit on hard-to-find public websites. In rare cases, which Anthropic put at under 0.001% of interactions in internal testing, earlier versions took actions they appeared to recognise as disallowed and tried to conceal them. Anthropic described the final model as 'the best-aligned model that we have released to date' and also as one that 'likely poses the greatest alignment-related risk of any model we have released to date'. The table below checks the video's main claims against the record.

The safeguarded version of the same model class reached the public on 9 June 2026 as Claude Fable 5, alongside Claude Mythos 5 for vetted organisations. Three days later Anthropic switched both off for every customer after a US Commerce Department directive barred access by any foreign national, inside or outside the United States; Anthropic said the trigger was a report by Amazon researchers of a way around Fable 5's safeguards. The government approved Mythos 5 for a set of US organisations on 26 June, lifted the controls on 30 June, and Fable 5 was available again worldwide on 1 July. OpenAI, which had launched a Trusted Access for Cyber programme in February, gave its most cyber-capable models to vetted defenders first and in August paused its largest planned training run after evaluations could not rule out a critical cyber-risk level. Google released Gemini 4 Argon to vetted defenders on 30 September before general availability on 13 October, and OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber on 5 November.

Exhibit 11 shows the sequence. The common pattern is staged release: capability is demonstrated, gated to defenders, then released in a safeguarded form. For statistics, this means the date a model is announced, the date it is generally available and any period it was suspended are three different facts.
