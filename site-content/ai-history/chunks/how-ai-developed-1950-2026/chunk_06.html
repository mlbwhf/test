4. 2017–2026: the scale era

Since 2010 the compute used to train leading models has grown about 4–5× a year, and the cost of a frontier run has reached hundreds of millions of dollars

Epoch AI, which maintains the most widely used database of notable models, estimates that training compute doubled roughly every 20 months before 2010 and about every six months afterwards, which is equivalent to growth of about 4–5× a year; for frontier language models since 2020 its estimate is about 5× a year. In 2018 OpenAI's 'AI and Compute' analysis put the growth from AlexNet to AlphaGo Zero at more than 300,000-fold.

Exhibit 3 plots landmark models on a logarithmic scale, where each gridline is a tenfold increase. GPT-3 (May 2020) used about 3.1×10²³ FLOP; GPT-4 (March 2023) about 2.1×10²⁵; Grok 3 (February 2025) was the first model estimated above 10²⁶; and Epoch's estimate for GPT-6 Astra (September 2026) is about 9.4×10²⁶, roughly 45 times GPT-4. These are estimates: labs have disclosed less since 2023, and Stanford HAI reports that the average Foundation Model Transparency Index score fell from 58 to 40.

Cost has followed compute. On Epoch AI's amortised basis (hardware depreciation plus energy, in 2023 dollars), the final training run of AlexNet in 2012 cost about $16, the original transformer about $440, GPT-3 about $2.1 million, GPT-4 about $37 million and Grok 4 about $390 million (Exhibit 4). Priced at cloud-rental rates, the basis the Stanford HAI AI Index uses, the same runs cost roughly two to four times more: about $81 million for GPT-4 and about $850 million for Grok 3, and Epoch's headline estimate for Grok 4 is about $490 million. Epoch estimates that the cost of frontier language-model training has grown about 3.5× a year since 2020. All these figures cover the final training run only, not research, staff, data or failed runs. That is also why DeepSeek's widely quoted $5.6 million for DeepSeek-V3 (Epoch's amortised estimate is about $5.4 million) describes one run, not the cost of building the model.

Parameter counts tell a less reliable story. GPT-3 had 175 billion parameters; the largest disclosed open models now exceed a trillion in total (DeepSeek V4-Pro, 1.6 trillion), but mixture-of-experts designs activate only a fraction per token, and most closed labs no longer publish the figure. The often-quoted 1.76 trillion for GPT-4 has never been confirmed by OpenAI.
