At a glance
  • Artificial intelligence has gone through three booms and two winters since the field was named in a proposal dated 31 August 1955. The current boom, which began with deep learning in 2012, is the first financed mainly by companies and revenue rather than government grants.
  • Epoch AI estimates that the compute used to train notable AI models grew about 4–5× a year after 2010, against a doubling roughly every 20 months before it. From the 1957 Perceptron to GPT-6 Astra in 2026 the increase is about 21 orders of magnitude (Report AI calculation).
  • Capability tests now fall in months rather than decades. ImageNet took five years to pass its human baseline (2010–2015); SWE-bench Verified went from 49% in late 2024 to 97.0% in September 2026.
  • The cheapest price for GPT-4-level output fell from $37.50 per million tokens at GPT-4's launch in March 2023 to about $0.20 in October 2026, a 187-fold drop (Report AI calculation), while ChatGPT passed 1 billion weekly users on 31 August 2026.
  • In 2026 capability began to outrun release: Anthropic withheld Claude Mythos Preview from general availability in April for its cyber capabilities, US export controls suspended Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for 19 days in June, and OpenAI and Google gated their most cyber-capable models to vetted defenders.

In this report
  1. 1. The shape of the story — Seventy years of AI produced three booms, two winters, and one boom that is still running
  2. 2. 1943–1993: rules and grants — The first thirty years ran on government grants and confident forecasts, and both collapsed when the promises came due
  3. 3. 1993–2017: learning from data — Learning from data beat hand-written rules, but only once chips and datasets caught up
  4. 4. 2017–2026: the scale era — Since 2010 the compute used to train leading models has grown about 4–5× a year, and the cost of a frontier run has reached hundreds of millions of dollars
  5. 5. Capability — Benchmarks that once lasted a decade now saturate within two years of release
  6. 6. Cost and diffusion — Capability got about 190 times cheaper in three and a half years and reached a billion weekly users
  7. 7. Capital and energy — Money arrived in waves: corporate AI investment rose about 29-fold from 2013 to 2025, with a two-year dip after the 2021 peak
  8. 8. 2026 — In 2026 capability began to outrun release: the most capable models went first to vetted defenders, and one was briefly switched off by government order
  9. Comparison — The current boom differs from the first two in who pays, what the core input costs and how many people use the result
