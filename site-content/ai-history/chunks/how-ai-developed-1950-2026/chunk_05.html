3. 1993–2017: learning from data

Learning from data beat hand-written rules, but only once chips and datasets caught up

Through the 1990s and 2000s AI advanced under other names: statistical machine learning, data mining, speech recognition. IBM's Deep Blue beat Garry Kasparov in a six-game rematch on 11 May 1997, 3½–2½, by evaluating about 200 million chess positions a second rather than by learning. Stanford's car won the DARPA Grand Challenge in 2005, a year after no vehicle finished; IBM Watson won Jeopardy! in February 2011; Siri shipped in October 2011.

The turning point was a benchmark. The ImageNet challenge began in 2010 with a winning top-5 error of 28.2%. In 2012 AlexNet, a deep convolutional network trained on two consumer graphics cards, cut the error to 15.3% against 26.2% for the next entry. Within three years ResNet reached 3.57%, below the 5.1% error estimated for a single human annotator (Exhibit 2).

Deep learning then spread to games and science: Google bought DeepMind in January 2014, and AlphaGo beat Lee Sedol 4–1 in Seoul in March 2016. In June 2017 Google researchers published the transformer architecture ('Attention Is All You Need'), which became the basis of every large language model that followed.
