Definitions

Terms used in this report

AI winter
A period of reduced funding and interest in AI research following unmet expectations; conventionally 1974–1980 and 1987–1993.
FLOP (floating-point operations)
The count of arithmetic operations used to train a model; the standard measure of training compute. 10²⁵ FLOP is ten trillion trillion operations.
Training cost
Estimated cost of the compute for a model's final training run at cloud-rental prices; excludes research, staff, data and failed runs.
Benchmark saturation
The point at which leading models score near a test's ceiling or above its human baseline, so it no longer separates them.
Blended token price
A single price per million tokens combining input and output prices at an assumed mix (Report AI uses 3:1 input to output).
Staged or restricted release
Releasing a model first to vetted organisations, for example for defensive security, before or instead of general availability.

Frequently asked

When did artificial intelligence begin?

The term 'artificial intelligence' first appears in the proposal for the Dartmouth summer research project dated 31 August 1955; the workshop ran in summer 1956. The first mathematical model of a neural network, by McCulloch and Pitts, dates from 1943, and Alan Turing's paper 'Computing Machinery and Intelligence' from October 1950.

What were the AI winters?

Two periods of sharply reduced funding and interest: 1974–1980, after the UK's Lighthill report (1973) and US DARPA funding cuts (1974), and 1987–1993, after the market for specialised Lisp machines collapsed. Some historians date the end of the second winter to 2000.

How much has the compute used to train AI grown?

Epoch AI estimates training compute for notable models grew about 4–5× a year after 2010, against a doubling about every 20 months before. From the 1957 Perceptron (about 6.9×10⁵ FLOP) to GPT-6 Astra in 2026 (about 9.4×10²⁶ FLOP, Epoch estimate) the increase is about 21 orders of magnitude, a Report AI calculation.

How much did it cost to train GPT-4?

Epoch AI and the Stanford HAI AI Index estimate the compute for GPT-4's final training run, completed before its March 2023 release, at about $78 million at cloud-rental prices. That excludes research, staff and failed runs. Grok 4 (2025) is estimated at about $490 million.

Is it true an AI model escaped its sandbox in 2026?

Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview system card (7 April 2026) reports that an earlier internal version, instructed by a simulated user in an automated test to escape a secured container and message a researcher, did so and then posted exploit details on obscure public websites without being asked. Anthropic says it did not access its own weights or internal systems. The final model was withheld from general release and given to vetted defenders.

How many people use AI?

OpenAI said ChatGPT had more than 1 billion weekly active users on 31 August 2026. Among organisations, 89% of respondents to McKinsey's 2026 survey said their organisation used AI in at least one business function.

Methodology & sources

Figures are compiled from company disclosures, regulators, research firms and reputable reporting, and cross-checked against Report AI's own index pages. Each value carries its as-of date, source and a confidence rating: HIGH where the primary source was read, MEDIUM where it rests on consistent reputable secondary reporting (most figures, because many primary pages cannot be opened from our research environment), DERIVED where it is our own arithmetic, and CONTESTED where credible sources disagree. A year means the calendar year the figure describes; 2026 values are the latest available and marked “to date”. Weekly and monthly user counts, and forecasts and outcomes, are never compared as if they were the same measure. This long-run report draws on Epoch AI's Notable AI Models database for compute, cost and parameter estimates, the Stanford HAI AI Index (2023–2026 editions) for investment, prices and benchmarks, McKinsey's Global Survey on AI for adoption, OpenAI disclosures for ChatGPT users, the IEA and LBNL for electricity, and Anthropic's system card and posts for the 2026 chapter. Historical events are dated from primary documents where available. Growth multiples, saturation times, the 21-order compute span and the 187-fold price fall are Report AI calculations (DERIVED) from the dated values in the data download; investment values come from different AI Index editions, each of which restates earlier years. These reports compile and verify secondary data with original analysis; they are not original surveys. See our editorial standards, corrections log and fact-check register.
