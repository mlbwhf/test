4. How the test changes what you see

One dataset and one split crowned 7 different winners

A chart built from a single dataset and a single random split, like the viral one, is one draw from a noisy process. Across our 95 single runs, 7 different models came first at least once. The overall winner, TabPFN v2, was first in 80.0% of runs, and the average gap between first and second place in a single run was just 0.9 points. The agreement between a single run's ranking and the overall ranking (Kendall's tau) averaged 0.68, where 1 would mean identical.

The metric matters too. Accuracy counts right answers at one threshold; ROC AUC measures how well a model orders cases from least to most likely, across all thresholds. Switching from accuracy to AUC moved Random forest 2 places and Extra trees 2.

The effect is stronger when no model dominates. Re-running the comparison without TabPFN, on 10 splits per dataset (190 single runs), 9 different models came first at least once, and the best of the 12, CatBoost, topped only 55.3% of single runs. A one-dataset, one-split chart has roughly the reliability of a coin toss weighted towards the favourite.
