7. What the research says

Trees led until 2023; pretrained tabular transformers lead the 2026 leaderboards

Grinsztajn, Oyallon and Varoquaux (arXiv July 2022; NeurIPS 2022) benchmarked tree-based models against deep learning on 45 tabular datasets and found that tree-based models such as XGBoost and random forests remain state-of-the-art on medium-sized data of about 10,000 samples. McElfresh et al. (2023) found that the neural-net-versus-boosted-tree debate is overemphasised: for many datasets the gap is negligible, or light hyperparameter tuning of a GBDT matters more than the choice between model families.

On TabArena's live leaderboard (default view: single models, 51 datasets, data regenerated 11 November 2026), the top five entries are NVIDIA's Kumo-Tabular (Elo 1971), LimiX-2 (1959), Kumo-Tabular-Medium (1909), Neuralk-AI's Seldon (1903) and Prior Labs' TabPFN-3.5 (1868), all tabular foundation models run with default settings. On TabArena's default leaderboard (11 November 2026), classic models rank far below: tuned-and-ensembled CatBoost scores Elo 1402, XGBoost 1360, RandomForest 1172, k-nearest neighbours 993 and linear models 990, against 1971 for the leader.

Our results sit between these: at 1,000 training rows and default settings, without the ensembling and long tuning budgets of the leaderboards, TabPFN v2 led, and the gaps among the top tree ensembles were small.
