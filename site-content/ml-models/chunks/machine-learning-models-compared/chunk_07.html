6. Scaling, imbalance and tuning

Three setup choices change the scoreboard as much as the choice of model

Four models measure distances or weighted sums directly on the raw features, so a feature measured in thousands drowns one measured in fractions unless the data is standardised first. Removing that one preprocessing line changed their accuracy by: SVM (RBF) -7.9 points; Neural network (MLP) -6.4 points; k-nearest neighbours -5.6 points; Logistic regression -1.0 points.

When only 10% of cases are positive, a model that always answers 'no' scores 90.0% accuracy while learning nothing; its balanced accuracy is 50.0%. Of the 12 real models, 9 scored within two points above that do-nothing baseline and 3 (Neural network (MLP), Decision tree, Naive Bayes) scored below it, yet their balanced accuracy ranged between 55.6% and 63.6% balanced accuracy. The leader changes with the metric: CatBoost had the highest accuracy, Neural network (MLP) the highest balanced accuracy. On imbalanced data, an accuracy figure is meaningless without the base rate. (TabPFN was not run in this test.)

Default settings are not the whole story. A modest search over 20 hyperparameter configurations, scored by 3-fold cross-validation, helped Decision tree most (+3.4 points) and moved XGBoost to the top of the nine tuned models, from 3rd at default settings. McElfresh et al. (2023) reached the same conclusion at larger scale: light tuning of gradient-boosted trees often matters more than the choice between model families.
