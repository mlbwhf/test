Analysis

The state of play: video follows the text curve

AI video in 2026 looks like AI text did in 2024: explosive volume growth, collapsing unit costs, and rapid consolidation around a handful of winners. Monthly active users across AI video platforms passed 124 million in January 2026, and the number of videos generated grew roughly 840% in two years. What was a research demo in early 2024 is now a routine production input — 78% of marketing teams use AI-generated video in at least one campaign per quarter, and 73% of Fortune 500 companies have integrated AI video tools into their content workflows.

The economics explain the speed. A ten-second clip that would have required a shoot, a crew and an edit suite now costs between five cents and a few dollars depending on the model, and renders in minutes. At those prices, video stops being a scarce asset and becomes a volume medium — the same shift text went through when token prices collapsed.

The market's defining event, though, was a failure: OpenAI's Sora — the product that made AI video famous — is being shut down in stages through 2026 even as the category booms. The lesson of the reshuffle is that model quality alone does not decide who survives; distribution and unit economics do. Kuaishou's Kling, attached to an existing short-video ecosystem, reached an estimated $500M revenue run-rate; Google's Veo, distributed through Gemini, Flow and Adobe, generated hundreds of millions of clips in months. The standalone-app model lost.

Key takeaways
  • Volume, not novelty. 124M monthly users and +840% generation growth in two years make video the fastest-scaling generative modality after text.
  • Costs are collapsing. Ten seconds of usable video now costs $0.05–$7.50 depending on tier — cheap enough to A/B-test video like display ads.
  • Distribution beats models. Sora's shutdown amid a boom shows ecosystems (Kling×Kuaishou, Veo×Google/Adobe) win over standalone apps.

Cross-platform monthly active users surpassed 124 million in January 2026, with generation volume up ~840% between January 2024 and January 2026 (Luma Labs industry tracking MEDIUM). The dominant use cases are short-form: social and ad creative, product explainers, localization (regenerating the same spot per market), and previsualization for traditional shoots. Long-form remains constrained by clip-length limits — most models generate 5–20 seconds per pass, so longer pieces are chained from short clips in editors. The Sora shutdown, explained

Sora made AI video mainstream — and its retirement is the category's biggest cautionary tale. The consumer app peaked at roughly 3.3 million monthly downloads in November 2025, but OpenAI confirmed a two-stage wind-down: the consumer app closed on April 26, 2026, and the API sunsets on September 24, 2026.

Why shut a famous product amid a boom? The consistent reading across industry analyses: video inference is enormously compute-hungry, Sora's standalone app lacked a native distribution ecosystem to monetize against those costs, and OpenAI chose to concentrate compute on its core model line. Users migrated primarily to Veo, Kling and Runway — a reshuffle that concentrated the market around players whose video products sit inside larger ecosystems.

Market size, briefly

Published market-size figures range from under $1B to over $6B for 2025–26 because analysts draw the market's boundary differently — pure generation engines, generation plus AI editing, or the full platform stack. The headline anchors: the broader AI video platform market was valued at ~$6.2B in 2025 and is projected to reach $47.8B by 2034 (25.4% CAGR, Grand View Research).
