At a glance
  • About 18.8% of the world's people aged 15–64 used a generative AI product in the second quarter of 2026, according to Microsoft's AI Diffusion data, up from 15.1% in the first half of 2025. That is roughly 990 million people (Report AI calculation).
  • The United Arab Emirates leads at 73.3%, followed by Singapore (64.3%); the United States ranks 21st at 33.0%, and China 67th at 17.5% on Microsoft's measure, which misses most Chinese domestic apps.
  • The divide is widening: the gap between the Global North (28.8%) and the Global South (16.2%) grew from 9.8 to 12.6 percentage points across four editions.
  • Growth has moved to East Asia: South Korea rose from 25.9% to 40.6% and Japan from 16.7% to 24.7% between H1 2025 and Q2 2026.
  • National trackers fill the gaps: China had 546M monthly users of AI-native apps in September 2026 (QuestMobile), and 55% of Russians aged 12 and over used an AI service monthly (Mediascope), led by Yandex's Alice AI.

Analysis

Three facts dominate the map of AI use. First, it is still a minority activity: on Microsoft's telemetry-based measure, fewer than one in five working-age people worldwide used a generative AI product in the second quarter of 2026. Second, the leaders are small, rich, connected economies rather than the countries that build frontier models: the United Arab Emirates, Singapore, Ireland, France and Norway all rank above the United States. Third, use is spreading fastest where governments and telecom operators have pushed it hard and where local-language quality has improved, notably in South Korea and Japan.

The divide is the uncomfortable finding. In every edition of Microsoft's data the Global North has pulled further ahead, from a 9.8-point gap to 12.6 points. Sub-Saharan Africa averages 10.6% and South Asia 16.4%, against 33.6% in North America. The constraint is mostly upstream of AI: internet access, digital skills, electricity and the quality of models in local languages.

Measures disagree, and the reasons matter. Microsoft's figures are estimated from Windows desktop telemetry on visits to 19 named AI services, scaled to mobile use and to population; the list includes some Chinese and Russian services but not ByteDance's Doubao, so the measure undercounts countries where people use domestic apps on phones: China's own surveys report far higher use than Microsoft's 17.5%, and Russia's 9.9% sits beside a Mediascope reach of 55%. Surveys of the online population, such as the KPMG and University of Melbourne study, report higher use again, because they exclude people who are offline. We publish all three kinds of measure side by side and never rank countries across them.

For businesses the picture is earlier still. Eurostat found that about one in five EU enterprises with ten or more staff used at least one AI technology in 2025, and the OECD reports a similar share of firms across its members. Individual use runs ahead of organisational adoption almost everywhere.

Key takeaways
  • Use the world figure with its definition: 18.8% of people aged 15–64 used generative AI in Q2 2026 (Microsoft), not '18.8% of internet users'.
  • Small, rich, well-connected economies lead; frontier-model builders do not top the adoption table.
  • The North–South gap is widening, not closing.
  • In China and Russia, national trackers are the better guide, because Microsoft's telemetry misses domestic apps.
  • Never rank countries across different measures: telemetry, surveys of online adults and app counts answer different questions.
1. The ranking

The United Arab Emirates and Singapore lead the world in AI use; the United States ranks 21st

Microsoft's AI Economy Institute estimates the share of each economy's working-age population that used a generative AI product in a period, from anonymised telemetry adjusted for device share, internet penetration and population. In the second quarter of 2026, 31 economies were above 30%, up from 26 a quarter earlier.

The top of the table is dominated by small, high-income economies with near-universal connectivity and active government programmes: United Arab Emirates (73.3%, #1), Singapore (64.3%, #2), Ireland (49.9%, #3), France (49.6%, #4) and Norway (49.4%, #5). Among the largest economies, the United Kingdom is at 43.7%, Germany 32.3%, Japan 24.7%, Brazil 20.2%, India 18.5% and Indonesia 14.8%.

2. The divide

The gap between the Global North and South widened in every edition, to 12.6 percentage points

Microsoft splits its data into Global North and Global South. Use grew in both, but faster in the North: from 22.9% to 28.8%, against 13.1% to 16.2% in the South. The same report notes large gaps in the foundations of use: internet access, digital skills and electricity.

By region, using a working-age-population-weighted average of the economies Microsoft covers (Report AI calculation), North America leads at 33.6%, followed by Europe and Central Asia at 26.6%; Sub-Saharan Africa is lowest at 10.6%. 36 economies, mostly in Africa and smaller territories, carry identical values within regional groups in Microsoft's data, so they are regional estimates rather than national measurements and are flagged in the table.

3. Growth

Growth has shifted to East Asia: South Korea, Japan and Thailand grew fastest

Between the first half of 2025 and the second quarter of 2026, South Korea's share rose by 57% in relative terms, from 25.9% to 40.6%, the fastest of any economy (Report AI calculation from Microsoft data). Japan (+48%) and Thailand (+47%) followed. In absolute terms the largest gains were in South Korea and the UAE, both up by about 14 percentage points.

The United States grew more slowly in relative terms, from 26.3% to 33.0%, from a higher base. China's measured rise was among the smallest of the large economies, from 15.4% to 17.5%, which says more about the measure's blind spots there than about Chinese use.

5. Other measures

National trackers and surveys show higher use where Microsoft's telemetry cannot see

Microsoft's measure is the only consistent cross-country series, but it is built on Microsoft products. In China, where Western assistants are blocked and phones run domestic apps, QuestMobile counted 546M monthly users of AI-native apps in September 2026, led by ByteDance's Doubao at 419.08M. In Russia, Mediascope found that 55% of people aged 12 and over used an AI service in a month, with Yandex's Alice AI reaching about ~48% (~59M).

Surveys answer different questions again. Eurostat found 32.66% of EU residents aged 16–74 used generative AI tools in 2025, while 19.95% of EU enterprises with ten or more staff used at least one AI technology. The KPMG and University of Melbourne study found 66% of people across 47 countries using AI with some regularity, a survey of online respondents that reads higher than population-wide measures.

6. Attitudes

Optimism is highest where measured use is often lowest: Asian and emerging economies are the most positive

Across 32 countries in March–April 2026, 51% of adults said AI products make them excited and 50% said they make them nervous (Ipsos AI Monitor 2026). The spread is wide: 85% in China and 77% in Indonesia said AI products have more benefits than drawbacks, against 38% in the United States and 38% in France, where 64% of Americans said AI makes them nervous.

Self-reported use follows the same pattern. In the University of Melbourne and KPMG study of 47 countries (fieldwork November 2024–January 2025), 89% of respondents in China said they use AI regularly, against 53% in the United States; emerging economies averaged about 80% and advanced economies 58%. These are surveys of online populations and measure something different from Microsoft's population-wide estimate, which is one reason China ranks low on one and high on the other. Pew's 25-country median in 2025 found 34% more concerned than excited about AI and 16% more excited than concerned.

Fact check

Numbers to stop quoting

Widely repeatedWhat the evidence supports
“Perplexity is Russia's most popular AI, with a 48% share.”StatCounter measures which chatbot sends clicks to websites running its tracker, not use. On that referral measure Perplexity had 47.92% in Russia in April 2026, fell behind ChatGPT from May to September, and led again in October 2026 at 39.34% (Gemini 32.44%, ChatGPT 23.38%). By use, Mediascope's September 2026 data put Yandex's Alice AI at about 48% monthly reach among Russians aged 12+, DeepSeek at 11% (13 million) and Sber's GigaChat at 5% (6 million).
“Kenya has the highest AI usage in the world (97.5%).”97.5% is GWI's share of ONLINE Kenyan adults using AI tools monthly; only ~35% of Kenyans used the internet in 2024 (ITU). Microsoft estimates 8.9% of Kenya's working-age population used generative AI in Q2 2026.
“Only 10.4% of Nigerians use AI (Microsoft).”Microsoft's technical paper (Table 2, †) marks Nigeria and 35 other economies as 'Region-imputed estimate due to insufficient telemetry coverage'; Nigeria shares 8.7% -> 9.3% -> 10.1% -> 10.4% with 11 other West African economies. Quote it as Microsoft's West Africa estimate. Survey contrast: KPMG/Melbourne found 92% of Nigerians use AI at least every few months (fieldwork Nov 2024-Jan 2025); the World Bank puts AI use among Nigerian formal firms at ~40% (2025).
“ChatGPT adoption is four times higher in low-income countries.”OpenAI's Sept 2025 'How People Use ChatGPT' study says adoption GROWTH RATES in the lowest-income countries were over 4x those in the highest-income countries by May 2025 - growth from a low base, not a higher level.
“India is ChatGPT's second-largest market.”True as of February 2026 (Sam Altman: 100M+ weekly users, #2 after the US). On 3 November 2026 OpenAI India MD Prabhjeet Singh told The Economic Times that India is now ChatGPT's largest market by weekly active users, with users doubling since January; no absolute figure was given.
“South Korea's most-used AI is Naver's HyperCLOVA X, not ChatGPT.”App trackers show the opposite: IGAWorks Mobile Index counted 18.76M ChatGPT app users in Korea in Oct 2026, ~91% of users across tracked AI apps; Wiseapp counted ChatGPT 24.7M, Gemini 9.87M and Claude 4.04M in Sep 2026. Neither tracker lists a HyperCLOVA X / CLOVA X app at comparable scale (Naver's AI is embedded in search, which these trackers do not count as AI-app use). StatCounter's referral data (Oct 2026) also show no Korean model: Gemini 65.25%, ChatGPT 23.62%, Perplexity 8.05% of chatbot-referred page views. Microsoft attributes Korea's adoption surge to Korean-language gains in GPT-4o and GPT-5 (Global AI Adoption in 2025, Jan 2026).
“92% of Indonesians use generative AI.”92% is the share of Indonesian KNOWLEDGE WORKERS in Microsoft/LinkedIn's 2024 Work Trend Index. Indonesia's national internet survey (APJII 2026) found 18.2% of respondents had used AI; Microsoft puts Indonesia at 14.8% of the working-age population (Q2 2026).
“One in six people worldwide use AI / 16.3% global adoption (Microsoft).”16.3% was H2 2025. Microsoft's latest reading is 18.8% of the world's working-age population in June 2026 (Q2 2026 report, 21 Sep 2026).
“The US ranks 24th in AI adoption.”24th was H2 2025 (28.3%). The US moved to 21st in Q1 2026 (31.3%) and stayed 21st in Q2 2026 at 33.0%.
“Israel uses Claude 7x more than expected - the world's highest per-capita Claude use.”7.0 was Israel's AUI in Anthropic's Aug 2025 sample. In the Feb 2026 sample Anthropic lists Singapore first, then Israel, Luxembourg, Switzerland, the US and Canada (AUI 4.4), with Australia 7th (4.1). Always date AUI figures.
“Most Chinese do not use AI: only 17.5% (Microsoft).”Microsoft's measure excludes several widely used Chinese domestic tools and Microsoft says its next edition will raise China most. CNNIC counted 700M+ Chinese generative-AI users (over 50%) in June 2026; QuestMobile counted 546M monthly users of AI-native apps in Sep 2026. Different measures - do not compare directly.
“Japan lags: only about a quarter of Japanese have used generative AI.”26.7% was the previous MIC survey. Japan's 2026 Information and Communications White Paper (survey Jan-Feb 2026) found 58.8% have used gen AI (US 75.6%, Germany 75.6%, China 93.6%). Microsoft's working-age measure: Japan 24.7% in Q2 2026, up from 16.7% in H1 2025.
“The US is less excited about AI than anywhere else.”Canada (26%) and Great Britain (29%) are less excited than the US (33%) in Ipsos's 2026 AI Monitor; the 32-country average is 51%. Canadians and Australians are the most nervous (67% each), the US 64%.
“Microsoft measures what share of people use AI in every country.”It estimates desktop-observed visits to 19 named AI sites in Windows telemetry, scaled to mobile and to the 15-64 population; 36 of 147 economies are regional imputations, telemetry is limited in Russia, Iran and partly China, and several Chinese domestic tools are excluded until the next edition.
Definitions

Terms used in this index

AI user share (Microsoft)
The share of people aged 15–64 who used a generative AI product in the period, estimated by Microsoft from anonymised Windows desktop telemetry on visits to 19 named AI services, scaled to mobile use with StatCounter ratios and to population with World Bank data. It is not a survey; the 19 services include some Chinese and Russian ones but not ByteDance's Doubao, and economies with low telemetry opt-in are pulled towards the global average.
Global North and Global South
Microsoft's grouping of economies by income and development; used here only as Microsoft defines it.
Regional estimate
An economy for which Microsoft reports the same value as others in its group in every edition, indicating a modelled rather than measured figure.
Monthly active users (MAU)
People who used an app or service at least once in a month; not comparable with weekly active users or with population shares.
Referral share (StatCounter)
The share of clicks from AI chatbots to websites that StatCounter tracks; it measures links followed, not how many people use each chatbot.

Frequently asked

Which country uses AI the most?

On Microsoft's AI Diffusion data, the United Arab Emirates had the highest share of working-age people using generative AI in Q2 2026, at 73.3%, followed by Singapore at 64.3% and Ireland at 49.9%.

What share of the world uses AI?

About 18.8% of the world's working-age population (15–64) used a generative AI product in Q2 2026, up from 15.1% in H1 2025, according to Microsoft's AI Economy Institute.

How does the United States rank for AI use?

The United States ranked 21st of 147 economies in Microsoft's Q2 2026 data, with 33.0% of working-age people using generative AI, up from 26.3% in H1 2025.

Why does China rank so low?

Microsoft's measure (17.5% for China in Q2 2026) relies on Microsoft-product telemetry and misses most Chinese domestic apps. National data show far higher use: QuestMobile counted 546M monthly users of AI-native apps in China in September 2026.

Is the AI divide between rich and poor countries growing?

Yes, on Microsoft's data: the gap between the Global North and Global South widened from 9.8 to 12.6 percentage points between H1 2025 and Q2 2026, as use grew faster in the North.

Methodology & sources

Figures are compiled from company disclosures, regulators, research firms and reputable reporting, and cross-checked against Report AI's own index pages. Each value carries its as-of date, source and a confidence rating: HIGH where the primary source was read, MEDIUM where it rests on consistent reputable secondary reporting (most figures, because many primary pages cannot be opened from our research environment), DERIVED where it is our own arithmetic, and CONTESTED where credible sources disagree. A year means the calendar year the figure describes; 2026 values are the latest available and marked “to date”. Weekly and monthly user counts, and forecasts and outcomes, are never compared as if they were the same measure. The core series is Microsoft's AI Diffusion dataset (147 economies, four editions), downloaded from Microsoft's public GitHub repository. National figures come from QuestMobile and CNNIC (China), Mediascope (Russia), Mobile Index and Wiseapp (South Korea), and app-intelligence firms (India); surveys from Eurostat, the OECD, KPMG and the University of Melbourne, Ipsos and Pew. Regional averages, growth rates, ranks of change and the world-user estimate are Report AI calculations (DERIVED). Different measures are shown side by side and never combined into one ranking. These reports compile and verify secondary data with original analysis; they are not original surveys. See our editorial standards, corrections log and fact-check register.
