At a glance
  • We catalogue 58 sovereign or national AI model families across 31 countries and regions. About 40 are described as trained from scratch and about 13 are adaptations of foreign open-weight models such as Meta's Llama, Google's Gemma, Alibaba's Qwen or Nvidia's Nemotron.
  • Measured by developer downloads, sovereign models are a niche: in the 30 days to 12 November 2026, 31 national model makers together recorded about 13.6 million Hugging Face downloads, about 5.8% of the 234.5 million for Alibaba's Qwen alone (Report AI calculation from Hugging Face data). France's Mistral accounts for most of that national total.
  • Measured by capability, the frontier is American and Chinese: 15 of the top 20 models on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (v5.0.1, November 2026) come from US developers and 5 from Chinese ones. The best-scoring model from any other country is Mistral Large 4, at 26.1 against 64.6 for the leader.
  • Measured by home-market use, local champions win only where foreign assistants are blocked or weak: in China, ByteDance's Doubao had 419 million monthly users in September 2026; in South Korea, ChatGPT had about 91% of AI-app users despite years of investment in local models.
  • The money is mostly going into compute rather than models: national programmes in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, India, Japan, South Korea, Canada and the EU buy chips and data centres, then run a mix of imported and local models on them.

Analysis

Sovereign AI has become one of the most used phrases in AI policy and one of the least defined. For this index we separate four things that are often run together: sovereign infrastructure (domestic compute under national control), sovereign models (trained from scratch or adapted from foreign open weights), sovereign data (control over national training corpora) and sovereign deployment (foreign models run in-country under local rules). Most countries pursue all four in name and fund mainly the first.

The popularity evidence is sobering for national models. On Hugging Face, the main distribution channel for open models, Chinese models took 41% of downloads between February 2025 and February 2026, ahead of US models, and Alibaba's Qwen alone outdraws every national model maker combined many times over. National models do find users where they serve a language or a regulated sector that global models serve poorly: Japanese government trials, Korean public services, Arabic-language deployments and Indian-language speech. But outside China and Russia, where policy or sanctions shape the market, no national assistant leads its home consumer market.

That does not make sovereign programmes pointless. Their realistic product is resilience and bargaining power rather than market share: domestic compute that keeps running if access is cut off, models that can be audited and adapted for public services, and the skills to do both. The June 2026 suspension of two US frontier models under export controls, and China's October 2026 draft controls on model weights, showed why governments value that. The risk is the one visible in our data: adapted models built on Qwen, Llama or Gemma inherit their base model's licence and, potentially, its home country's export rules.

The compute build-out is also slower than the headlines. Stargate UAE had no capacity operating in November 2026; Saudi Arabia's HUMAIN's '600,000 GPUs' is a three-year ceiling against signed deals for about 27,000; and India's common compute portal had onboarded about 38,000 GPUs by March 2026, roughly the figure it reported six months earlier.

Key takeaways
  • Separate the layers: infrastructure, models, data and deployment are different kinds of sovereignty.
  • Most national models are adaptations of foreign open weights, chiefly Llama, Gemma, Qwen and Nemotron.
  • Downloads, users and leaderboard ranks all show national models as niche outside China.
  • Home-market leadership by a domestic assistant happens where foreign assistants are blocked or restricted.
  • Announced compute (GW, GPU ceilings) runs well ahead of operating compute.

3. Popularity: downloads

National model makers together draw about 6% of the downloads of Alibaba's Qwen alone

Hugging Face downloads are the most complete public measure of open-model popularity, though they count developers and automated systems rather than end users. In the 30 days to 12 November 2026, the Qwen organisation recorded about 234.5 million downloads. The 31 national and sovereign model makers we track recorded about 13.6 million between them, and France's Mistral alone accounted for about 60% of that (Report AI calculation).

Over longer windows the pattern is the same. The ATOM project's count of cumulative downloads put Qwen at about 942 million by March 2026, roughly twice Meta's Llama, and Hugging Face reported that Chinese models took 41% of all downloads between February 2025 and February 2026. Qwen is also the most popular base for national adaptations, which means part of the 'sovereign' ecosystem in Southeast Asia and elsewhere now runs on a Chinese foundation.

4. Popularity: capability and usage share

On capability leaderboards and developer platforms, the US and China take almost every top spot

On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (v5.0.1, 12 November 2026), the top nine models are all from US developers, led by Claude Fable 5.2 at 64.6; the best Chinese model is Kimi K3.1 Pro at 46.5, the highest-ranked open-weight model. The best model from any third country is Mistral Large 4 from France at 26.1, followed by the UAE's K2 Horizon (17.8) and South Korea's Solar Pro 4 (14.2). No model from India, Japan, Russia or Germany appears on the index.

On OpenRouter, a developer marketplace that routes requests to many models, Chinese developers took about 47% of requests in the week beginning 2 November 2026 and US developers about 39% (Report AI calculation by developer country); DeepSeek alone had about 32%. OpenRouter notes that its rankings measure traffic through its own platform, not users or spending. LMArena's crowd-sourced rankings could not be verified for this edition.

5. Popularity: home markets

Domestic assistants lead their home markets only where foreign ones are blocked or restricted

The consumer test of sovereignty is whether people at home choose the national assistant. In China, where Western assistants are blocked, domestic apps dominate: ByteDance's Doubao had about 419 million monthly users in September 2026, Alibaba's Qianwen about 158 million and DeepSeek about 123 million (QuestMobile). In Russia, Yandex's Alice AI reached about 48% of people aged 12 and over in September 2026, against 5% for Sber's GigaChat (Mediascope).

Elsewhere, global assistants lead even in countries with strong national programmes. In South Korea, ChatGPT's app had 18.76 million monthly users in October 2026, about 91% of AI-app users, and Naver closed its standalone chatbot in April 2026 to focus on AI answers inside search. In France, ChatGPT dominates consumer use while Mistral's strength is in enterprise and government contracts. In India, OpenAI said in November 2026 that the country had become ChatGPT's largest market.

6. Compute and money

Announced sovereign compute runs well ahead of what is operating

Most sovereign money buys infrastructure. The table lists the main national programmes with their headline and, where available, delivered figures. Four kinds of money appear and should never be added together: budget appropriations, state equity funds, sovereign-wealth vehicles, and 'mobilisation' headlines that count expected private investment.

Delivery lags announcement. Epoch AI's data-centre tracking shows Stargate UAE with no capacity operating in November 2026, against a first 200 MW projected for December. HUMAIN's 600,000-GPU figure is a three-year ceiling; its signed October 2026 deal covers about 27,000 GPUs and 84 MW through 2027. Nvidia, the clearest commercial proxy, reported more than $30 billion of sovereign AI revenue in its fiscal 2026 and has given only growth rates since.

Definitions

Terms used in this index

Sovereign AI (umbrella)
A state's capacity to develop, run and govern AI on infrastructure, data and models it controls, rather than renting them from foreign providers. Definitions are contested: 'hard' sovereignty means self-sufficiency across the stack; 'soft' sovereignty means control over the layers that matter for a given use case.
Sovereign infrastructure (compute)
Domestic data centres and GPU clusters under national legal control, funded by budgets, state banks or sovereign-wealth vehicles.
Sovereign models - from scratch
Foundation models pre-trained from random initialisation by a national lab or champion.
Sovereign models - adapted (continued pre-training / fine-tune of foreign open weights)
A national model built on a foreign open-weight base (Llama, Gemma, Qwen, Mistral, Nemotron) with local data.
Sovereign deployment of foreign models
Foreign proprietary or open models run in-country under local data residency, often via 'sovereign cloud' offers.
Sovereign data
Legal and practical control over national training corpora and the data pipeline (licensed national-library, news and administrative text).

Frequently asked

What is sovereign AI?

Sovereign AI is a country's capacity to develop, run and govern AI on infrastructure, data and models it controls, rather than renting them from foreign providers. In practice it covers four layers: domestic compute, national models (from scratch or adapted), control of training data, and in-country deployment of foreign models under local rules.

Which countries have built their own AI models?

Report AI's November 2026 index catalogues 58 national model families across 31 countries and regions, including France (Mistral), the UAE (Falcon, Jais, K2), Saudi Arabia (ALLaM), India (Sarvam, BharatGen), Japan (PLaMo, LLM-jp, tsuzumi), South Korea (EXAONE, A.X, Solar), Switzerland (Apertus), Singapore (SEA-LION), Russia (GigaChat, Alice AI) and China (Qwen, DeepSeek, Doubao, Kimi, GLM).

Are sovereign AI models popular?

Mostly not, outside China. In the 30 days to 12 November 2026, 31 national model makers together had about 13.6 million Hugging Face downloads, about 5.8% of Alibaba's Qwen alone, and no model from outside the US and China ranks in the top 20 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index.

What is the most popular sovereign AI model?

By home-market users, China's Doubao (ByteDance) with about 419 million monthly users in September 2026. Among models outside the US and China, France's Mistral leads on developer downloads and capability scores.

How much are governments spending on sovereign AI?

Announced figures run to hundreds of billions, but they mix budgets, state funds, sovereign-wealth vehicles and private 'mobilisation' totals that cannot be added together. Delivered compute is much smaller: Stargate UAE had no capacity operating in November 2026, and India's common compute portal had about 38,000 GPUs onboarded by March 2026.

Methodology & sources

Figures are compiled from company disclosures, regulators, research firms and reputable reporting, and cross-checked against Report AI's own index pages. Each value carries its as-of date, source and a confidence rating: HIGH where the primary source was read, MEDIUM where it rests on consistent reputable secondary reporting (most figures, because many primary pages cannot be opened from our research environment), DERIVED where it is our own arithmetic, and CONTESTED where credible sources disagree. A year means the calendar year the figure describes; 2026 values are the latest available and marked “to date”. Weekly and monthly user counts, and forecasts and outcomes, are never compared as if they were the same measure. The catalogue was compiled from developer announcements, model cards and national programme documents. Popularity measures come from the Hugging Face API (organisation download totals, read 12 November 2026), Artificial Analysis (Intelligence Index v5.0.1), OpenRouter's public rankings, StatCounter, and national app and audience trackers (QuestMobile, Mediascope, Mobile Index). Each measures something different; none is a count of users across all channels. Shares of downloads and requests by country, and counts of models by build type, are Report AI calculations (DERIVED).
