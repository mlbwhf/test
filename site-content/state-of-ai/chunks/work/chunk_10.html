Numbers to stop quoting

Widely repeatedWhat the evidence supports
“AI is the top reason for US layoffs in 2026.”AI led Challenger's stated reasons for several months from March 2026, but in Challenger's 5 November 2026 report 'market/economic conditions' (130,531 cuts) overtook AI (121,401) for January–October, and AI-attributed cuts fell to 1,265 in October. · detail
“AI will wipe out 92 million jobs by 2030.”The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 forecasts 92 million jobs displaced and 170 million created worldwide by 2030, a net gain of 78 million, from all structural trends (technology, green transition, demographics, economics), not AI alone. It is an employer-survey forecast, not a measurement. · detail
“Workers with AI skills earn a 56% wage premium (or 'up to 25%').”Superseded. PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer (June 2026) puts the average premium in job advertisements at 62%; the 56% describes 2024 ads and was restated as 57%; 25% describes 2023 ads. It is an advertised-wage gap between comparable roles, not a measured pay rise for individual workers. · detail
“Stanford found entry-level jobs in AI-exposed occupations are down 13%.”Superseded. The August 2025 working paper's 13% was revised to 16% in November 2025, and the August 2026 update puts employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations 19% below the path of less-exposed peers. It is a relative, descriptive measure, not a count of jobs lost to AI. · detail
“Half of AI layoffs have already been reversed.”That is Forrester's forecast for the end of 2026, which resolves in January 2027. The interim evidence is that 32% of US hiring managers who cut a role because of AI have rehired for it (Robert Half, 2026). · detail
“Software developer job postings are still collapsing because of AI.”Indeed's US software-development postings index was flat at about 67 (one-third below February 2020) through 2024 and 2025, then rose to 81.6 on 30 October 2026, up 21% in ten months. The steeper 'entry-level postings −67% since 2022' figure is a vendor estimate for junior roles only. · detail
Outlook

Whether 2026's fall in AI-attributed cuts is a pause or a turn will be visible in the first half of 2027

The indicators to watch in 2027 are the ones that would change the reading in this report. If AI-attributed cuts return above 10,000 a month, the October 2026 low was a pause in restructuring; if they stay below about 5,000 a month through the first quarter, the 2026 peak looks like a one-off wave. If the Stanford early-career gap widens beyond about 25%, the entry-level squeeze is deepening rather than stabilising. Forrester's reversal forecast resolves in January 2027, and the 2027 PwC Barometer will show whether the advertised AI-skill premium is still rising or beginning to compress as AI skills become common.

Two longer-run signals matter more than any single month. The first is whether firms begin to report measurable productivity effects: in early 2026, 89% of executives in NBER's survey reported none. The second is whether the recovery in software postings reaches junior roles; so far it has been concentrated among senior engineers, which leaves the pipeline problem described in Chapter 3 unresolved. A new WEF Future of Jobs edition, if published, will show whether employers still expect net job creation by 2030. Each signal below is stated with its current value and date, so that the next edition of this report can say plainly which way it moved and whether our reading should change.

Definitions
AI-attributed job cut
A job cut announced by a US employer for which the employer named artificial intelligence as the reason, as classified by Challenger, Gray & Christmas. It counts planned cuts at announcement, not completed separations, and depends on the reason the employer chooses to give.
Exposure
An estimate of how much of an occupation's work current AI could perform or assist. It is not a forecast of job loss; exposed jobs may be augmented rather than replaced.
Task-automation share
The share of an occupation's tasks that a study judges automatable. A 46% task share does not mean 46% of jobs are lost.
Relative employment decline
In the Stanford Canaries studies, how far employment of a group (here, workers aged 22–25 in the most AI-exposed occupations) has fallen behind a comparison group since late 2022. It is descriptive, not a causal estimate.
Job postings index
Indeed Hiring Lab's seasonally adjusted measure of job postings on Indeed relative to 1 February 2020 (= 100). It measures advertised demand, not hires.
AI skills wage premium
In PwC's Global AI Jobs Barometer, the average difference in advertised wages between job ads that require AI skills and comparable ads that do not. It is not a measured pay rise for individual workers.
Deflection
The share of customer contacts an AI system resolves without passing them to a human. Vendors measure it differently, which is why published figures range from about 20% to 90%.
Reversal
Any rehiring, re-scoping or cancellation of an AI-justified job cut. Definitions vary across surveys, which makes forecasts such as Forrester's hard to score.
