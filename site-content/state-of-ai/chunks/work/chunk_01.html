At a glance
  • Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 121,401 US job cuts attributed to AI from January to October 2026, more than twice the 54,836 in all of 2025 and almost ten times the 12,742 in 2024.
  • AI-attributed US job cuts fell to 1,265 in October 2026, the lowest month of the year, and by Challenger's 5 November 2026 report 'market/economic conditions' had overtaken AI as the leading stated reason for 2026 cuts.
  • Robert Half found in 2026 that 32% of US hiring managers who eliminated a role primarily because of AI had later rehired for it, typically in hybrid roles paying 20–35% more.
  • Stanford Digital Economy Lab's August 2026 update found employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in AI-exposed US occupations 19% below where it would have been had it kept pace with less-exposed peers.
  • Indeed Hiring Lab data show US software-development job postings at 81.6% of their February 2020 level on 30 October 2026, up from 67.3% at the end of 2025, with senior roles leading the recovery.
  • Gallup found in May 2026 that 52% of US employees used AI at work at least a few times a year and 15% used it daily, against about 4% daily use in mid-2024.
  • PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer, published on 15 June 2026, found job advertisements requiring AI skills offered wages 62% higher on average than comparable roles without them.

In this report
  1. 1. Layoffs — US job cuts attributed to AI rose almost tenfold in two years, then fell to their lowest monthly level of 2026 in October
  2. 2. Reversals — About a third of US hiring managers who cut a role for AI have already rehired for it, usually at higher pay
  3. 3. Entry level — Software hiring recovered in 2026, but the gap for young workers in AI-exposed jobs widened to 19%
  4. 4. Use, demand and pay — AI moved into a majority of jobs in 2026, demand for AI skills nearly tripled in postings, and the advertised pay premium kept rising
  5. 5. Exposure and productivity — Exposure is highest in translation, administrative and legal work, but measured productivity gains across whole jobs remain small
  6. 6. Forecasts — The leading forecast still expects AI-era job creation to exceed displacement by 78 million worldwide by 2030, but it is a forecast built on employer expectations
