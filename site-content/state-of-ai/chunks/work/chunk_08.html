Comparison

Between 2024 and 2026, AI-attributed cuts and AI demand in job postings rose almost tenfold and the share of postings mentioning AI nearly tripled, while early-career employment in exposed jobs kept falling behind

Read year over year, the indicators in this report move together in direction but not in scale. AI-attributed US job cuts rose from 12,742 in 2024 to 54,836 in 2025 and 121,401 in January–October 2026, from about 1.7% to 19.7% of all announced cuts. Over the same period the share of US Indeed postings mentioning AI rose from 2.71% to 7.34%, daily AI use among US employees rose from about 4% to 15%, and PwC's advertised premium for AI skills rose from 56% to 62%. Employers were cutting some roles in AI's name and paying more for AI skills in others at the same time.

The measures that diverge are the most informative. Software-development postings were flat at about a third below their 2020 level through 2024 and 2025, then recovered to 18.4% below in October 2026, while the Stanford early-career gap in AI-exposed jobs widened from 16% to 19%. AI-attributed cuts peaked in May 2026 and fell to 1,265 in October. And the share of executives reporting any employment effect from AI remains under 10%. The comparison does not support a story of steady, economy-wide displacement; it supports one of reallocation towards experienced workers and AI-skilled roles. Productivity, the indicator that would most justify further cuts, has moved least: self-reported time savings rose from about 1.4% to about 2.2% of US work hours between November 2024 and the first quarter of 2026.
