At a glance
  • The EU's AI Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026, moved the AI Act's stand-alone high-risk obligations from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027, a delay of 16 months.
  • No fine under the EU AI Act had been published as of late September 2026, although the European Commission's power to fine general-purpose AI providers up to €15 million or 3% of turnover began on 2 August 2026.
  • NCSL counted 280 AI bills enacted and 1,539 introduced in US state legislatures in 2026 as of early October 2026, compared with 227 enacted in all of 2025.
  • By Report AI's count, eight AI-focused US executive orders were signed in 2025, including EO 14365 of 11 December 2025, which ordered a Justice Department task force to challenge state AI laws.
  • Public Citizen counted 31 US states with election-deepfake laws in October 2026, up from 21 at the end of 2024; roughly 45–48 states address sexual deepfakes.
  • China had 1,112 generative-AI services filed with the Cyberspace Administration of China by 31 August 2026, up from 302 at the end of 2024, and still had no national AI law.
  • The AI Kill Switch Act, introduced in the US House on 23 July 2026, proposes fines of up to $20 million a day for defying a federal shutdown order; Report AI puts its chance of enactment as written this Congress at about 5%.

In this report
  1. 1. European Union — The EU AI Act began to apply in 2025, but in 2026 its main high-risk duties were pushed back 16 months and it has not yet produced a fine
  2. 2. United States: federal — Washington still has no comprehensive AI statute; it has governed AI by executive order, and since December 2025 those orders have been aimed at state AI laws
  3. 3. United States: states — With Congress stalled, US states enacted more AI laws each year, though most are narrow rules on disclosure, deepfakes and chatbots rather than broad frameworks
  4. 4. China — China has no AI law, but it regulates AI through filing and labelling rules that cover more services every year
  5. 5. Enforcement and the rest of the world — Most AI enforcement still runs through privacy, consumer and telecoms law, and the largest privacy fine on a chatbot provider was overturned in court
