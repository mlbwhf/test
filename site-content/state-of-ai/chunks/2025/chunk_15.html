Infrastructure & Energy

In 2025 chip revenue kept compounding, custom silicon gained ground and power became the visible constraint on AI capacity

  1. Nvidia reported data-centre revenue of $193.7 billion for its fiscal year ended January 2026, up 68%, within total revenue of $215.9 billion.
  2. The IEA estimates that global data-centre electricity use rose to about 485 terawatt-hours in 2025, with consumption by AI-focused data centres up about 50%; Gartner puts the same year at 447 terawatt-hours.
  3. OpenAI's chief financial officer said on 18 January 2026 that the company ended 2025 with about 1.9 gigawatts of compute, up from 0.6 gigawatts at the end of 2024.
  4. Amazon signed a 1,920-megawatt nuclear power purchase agreement with Talen Energy's Susquehanna plant in June 2025, running to 2042 and worth about $18 billion to Talen.
  5. PJM's capacity auction held in July 2025 cleared at its $329.17 per megawatt-day cap for the 2026/27 delivery year, up 22% from $269.92 a year earlier, as data-centre load forecasts rose.

Nvidia's data-centre revenue rose 68% to $193.7 billion in the fiscal year that ended in January 2026, within total revenue of $215.9 billion; networking grew fastest as customers bought whole rack-scale systems. Broadcom's AI revenue reached about $20 billion in its fiscal 2025, and AMD's Data Center segment grew 32% to $16.6 billion, slowed by export limits on its China-specific MI308. TSMC roughly doubled CoWoS capacity to 75,000–80,000 wafers a month. Rental prices for H100s drifted lower through the year on Silicon Data's new index, from about $2.36 an hour in June to $2.00 in early December. Large supply agreements followed the revenue: OpenAI announced a 6-gigawatt GPU deal with AMD in October, and Nvidia a letter of intent for up to $100 billion of investment tied to 10 gigawatts.

Capacity announcements outran delivered capacity. Stargate launched in January as a $500 billion private commitment and by September counted nearly 7 gigawatts planned, while OpenAI later said it ended the year with about 1.9 gigawatts of compute, up from 0.6 gigawatts. Meta announced its 1-gigawatt Prometheus and multi-gigawatt Hyperion clusters, financing Hyperion through a roughly $27 billion joint venture with Blue Owl in October. CBRE's primary North American markets reached a record-low vacancy of 1.6% in the first half, with 5,243 megawatts under construction. The IEA estimates global data-centre electricity rose to about 485 terawatt-hours, with AI-focused facilities up about 50%; Gartner's estimate for the same year is 447 terawatt-hours.

Power contracting scaled up and grid prices followed. In June Amazon signed a 1,920-megawatt agreement with Talen Energy's Susquehanna nuclear plant running to 2042, and Meta a 1,121-megawatt agreement for the Clinton plant's output from 2027. PJM's July auction cleared at its $329.17 cap for 2026/27, up 22%, and its December auction at its $333.44 cap for 2027/28. Measurement of AI's per-query footprint improved: in August Google reported 0.24 watt-hours and 0.26 millilitres of water for a median Gemini text prompt, and its data centres' electricity use grew about 39% over the year to about 42 terawatt-hours, with company-wide water consumption up 34% to 10.9 billion gallons.
