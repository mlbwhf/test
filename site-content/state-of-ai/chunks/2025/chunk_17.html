In 2025 US private AI investment rose to $285.9 billion, 23 times China's, as DeepSeek briefly matched the US frontier

  1. US private AI investment reached $285.9 billion in 2025, more than 23 times China's $12.4 billion, according to Stanford HAI's 2026 AI Index.
  2. US-headquartered companies raised $159 billion, or 79%, of the $202.3 billion invested in AI start-ups worldwide in 2025, according to Crunchbase.
  3. The United States took about 72% of the AI computing capacity added worldwide in 2025, up from around 60% a year in 2022-2024, according to Epoch AI's chip-imports data.
  4. China's generative AI users rose to 602 million in December 2025 from 249 million a year earlier, according to CNNIC.
  5. Microsoft estimated that 16.3% of the world's working-age population used generative AI in the second half of 2025, ranging from 64.0% in the UAE to 28.3% in the United States.

Stanford HAI's 2026 AI Index put US private AI investment at $285.9 billion in 2025, more than 23 times China's $12.4 billion and about 82.9% of the $344.7 billion global private total, by Report AI's calculation. Crunchbase recorded US companies raising $159 billion of the $202.3 billion invested in AI start-ups worldwide. Europe's AI venture funding rose to about $17.5 billion, and Mistral's valuation doubled to €11.7 billion in September. Beijing registered a ¥60.1 billion National AI Industry Investment Fund in January, and the EU announced its €200 billion InvestAI mobilisation target in February.

DeepSeek-R1, released in January, briefly matched the top US model on the Chatbot Arena leaderboard in February, when the US lead stood at 1.70%. US institutions still released more notable models: 50 against China's 30 by one reading of Stanford's data, 59 against 35 by another. The United States took about 72% of the AI compute added worldwide in 2025, according to Epoch AI. Export policy changed repeatedly: the AI Diffusion Rule was rescinded in May, H20 exports required licences from April, Gulf firms received approvals for up to 35,000 GB300-equivalent chips each in November, and an H200 sales arrangement with a 25% government share was announced in December.

Use of generative AI spread fastest in small, wealthy economies. Microsoft estimated that 16.3% of the world's working-age population used it in the second half of 2025, from 64.0% in the UAE and 60.9% in Singapore to 28.3% in the United States. In China, CNNIC counted 602 million generative AI users by December, up from 249 million a year earlier, and 748 services had completed filing with the Cyberspace Administration. Anthropic's Economic Index found the US produced 21.6% of Claude.ai use and Israel used Claude at seven times its population share. The net flow of top AI talent into the US fell about 80% over the year to September.
