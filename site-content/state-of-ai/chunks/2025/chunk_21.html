Risk

In 2025 the measures rose together and AI moved from assisting attacks to operating one.

  1. The AI Incident Database recorded 362 AI incidents that occurred in 2025, up 55% from 233 in 2024, according to Stanford HAI's AI Index 2026.
  2. The FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report, published in April 2026, counted 22,364 US complaints with an AI-related descriptor and $893 million in losses — the first year the report gave AI its own section.
  3. NewsGuard's audit found that leading AI chatbots repeated false news claims 35% of the time in August 2025, almost double the 18% of a year earlier.
  4. Anthropic reported on 13 November 2025 that a Chinese state-linked group used its Claude Code agent to run a cyber-espionage campaign against about 30 organisations, with the AI performing an estimated 80–90% of the tactical work.
  5. The Internet Watch Foundation found 3,443 AI-generated child sexual abuse videos in 2025, up from 13 in 2024.

2025 produced the first official floor under AI-enabled crime. The FBI's report for the year, published in April 2026, counted 22,364 complaints with an AI descriptor and $893 million in losses, about 4% of the $20.9 billion in all reported losses, which rose 26%. Vendor data pointed to better attacks rather than more: Sumsub found the identity-fraud rate fell to 2.2% while the share using AI-driven, multi-step techniques rose from 10% to 28%. Arup's $25 million remained the largest known single loss. KnowBe4's widely quoted finding that 82.6% of phishing emails showed AI involvement counted any machine assistance, not authorship.

Incidents and misinformation indicators climbed in step. The AI Incident Database recorded 362 incidents, up 55%. NewsGuard's August audit found chatbots repeating false claims 35% of the time, almost double the year before, as assistants stopped declining news questions. NewsGuard tracked 1,271 AI news sites in May, and the count was growing fast. The share of new web articles written mainly by AI, by contrast, levelled off near half, at 50.9% in the fourth quarter on Graphite's later measure. The Internet Watch Foundation found 3,443 AI-generated child abuse videos, up from 13.

Two shifts in kind mattered more than the percentages. In November Anthropic disclosed that a state-linked group had used its Claude Code agent to run an espionage campaign against about 30 organisations, with the model doing an estimated 80–90% of the tactical work. And chatbot harm moved to general-purpose assistants: OpenAI disclosed in October that about 1.2 million of its then roughly 800 million weekly users discussed suicide each week, and eight lawsuits over ChatGPT were filed between August and November, beginning with Raine v. OpenAI. Earlier, in May, a federal judge in Garcia v. Character Technologies had declined at the pleading stage to treat chatbot output as protected speech, opening the product-liability route those suits relied on.
