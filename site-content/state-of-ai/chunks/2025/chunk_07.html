Finance

In 2025 AI took about half of all venture money and valuations ran ahead of revenue, as the build-out began to borrow

  1. Crunchbase counted $202.3 billion of venture funding to AI companies in 2025, close to half of the $425 billion invested globally; the OECD, using a broader definition, put AI's share at 61%, or $258.7 billion of $427.1 billion.
  2. OpenAI's $40 billion round led by SoftBank in March 2025, at a $300 billion valuation, was the largest venture round of 2025, and an employee share sale in October 2025 valued the company at $500 billion.
  3. OpenAI booked about $13.07 billion of revenue and a $20.92 billion operating loss in 2025, according to leaked audited accounts reported by the Financial Times.
  4. Nvidia reported data-centre revenue of $193.7 billion for its fiscal year ended January 2026, roughly calendar 2025, up 68% on the year before.
  5. Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Oracle sold about $108 billion of bonds in 2025, according to a Reuters analysis of LSEG data, against an average of about $28 billion a year for the five hyperscalers in 2020–2024 (Bank of America).

In 2025 AI took about half of global venture funding. Crunchbase counted $202.3 billion of the $425 billion invested, the OECD $258.7 billion of $427.1 billion (61%) on its broader definition, and PitchBook 65.4% of US deal value. OpenAI's $40 billion round at $300 billion in March, led by SoftBank, was the largest of the year. Anthropic raised $13 billion at $183 billion in September, and Microsoft and Nvidia's November investment implied about $350 billion. Mistral reached €11.7 billion. Five companies each raised more than $5 billion, together about a fifth of all venture funding. Global venture funding rose about 30% to $425 billion on Crunchbase's count, and AI accounted for most of the increase. Rounds of $100 million or more made up 77% of AI funding, $163 billion across 233 companies.

Valuations rose faster than revenue, though revenue grew quickly. OpenAI's October employee share sale valued it at $500 billion. Its revenue was about $13.07 billion, with a $20.92 billion operating loss and a net loss of about $38.5 billion, mostly a non-cash restructuring charge. Anthropic's revenue was about $4.6 billion, with an $8.06 billion operating loss. By year end the run-rates were about $20 billion and $9 billion, and Cursor passed $1 billion ARR on a $29.3 billion valuation. Nvidia's data-centre revenue reached $193.7 billion in the fiscal year to January 2026, while big-four capex rose to about $410 billion. That was about 23 times the two leading labs' combined revenue, down from about 60 times in 2024: the labs' revenue had grown faster than the build-out, but from a far smaller base.

The financing model began to change. Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Oracle sold about $108 billion of bonds, against an average of about $28 billion a year before 2025. Meta's roughly $27 billion Hyperion joint venture with Blue Owl became the largest private-credit deal on record, and Morgan Stanley put data-centre debt issuance at about $182 billion. Nvidia's September letter of intent to invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI, which would buy Nvidia systems, made circular financing the year's main bubble argument; it was still unsigned in December. Public investors stayed cautious: CoreWeave's March IPO raised $1.5 billion at $40, below its target range. In December Nvidia paid about $20 billion in a licensing deal with Groq, the year's largest AI transaction, though not a standard acquisition.
