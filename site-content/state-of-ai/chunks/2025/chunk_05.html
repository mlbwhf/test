Spending

In 2025 most AI spending measures roughly doubled, and enterprise money shifted between model providers

  1. Gartner's September 2026 forecast puts worldwide AI spending in 2025 at about $1.79 trillion, against the $1.48 trillion Gartner projected for the same year in September 2025.
  2. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spent about $413 billion on capital expenditure in calendar 2025, roughly 69% more than in 2024, by Report AI's sum of their reported figures.
  3. IDC counted $318.7 billion of worldwide AI infrastructure spending in 2025, up 107% from 2024, according to its April 2026 tracker release.
  4. Menlo Ventures estimated in December 2025 that US enterprises spent $37 billion on generative AI in 2025, 3.2 times its restated 2024 figure.
  5. The IEA estimates that global data-centre electricity use reached about 485 TWh in 2025, with electricity for AI-focused data centres up about 50% in the year.

Big-four capex rose to about $413 billion in calendar 2025, roughly 69% more than in 2024: Amazon about $131.8 billion, Microsoft about $118.0 billion, Alphabet $91.4 billion and Meta $72.2 billion. IDC counted $318.7 billion of AI infrastructure spending, up 107%, including about $90 billion in the fourth quarter alone. Gartner's estimate of worldwide AI spending for the year rose with each edition, from a $1.48 trillion forecast in September 2025 to about $1.79 trillion in its September 2026 table, partly because it added categories. Oracle's capex for its fiscal year to May 2026 reached about $55.7 billion. Alphabet's capex rose about 74% and Meta's about 84%, by our calculation from the reported figures, and Microsoft's fiscal year to June 2025 alone came to $88.7 billion. Each company ended the year guiding to more for 2026.

Enterprises spent about $37 billion on generative AI, according to Menlo Ventures, 3.2 times its restated 2024 figure, with coding tools the largest application at about $4 billion. API spending reached about $8.4 billion annualised by mid-year, and Anthropic overtook OpenAI, taking 40% of enterprise API spending against 27%. Gartner's later figures put spending on standalone AI agents and assistants at $16.5 billion in 2025 and on all software with agentic features at $86.4 billion. Menlo also estimated a $3.5 billion vertical generative-AI segment, led by healthcare at $1.5 billion. In June 2025 Gartner forecast that over 40% of agentic AI projects would be cancelled by the end of 2027, and McKinsey's November 2025 survey found 23% of organisations scaling an agentic system.

Data-centre electricity use rose to about 485 TWh, according to the IEA, with AI-focused sites up about 50%. Governments made large announcements but smaller commitments: the EU's €200 billion InvestAI was a mobilisation headline over a >€2.7 billion AI Factories programme, the $500 billion Stargate venture was private, China registered a ¥60.1 billion AI fund, the UK set a £2 billion envelope to 2030 and Saudi Arabia launched HUMAIN. The UK's envelope included about £1 billion for sovereign compute. In November 2025 the US authorised advanced chip exports to HUMAIN and G42, each cleared to buy the equivalent of up to 35,000 Nvidia GB300 chips. Data-centre debt issuance nearly doubled to about $182 billion in 2025, according to Morgan Stanley, showing how much of the build-out was already financed by borrowing.
