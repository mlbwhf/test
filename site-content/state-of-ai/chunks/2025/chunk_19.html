Industries

In 2025 healthcare became the fastest-moving regulated sector and the gap between individual and institutional AI use became the defining pattern

  1. The FDA authorized 335 AI-enabled medical devices with 2025 decision dates, according to the September 2026 snapshot of its AI device list, 42.6% more than in 2024; other counts of the same year range from 295 to 333.
  2. Menlo Ventures estimated in October 2025 that 22% of US healthcare organizations had implemented domain-specific AI tools, up from roughly 3% in 2024, and that healthcare AI spending reached $1.4 billion in 2025.
  3. HEPI's survey of 1,041 UK full-time undergraduates, fielded in December 2024, found that 92% used AI in some form during the 2024-25 academic year, up from 66% a year earlier.
  4. Eurostat reported that 19.95% of EU enterprises with 10 or more employees used AI in 2025, up from 13.5% in 2024, led by the information and communication sector at 62.52%.
  5. Salesforce estimated that AI and agents influenced $262 billion of global online holiday sales between 1 November and 31 December 2025, about 20% of the total, up from $229 billion in 2024.

In 2025 headline adoption figures for large organizations stopped rising much. McKinsey found 88% of organizations using AI in at least one function, against 78% in 2024. Representative surveys showed more room: Eurostat's EU enterprise rate rose to 19.95%, with the information and communication sector at 62.52%, and Ramp's paid-adoption index ended the year at 46.6%. The Census Bureau changed its AI question in November 2025, and the measured US rate moved from about 10% on the old question to 17.3% on the new one. Within finance, Ramp's measure reached 58.1% in December 2025.

Healthcare recorded the largest changes. The FDA list attributes a record 335 AI device authorizations to 2025, 42.6% more than in 2024 on the same snapshot, although other counts range from 295 to 333. Menlo Ventures found 22% of healthcare organizations had implemented domain-specific AI tools, about seven times the 2024 level, and estimated healthcare AI spending at $1.4 billion; its enterprise survey put healthcare at $1.5 billion, 43% of vertical generative-AI spending. Legal generative-AI spending reached $650 million. In schools, Gallup and the Walton Family Foundation found 60% of US teachers had used AI in the 2024-25 school year, and HEPI found 92% of UK undergraduates using it, though only 23% received tools from their university.

Evidence on outcomes was mixed. METR's randomized trial found experienced open-source developers took 19% longer with early-2025 AI tools, while Stack Overflow found developer use rising to 78% and trust falling to 33%. Pre/post studies of ambient scribes reported lower burnout but lacked control groups. In retail, Adobe recorded a 693.4% rise in generative-AI referral traffic over the holiday season, and Salesforce estimated that AI and agents influenced $262 billion of global online holiday sales, about 20% of the total. US federal agencies reported 3,611 AI use cases, up from 2,133, partly because of a change in what agencies had to report.
