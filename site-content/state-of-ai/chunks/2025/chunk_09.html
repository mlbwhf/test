Work

In 2025, AI-attributed US job cuts quadrupled to 54,836 and the first payroll evidence of an early-career squeeze appeared

  1. Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 54,836 US job cuts attributed to AI in 2025, 4.5% of the 1,206,374 cuts announced that year and more than four times the 2024 count.
  2. The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025, published in January 2025, forecast that 170 million jobs will be created and 92 million displaced worldwide by 2030, a net gain of 78 million driven by all structural trends, not AI alone.
  3. A November 2025 revision of Stanford Digital Economy Lab research, using ADP payroll data, found a 16% relative decline since late 2022 in employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed US occupations.
  4. PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer, published on 15 June 2026 and based on the previous year's job advertisements, put the average wage premium for jobs requiring AI skills at 62%.
  5. Indeed Hiring Lab data show that 4.44% of US job postings mentioned AI-related terms on 31 December 2025, while US software-development postings remained 32.7% below their February 2020 level.

AI became a recognised reason for US layoffs in 2025. Challenger counted 54,836 AI-attributed job cuts, more than four times the 2024 figure, though still 4.5% of the 1,206,374 cuts announced in a year when total cuts rose 58% to their highest level since 2020. Early reversals followed. Klarna, which had said in February 2024 that its AI assistant did the work of about 700 agents, acknowledged in May 2025 that cost had been 'too predominant' a factor, began rehiring human agents for complex cases, and kept the AI for routine work, which by November 2025 did the work of 853 employees. In October 2025 alone, Challenger counted 153,074 announced US cuts, with cost-cutting and AI among the reasons it highlighted.

The first payroll evidence of an AI effect on young workers appeared. Stanford Digital Economy Lab researchers, using ADP data, reported in August 2025 a 13% relative decline since late 2022 in employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations, revised to 16% in November 2025. Older workers in the same occupations held steady. US software-development postings on Indeed fell to a low of 62.5 in March 2025 and ended the year at 67.3, 32.7% below February 2020. Graduate hiring was flat: NACE's spring 2025 update projected 0.6% more hiring for the class of 2025. The Stanford authors cautioned that the pattern could partly reflect higher interest rates and post-pandemic over-hiring rather than AI alone.

Use and demand rose while the productivity evidence stayed mixed. Gallup found 46% of US employees used AI at work at least a few times a year by the fourth quarter and about 12% used it daily. The share of US Indeed postings mentioning AI reached 4.44% at year-end, and PwC's 2026 Barometer, which analyses the previous year's advertisements, put the AI-skill wage premium at 62%. In January the World Economic Forum forecast 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced worldwide by 2030. METR's July 2025 trial found experienced developers took 19% longer with early-2025 AI tools, and the St. Louis Fed's series put time saved at about 2% of US work hours by the fourth quarter.
