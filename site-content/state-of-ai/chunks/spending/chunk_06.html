5. Industries and budgets

AI is taking a growing share of technology spending, but industry rankings depend on what is measured

Comparing Gartner's AI spending forecast with its own forecast of worldwide IT spending gives a rough measure of AI's weight in technology budgets. On our arithmetic, AI spending was about 19% of IT spending in 2024, about 32% in 2025 and is forecast at about 42% in 2026, when Gartner expects IT spending of $6.39 trillion. The ratio assumes that Gartner's AI categories sit inside its IT total, which Gartner does not state, and part of the rise reflects Gartner widening its AI definition. It describes market spending, not the budget of a typical company. The same calculation using Gartner's earlier editions would give different values, so the ratio should be quoted with its inputs and years. It is a rough indicator, not a statistic.

Surveys of buyers point in the same direction without giving a clean share. Futurum's September 2026 survey of 1,636 enterprise decision-makers found 46.9% reporting AI spending over budget in the second half of 2026, and 38.7% of those said they had moved money from elsewhere in the IT budget to cover it. RBC's December 2025 survey of 117 IT leaders found 90% planned to increase AI spending in 2026. Neither survey gives the share of IT budgets spent on AI. Both surveys suggest AI is being funded partly by moving money, not only by adding it. BCG's May 2026 survey of 423 IT leaders found they expected overall IT spending to grow 5.8% in 2026, far less than any AI spending series, and BCG reported that buyers were pulling back in other areas to pay for AI.

By industry, the ranking depends on the measure. On IDC's all-AI measure, banking was the largest spender worldwide in 2024, with about $31.3 billion and more than 20% of the total; banking, software and information services, and retail together accounted for about $89.6 billion. In Europe, IDC puts banking at 12.6% of AI spending in 2026 and financial services as a whole at 19.2%. IDC's worldwide industry tables for 2025 and 2026 are not public. IDC expects the top three verticals to reach about $222 billion by 2028. Financial services' weight reflects long-standing use of machine learning in fraud detection, credit scoring and trading as well as newer generative tools; JPMorgan alone has said it spends about $2 billion a year on AI within an $18 billion technology budget.

On Menlo's narrower measure of vertical generative-AI software, healthcare led in 2025 with $1.5 billion, 43% of about $3.5 billion, followed by legal at $650 million. The two rankings are not in conflict. Banks spend heavily across all forms of AI, including fraud detection and risk models built over many years, while healthcare providers are buying new generative tools, such as clinical documentation, faster than other sectors. Any statement that one industry 'spends the most on AI' should name the measure. Healthcare organisations' adoption of domain-specific AI tools reached 22% in 2025, about ten times the 2023 level, according to Menlo. 'Other verticals', including financial services, made up about $990 million. All of these sums are small beside capex.

How we derived AI's share of IT spending

Share = Gartner worldwide AI spending ÷ Gartner worldwide IT spending for the same year. 2024: $987.9 billion ÷ $5,114.8 billion = 19.3%, from the September 2025 AI table and the January 2025 IT table. 2025: $1,786.7 billion ÷ $5,581.7 billion = 32.0%, where 2025 IT spending is Gartner's 2026 total of $6,391 billion divided by its stated growth of 14.5%. 2026: $2,670.5 billion ÷ $6,391 billion = 41.8%. All inputs are Gartner's; the ratio is ours and is marked DERIVED. It is not a survey of company IT budgets.
