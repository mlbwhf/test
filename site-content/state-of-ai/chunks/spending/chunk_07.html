6. Governments

Government AI spending is small next to corporate capex, and its biggest headlines are not budgets

Public AI money is measured in single-digit billions a year where corporate capex is measured in hundreds of billions. The US requested $3.3 billion of non-defence federal AI research and development for fiscal 2025, up from $3.1 billion for fiscal 2024; we did not find the fiscal 2026 total. Defence is larger: the Pentagon's fiscal 2026 request included $13.4 billion for AI and autonomy. Brookings counted $7.2 billion of federal AI contract obligations in 2026 to May, almost all for the Department of Defense, though its 2024 base differs between accounts ($355 million or $675 million). These are budget requests and contract records, not outlays, and they use different definitions. GAO counted $5.6 billion of federal AI contract obligations over fiscal 2022–24 using another method, so neither series can be extended with the other.

Elsewhere the largest commitments are one-off vehicles. China registered the ¥344 billion (about $47.5 billion) semiconductor 'Big Fund III' in May 2024 and a ¥60.1 billion (about $8.2 billion) national AI fund in January 2025. The EU's verifiable public commitment is more than €2.7 billion for EuroHPC AI Factories over 2024–27; its €200 billion InvestAI is a mobilisation total that includes hoped-for private capital. The $500 billion Stargate venture contains no US federal funds. Both Chinese funds are equity vehicles registered once and invested over many years, not annual budgets. China also announced in March 2025 a national venture-capital guidance fund intended to mobilise about ¥1 trillion over 20 years. InvestAI includes a planned €20 billion public-private fund for AI gigafactories.

The UK set a £2 billion AI envelope for 2025–30 in its June 2025 Spending Review, about half for sovereign compute, launched a £500 million Sovereign AI Fund in April 2026 and added £60 million in the October 2026 Budget. Reports of a '£700 million sovereign AI fund' describe the Budget's wider AI package, whose size is reported as £600 million or £700 million. In the Gulf, Abu Dhabi's MGX closed a $49 billion first fund in July 2026, and Saudi Arabia's HUMAIN, launched in May 2025, has not disclosed its capital. MGX invests commercially for return and holds stakes in OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI, so it is better compared with a sovereign-wealth fund than with a budget. Its stated goal is more than $100 billion of assets.

These figures cannot be added together. An annual appropriation, a fund registered once and invested over years, a sovereign-wealth vehicle that invests for return and a mobilisation headline are four different things. By verifiable annual budget, Japan's ¥1.23 trillion (about $7.9 billion) for chips and AI in fiscal 2026 and South Korea's ₩10.1 trillion (about $7.4 billion) for 2025 remain larger than the US non-defence AI R&D line. None is close to a single quarter of one hyperscaler's capex. India's IndiaAI Mission, at about $1.25 billion from 2024, and Singapore's more than S$1 billion are smaller still. Governments shape where AI capacity is built through permits, power and export rules far more than through their own spending.

Four kinds of public AI money

Appropriation: money a legislature or ministry has allocated for a year, such as US federal AI R&D; the only kind that can be compared across countries. Equity vehicle: capital registered once and invested over years, such as China's national AI fund. Sovereign vehicle: public capital invested commercially for return, such as MGX or HUMAIN. Mobilisation headline: a total that bundles public money with private capital a government hopes to attract, such as InvestAI's €200 billion. We label every government figure by type and never sum across types.
