1. Worldwide spending

Forecasters raised their 2026 AI spending estimates all year, and most of the new money is going to hardware

Gartner forecast on 16 September 2026 that worldwide AI spending will reach about $2.67 trillion in 2026, up 49.5% on its estimate of about $1.79 trillion for 2025. It was the third time in eight months that Gartner raised its figure for the year: the January edition said $2.52 trillion and the May edition $2.59 trillion. Gartner expects a further rise to about $3.64 trillion in 2027. Its total is broad. It counts AI infrastructure, software, services, AI models, security and AI-enabled consumer devices, so it is best read as a measure of all spending that has some AI content rather than spending on AI alone. Gartner's figures are built from vendor revenue and buyer surveys, and the company does not publish its full method. Treat the total as an order of magnitude and the direction of revision as the signal.

Infrastructure is the largest component and the main reason for the revisions. Gartner puts 2026 AI infrastructure spending at about $1.48 trillion, roughly 56% of its total. IDC, which counts only AI-optimised servers and storage, measured $153.9 billion of such spending in 2024 and $318.7 billion in 2025, a rise of 107%. On 6 November 2026 IDC raised its 2026 forecast to $521 billion, from $497 billion in July and $487 billion in April, citing higher component prices, demand for CPU-based systems and continued cloud-provider capex. IDC expects the series to pass $1 trillion in 2029. The infrastructure share matters because hardware is bought ahead of demand: servers and data centres are paid for before the services that run on them earn revenue, which is why infrastructure leads every other category in both firms' figures.

The quarterly data show why the forecasts moved. IDC counted about $90 billion of AI infrastructure spending in the fourth quarter of 2025 and $89.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a flat start to the year that IDC attributes to paused deployments. Spending then rose to $121.4 billion in the second quarter, up 53% year on year and about a third on the previous quarter. Servers made up 98% of the value. One strong quarter was enough to lift the full-year forecast by $24 billion. The quarterly line is the closest thing to a measured series in this report. It is still an estimate, built from vendor revenue rather than buyers' accounts, and IDC revises past quarters when vendors restate, so the latest edition should always be used.

Two cautions apply to the headline totals. First, Gartner's figures for completed years are estimates that it revises: its September 2025 forecast put 2025 at $1.48 trillion, its January 2026 table at $1.757 trillion and its September 2026 table at $1.79 trillion, partly because categories were added. A growth rate computed across editions is not a measured growth rate. Second, the IDC and Gartner totals measure different things and should never be added together or used to check one another. They agree on direction, not on size. For 2026 the safest statement is the range of forecasts and their direction: every published figure for the year was revised upward between January and November, and none was revised down. That pattern can change quickly if hardware deliveries slip.

Why AI spending forecasts disagree

Gartner's $2.67 trillion and IDC's $521 billion for 2026 are both correct on their own terms. Gartner counts any spending with AI content, including AI-enabled smartphones and PCs, software with AI features and AI services. IDC's tracker counts AI-optimised servers and storage only. IDC also publishes a separate, broader spending guide, whose 2029 figure of $1.3 trillion is often misquoted as 'agentic AI spending'. Before comparing two figures, check four things: which categories are included, whether the year is complete (an estimate) or still ahead (a forecast), which edition of the forecast is being quoted, and whether the figure is an annual total or an annualised run-rate.
