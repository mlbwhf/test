4. Enterprise spending

Enterprise spending on AI models is growing faster than adoption, and one provider now takes most of it

Menlo Ventures' annual survey of US enterprise decision-makers is the most cited measure of company spending on generative AI. It estimated $37 billion of spending in 2025, 3.2 times its figure for 2024, split roughly evenly between applications (about $19 billion) and infrastructure (about $18 billion). Coding tools were the largest application category, at about $4 billion. Menlo's numbers have been restated as its method changed: it first reported $13.8 billion for 2024, then restated that to $11.5 billion after excluding inference. We keep both values. The survey covers about 500 US enterprise decision-makers combined with a bottom-up model of the market, so it describes large US buyers rather than all companies worldwide. Menlo invests in some of the companies whose revenue it estimates, which it discloses.

In November 2026 Menlo moved to a different measure: enterprise AI deployment spending as an annualised run-rate. It estimated that rate at $176.8 billion in September 2026, of which $99.5 billion was infrastructure and $77.3 billion applications, 5.4 times a restated $33 billion a year earlier. The figure excludes model training. It is not money spent in 2026 and it is not a successor to the $37 billion, so a growth rate from one to the other would be meaningless. SaaStr's reading of the report is that only 22% of the spending came out of software budgets. The run-rate is useful for one thing: it shows the level of spending at the end of the period, which is higher than the average across the year when spending is growing quickly.

The split by provider has changed faster than the totals. Menlo estimated that Anthropic took 24% of US enterprise LLM API spending in 2024, 40% in 2025 and 55% in 2026, while OpenAI went from 34% to 27% and then 31%. Google rose to 21% in 2025 and then fell to 9%. API spending itself ran at about $3.5 billion annualised in November 2024 and $8.4 billion by mid-2025; we found no dollar total for 2026. Menlo is an investor in Anthropic, and these are survey estimates, so we rate them MEDIUM. Together, Anthropic and OpenAI took 86% of enterprise API spending in 2026, up from 67% a year earlier; xAI and open-weight Chinese models together took just under 5%. Concentration rose even as the number of usable models grew.

Spending on AI agents is the most inflated category in public discussion. Gartner's widely quoted $206.5 billion for 2026 counts all spending on software that has agentic features built in, much of it existing enterprise applications. Its line for standalone AI agents and assistants is $29.2 billion for 2026, up from $16.5 billion in 2025. Research firms that size a standalone agents market put it at about $7–8 billion in 2025, with a median 2030 forecast of about $49 billion. Adoption data support the lower figures: Gartner's 2026 survey found 17% of organisations had deployed agents. McKinsey's 2025 survey found 23% of organisations scaling an agentic system somewhere and no more than 10% in any single function, and Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027.

Why Menlo's figures cannot be chained

Menlo Ventures has published four different bases in three years. Its November 2024 report gave $13.8 billion for 2024, including inference. Its December 2025 report gave $37 billion for 2025 and restated 2024 to $11.5 billion and 2023 to $1.7 billion, excluding inference. Its November 2026 report measures deployment spending as an annualised run-rate, $176.8 billion in September 2026 against a restated $33 billion a year earlier, excluding model training. Each figure is useful within its own edition. Growth rates should only be computed within one edition, and every quotation should name the edition and the measure.
