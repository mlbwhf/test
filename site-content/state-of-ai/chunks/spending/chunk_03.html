2. Big-tech capex

The four largest spenders' capex roughly doubled in two years and now runs ahead of the cash their businesses generate

Capital expenditure is the most concrete measure of AI spending because it is reported quarterly in audited accounts. By Report AI's sum of reported figures, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spent about $245 billion in calendar 2024 and about $413 billion in calendar 2025, an increase of roughly 69%. From January to September 2026 they spent about $508 billion, already more than in all of 2025. The third quarter of 2026 alone accounted for about $207 billion: Amazon $75.9 billion, Alphabet $51.4 billion, Microsoft $45.2 billion and Meta $34.91 billion. Quarterly capex for the four has risen steeply since 2024: the third-quarter figure for 2026 alone was more than four-fifths of their combined spending for the whole of 2024.

Not all of this is AI. Capex also covers warehouses, offices, network equipment and conventional cloud capacity. But each company has said that the increase is driven by data centres, servers and accelerators for AI, and none breaks out an AI-only figure. The totals are also approximate because the companies use different bases: Amazon reports cash capex net of incentives, Microsoft and Meta include assets acquired under finance leases, and Alphabet reports purchases of property and equipment. Off-balance-sheet structures such as Meta's roughly $27 billion Hyperion financing with Blue Owl are not in these numbers at all. Analysts often call these totals 'AI capex'; it is more accurate to call them capex driven by AI, and to quote each company's own basis alongside each number.

Guidance points higher still. In late October 2026 Amazon reaffirmed about $220 billion for 2026, Alphabet held $195–205 billion, Meta narrowed its range to $135–145 billion and Microsoft gave about $170 billion, which most outlets read as calendar 2026 and one as its fiscal year to June 2027. Together that is roughly $720–740 billion, which implies about $220 billion in the fourth quarter. At the start of 2026 the same four had guided to roughly $630–650 billion. Alphabet and Amazon have said spending will rise again in 2027, without giving numbers. Guidance has moved in one direction: Alphabet's 2026 range rose from $175–185 billion in February to $180–190 billion in April and $195–205 billion in July, and Amazon's from about $200 billion to about $220 billion.

Oracle, which reports fiscal years ending in May, shows the same pattern from a smaller base: gross capex of $21.2 billion in fiscal 2025, $55.7 billion in fiscal 2026 and guidance of $90–95 billion for fiscal 2027, with $28.5 billion spent in its first quarter to August 2026. For Oracle, Meta, Alphabet and Amazon, capex now exceeds operating cash flow. Meta's free cash flow was negative $2.12 billion in the third quarter, Alphabet's about negative $9.1 billion and Amazon's negative $42.4 billion over twelve months. Between 26 October and 5 November 2026, Meta, Oracle and Alphabet raised more than $70 billion of new equity and debt. Oracle's new notes were priced at coupons of up to 8.65%. The cost of financing is now part of the cost of the build-out.

How we count capex

We use each company's reported capital expenditure for calendar years. Microsoft's fiscal year ends in June, so its calendar figures are sums of four fiscal quarters, marked DERIVED. Oracle's fiscal year ends in May and is shown separately. We use the basis each company reports and do not adjust for it: Amazon net of proceeds and incentives, Microsoft and Meta including finance leases, Alphabet purchases of property and equipment. Combined totals are therefore an order of magnitude, not a like-for-like sum. Guidance is shown as guidance and never on the same line as outcomes. Off-balance-sheet leases and joint ventures are excluded.
