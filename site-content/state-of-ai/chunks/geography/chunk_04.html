3. Models

China has closed most of the frontier-model gap, to 2.7% in March 2026, and leads the world in open-weight distribution

On the most widely cited head-to-head measure, the distance between the best American and best Chinese models has nearly disappeared. Stanford HAI's AI Index reports that the top US model led the top Chinese model by 9.26% on the Chatbot Arena leaderboard in January 2024 and by 1.70% in February 2025, when DeepSeek-R1 briefly matched the US leader. The 2026 AI Index puts the gap at 2.7% in March 2026, with Arena scores of 1,503 and 1,464. The gap widened slightly over the year to March 2026, which suggests the two countries are now trading places within the noise of the leaderboard rather than China still converging towards a distant US frontier.

Other indexes give the same picture with different details. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index in September 2026, Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max scored 58 and Moonshot's Kimi K3 57.1, against 58.9 for OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol; two Chinese models sat in the global top four. Those scores need care: Qwen3.8-Max's score moved from 53 to 56 to 58 after re-runs, and the top-ranked US entry, Anthropic's Claude Fable 5, was withdrawn within a week of launch under US export controls. The US still releases more frontier-class models. Stanford counted 40 notable US models and 15 Chinese in 2024; for 2025 the 2026 AI Index gives either 50 and 30 or 59 and 35, depending on which version of its Epoch AI data is read.

The clearer Chinese lead is in open-weight models, where Chinese labs publish weights and the leading US labs do not. Chinese models accounted for 41% of downloads on Hugging Face in the year to February 2026, against 36.5% for US models, according to Hugging Face's spring 2026 report; a narrower MIT and Hugging Face measure for August 2024 to August 2025 found 17.1% against 15.8%. On OpenRouter, Chinese open-weight models went from 1.2% of tokens in late 2024 to an average of about 13% over the following year, and one venture firm estimates about 61% by May 2026. Each platform is a partial view, but every one of them moves in the same direction.

Research volume also favours China, influence less so. Stanford's 2026 AI Index reports that China was granted 74.2% of the world's AI patents in 2024, 97,206 of 131,121, against 12.1% for the US, up from 69.7% in 2023. China's share of AI citations, 20.6%, is well below its patent share, and US work still accounts for more of the most-cited papers. Europe, despite producing a large share of AI publications, released only three notable models in 2024. The geography of models is therefore three-layered: the US leads at the closed frontier, China leads in open distribution and research volume, and the rest of the world mostly builds on one or the other.

Why the gap figure moves

The US-China gap that Stanford reports is a difference in crowd-sourced Elo-style ratings on one leaderboard, LMArena (formerly Chatbot Arena), at one moment. Small changes in which model is on top, how many votes it has received, or how a provider's endpoint behaved on the day can move it by a point or more. Other composite indexes, such as Artificial Analysis, weight reasoning, coding and knowledge tests differently and sometimes re-run models, as happened when Qwen3.8-Max moved from 53 to 58. A gap below about three percent should be read as rough parity. The more meaningful signals are sustained leadership over several quarters and differences on specific workloads such as agentic coding.
