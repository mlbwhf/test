Spending

In 2026 to date, big-tech capex passed the 2025 total by September and every forecast was raised

  1. Gartner forecast on 16 September 2026 that worldwide AI spending will reach about $2.67 trillion in 2026, up 49.5% year on year and its third upward revision of 2026 this year.
  2. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spent about $508 billion on capital expenditure from January to September 2026, already more than in all of 2025, by Report AI's sum of their quarterly results.
  3. IDC raised its forecast for 2026 AI infrastructure spending to $521 billion on 6 November 2026, from $497 billion in July, after second-quarter spending of $121.4 billion.
  4. Menlo Ventures estimated on 3 November 2026 that US enterprise AI deployment spending was running at an annualised $176.8 billion in September 2026, with Anthropic taking 55% of enterprise LLM API spend.
  5. Gartner forecasts $29.2 billion of spending on standalone AI agents and assistants in 2026 (16 September 2026), against $206.5 billion for all software with agentic features built in (5 May 2026).

From January to September 2026, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spent about $508 billion on capital expenditure, more than in all of 2025, including about $207 billion in the third quarter. Their full-year guidance adds up to roughly $720–740 billion. Meta, Alphabet and Amazon spent more than their operations generated in cash, and between 26 October and 5 November Meta, Oracle and Alphabet raised more than $70 billion of new equity and debt. Oracle guided to $90–95 billion of gross capex for its fiscal year to May 2027. Amazon's nine-month total of $172.2 billion was larger than any company's full-year capex in 2025. Microsoft's third-quarter spending of $45.2 billion fell short of its own outlook of more than $50 billion because some third-party capacity was delayed, and Alphabet's free cash flow was about negative $9.1 billion in the quarter.

Forecasts rose throughout the year. Gartner's 2026 forecast for worldwide AI spending went from $2.52 trillion in January to $2.67 trillion on 16 September, up 49.5% on 2025. IDC's 2026 AI infrastructure forecast went from $487 billion to $521 billion after second-quarter spending of $121.4 billion. On these forecasts, AI spending will be about 42% of Gartner's $6.39 trillion of IT spending in 2026, by our calculation. IDC's second-quarter figure was up 53% on a year earlier, and servers made up 98% of its value. Gartner's September forecast split out spending on AI agents and assistants and raised its growth forecast for generative-AI models from 110% to 117%. Every forecast for 2026 made during the year was revised upward.

Menlo Ventures put US enterprise AI deployment spending at an annualised $176.8 billion in September, on a new definition, with Anthropic taking 55% of enterprise API spending. Gartner forecasts $29.2 billion for standalone AI agents and assistants. Public money stayed small: the UK launched a £500 million Sovereign AI Fund in April, Abu Dhabi's MGX closed a $49 billion fund in July, and Brookings counted $7.2 billion of US federal AI contract obligations by May. Menlo's figure is not comparable with its $37 billion for 2025. Google's share of enterprise API spending fell to 9% and OpenAI's rose to 31%. Gartner's broader $206.5 billion for 2026 counts all software with agentic features built in. A Futurum survey in September found 46.9% of enterprises over their AI budget in the second half, and Brookings' contract total was almost entirely defence.
