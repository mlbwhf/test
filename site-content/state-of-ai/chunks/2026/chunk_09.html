In 2026 to date, AI-attributed cuts more than doubled to 121,401 and then fell sharply, while rehiring, AI use and AI demand all rose

  1. Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 121,401 US job cuts attributed to AI from January to October 2026, more than twice the 2025 total, but only 1,265 of them came in October, the lowest month of the year.
  2. Gallup found in May 2026 that 52% of US employees used AI at work at least a few times a year and 15% used it daily.
  3. Indeed Hiring Lab data show that 7.34% of US job postings mentioned AI-related terms on 30 September 2026, up from 4.44% at the end of 2025.
  4. Stanford Digital Economy Lab's August 2026 update found that employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in AI-exposed US occupations was 19% below where it would have been had it kept pace with less-exposed peers, using payroll data through June 2026.
  5. Robert Half found in 2026 that 32% of US hiring managers who eliminated a role primarily because of AI had later rehired for it, typically in hybrid roles paying 20–35% more.

AI-attributed US job cuts rose to 121,401 in January–October 2026, more than twice the 2025 total and about 19.7% of the 617,189 cuts announced in the period, by Report AI's division of Challenger's figures. AI became the most-cited reason for US cuts in March, at 25% of that month's total, and May alone accounted for about 38,600 AI-attributed cuts by Report AI's subtraction. The count then fell each month to 1,265 in October, the lowest of the year, and by Challenger's 5 November report 'market/economic conditions' had overtaken AI as the leading reason year to date. Total announced cuts were down 44% on 2025, and October's 43,994 was 71% below October 2025.

Reversals became measurable. Robert Half found that 32% of US hiring managers who cut a role primarily because of AI later rehired for it, typically at 20–35% higher pay, and Forrester reported that 55% of employers regret AI-related layoffs. Meta cancelled the second wave of its Project OT cuts after AI-assisted code changes rose 220% and major incidents 40%. Young workers in exposed jobs fell further behind: Stanford's August 2026 update put their employment 19% below the path of less-exposed peers. US software-development postings recovered to 81.6 on 30 October 2026, 18.4% below February 2020, with senior roles leading the gain. Forrester's forecast that half of AI-attributed layoffs would be reversed by year-end resolves in January 2027.

Use of AI at work passed half of US employees. Gallup found in May 2026 that 52% used AI at least a few times a year, 30% a few times a week and 15% daily. The share of US Indeed postings mentioning AI reached 7.34% on 30 September 2026, and PwC's June 2026 Barometer reported a 62% advertised premium for AI skills. Aggregate effects remained small: in NBER's February 2026 study, 89% of executives reported no effect of AI on labour productivity over the previous three years, and the St. Louis Fed's series put time saved at about 2.2% of US work hours in the first quarter. These are 2026 figures to date.
