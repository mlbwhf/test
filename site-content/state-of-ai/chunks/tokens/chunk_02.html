1. Price per token

A dollar buys about 188 times more GPT-4-level output than in 2023, but at OpenAI's frontier it buys only about 9% more than in 2024

There is no single price of a token, and most confusion about AI costs comes from comparing different prices. We track three. The first holds capability fixed: what is the cheapest way to buy output as good as GPT-4 was when it launched in March 2023? The second follows the frontier: what does the newest flagship model cost at list price? The third, covered in chapter 2, is the average price the market actually pays, weighted by usage. All three are expressed per million tokens and converted to tokens per dollar using a blend of three input tokens to one output token, which is close to the mix of a typical chat or coding request. The blend matters because output tokens cost four to eight times more than input tokens on most price lists.

At the fixed GPT-4 bar the gain is large and mostly in the past. GPT-4 listed at $30 per million input tokens and $60 per million output tokens in March 2023, a blended $37.50, or about 26,667 tokens per dollar. By December 2024 DeepSeek-V3, which reports a higher MMLU score than GPT-4, listed at $0.27 and $1.10: about 2.1 million tokens per dollar, 78 times more. DeepSeek's September 2025 price raised that to about 3.2 million, and by October 2026 small models from OpenAI and Anthropic at $0.10 and $0.50 reached 5 million. That is 188 times the 2023 figure, but only 2.4 times the 2024 figure. Our series uses named models with a published MMLU above GPT-4's, so it is a conservative floor; smaller models that pass the bar on other tests are cheaper still.

The frontier tells a different story. OpenAI's flagship bought about 229,000 tokens per dollar in 2024 (GPT-4o at $2.50 and $10), about 291,000 in 2025 (GPT-5 at $1.25 and $10) and about 250,000 in October 2026 (GPT-6.1 Sol at $2 and $10). Within 2026 the range was wide: GPT-5.6 Sol listed at $5 and $30 in July, only 88,889 tokens per dollar, before OpenAI halved prices in September. Anthropic's Opus tier moved from 33,333 tokens per dollar for Claude 3 Opus in 2024 to 100,000 for Opus 4.5 in late 2025 and 125,000 for Opus 5.5 in 2026, while its top Fable 5.1 tier sits at 50,000. Google went the other way, scheduling Gemini 4 Argon's price to double to $4 and $20 on 30 November 2026.

The pattern is consistent with how a16z and Epoch AI describe the market. A given capability falls in price quickly once several vendors and open-weight models can supply it: a16z put the rate at about ten times a year in 2024, Epoch measured a median of about 50 times a year in early 2025, and Epoch's 2026 work implies about 13 times a year. New frontier capability is priced for what buyers will pay and gets cheaper only when competitors catch up. So a claim that AI is getting cheaper is true at a fixed capability level and largely untrue at the frontier. These are list prices; negotiated enterprise discounts, batch rates and cached-input pricing, which Anthropic lists at $0.10 to $0.25 per million, can lower actual bills considerably.

How we count tokens per dollar

A token is a chunk of text, roughly three-quarters of an English word. Providers charge separately for input tokens (what you send) and output tokens (what the model writes), and output is usually four to eight times dearer. To turn two prices into one we use a blend of three input tokens to one output token: blended price = (3 x input + output) / 4. Tokens per dollar is one million divided by that blended price. A different blend changes the level but rarely the direction. Reasoning models add a third kind of token, hidden 'thinking' output billed at the output rate, which is why a cheap per-token price can still mean an expensive task. Tokenizers also differ: the same paragraph can be 30% more tokens on one model than another.
