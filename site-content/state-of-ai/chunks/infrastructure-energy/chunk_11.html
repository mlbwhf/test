Fact check

Numbers to stop quoting

Widely repeatedWhat the evidence supports
“A ChatGPT query uses about 2.9 Wh, ten times a Google search.”That is a 2023–24 top-down estimate. Google measured 0.24 Wh for a median Gemini text prompt (Aug 2025), and a 2026 Microsoft Research study puts a typical query at a median 0.31 Wh; long reasoning responses are about 3.9 Wh. · detail
“OpenAI now runs 10 gigawatts of compute.”OpenAI said in April 2026 it had secured 10 GW of US capacity; reporting reads this as contracted. The company said it ended 2025 with about 1.9 GW available.
“Stargate is a $500 billion US government programme.”Stargate is a private joint venture (OpenAI, Oracle, SoftBank, MGX). The federal role at the Ohio site is a leased former DOE facility and permitting, not appropriated money. · detail
“Nvidia is guaranteeing $250 billion of Stargate debt.”Nvidia's guarantee for the Ohio site was cut to up to $105 billion in August 2026 and covers only phase one (~5 GW); chip financing of up to ~$350 billion is separate. · detail
“US data centres will use 12% of US electricity by 2028.”12% is the top of LBNL's 6.7–12% scenario range for 2028 (325–580 TWh), not a forecast. EPRI's 2026 update gives 9–17% by 2030, also as scenarios. · detail
“xAI's Colossus 2 became the world's first gigawatt AI training cluster in January 2026.”This is Elon Musk's claim (17 Jan 2026). Satellite analysis of cooling capacity suggested roughly 350 MW at the time; independent confirmation of 1 GW was not found.
“Nvidia has 94% of the AI chip market.”94% is Jon Peddie Research's Q4 2025 share of add-in-board GPUs, mostly gaming cards. Estimates of Nvidia's AI accelerator revenue share in 2026 range from about 70–75% (Bloomberg Intelligence, forecast) to about 85% (Morgan Stanley). · detail
Outlook

2027 will test whether contracted gigawatts become energised megawatts on schedule, and whether grids and turbine makers can keep pace

The 2024–2026 record establishes that money and chips are available in quantity; the open questions for 2027 are physical. OpenAI's 10 contracted gigawatts, Meta's Hyperion and El Paso campuses, the Ohio PORTS site and the next wave of custom accelerators all depend on power arriving on time. Gas turbines are booked years ahead, nuclear restarts and new reactors arrive from 2027 into the 2030s, and the largest US grid has failed two consecutive reliability targets. We expect vendor revenue to keep rising into 2027 on current guidance, but we will treat any widening gap between reported chip shipments and energised capacity as the main sign that the build-out is outrunning its inputs.

Three findings would change our reading. A sustained fall in GPU rental prices back below $2 an hour would indicate that capacity has caught up with demand. A capacity auction that clears below its cap with no shortfall would show supply responding to price. And published operating-gigawatt figures from OpenAI, Meta or xAI that close most of the gap with their contracted numbers would show that announcements are being delivered. Equally, guidance cuts from Nvidia or Broadcom, or project cancellations like the dropped 600-megawatt Abilene expansion repeated at scale, would indicate that financing or power, not demand, has become the limit.

Definitions
Gigawatt (GW) of data-centre capacity
A unit of power, not energy. For data centres it may refer to planned design capacity, contracted capacity or operating IT load; 1 GW running all year uses about 8.8 TWh.
IT load
Power drawn by servers, storage and networking, excluding cooling and other facility overhead. Epoch AI's Abilene estimate is IT load.
Terawatt-hour (TWh)
A unit of electrical energy equal to one billion kilowatt-hours; used for annual consumption.
PUE (power usage effectiveness)
Total facility energy divided by IT energy. A PUE of 1.1 means 10% overhead for cooling and power conversion.
CoWoS
Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate, TSMC's advanced-packaging process that places a GPU and high-bandwidth memory on one substrate; measured in wafers per month.
HBM (high-bandwidth memory)
Stacked DRAM placed beside an accelerator to supply data quickly; HBM3E and HBM4 are current generations.
XPU / custom ASIC
An accelerator designed for one customer's workloads, such as Google's TPU or Amazon's Trainium, often co-designed with Broadcom or Marvell.
H100-equivalent
A way of expressing mixed GPU fleets as the number of Nvidia H100 chips with the same computing power.
Neocloud
A cloud provider specialising in GPU capacity, such as CoreWeave, Nebius or Lambda, as distinct from the large general-purpose clouds.
Preleasing
Capacity in a data centre under construction that a tenant has already committed to lease.
Capacity auction (MW-day)
A market in which a grid operator buys future availability of supply; prices are in dollars per megawatt per day and are recovered from all customers.
PPA (power purchase agreement)
A long-term contract to buy electricity, capacity or clean-energy attributes from a specific generator at agreed terms.
