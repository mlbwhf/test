1. Chips

Nvidia earned $164 billion from data centres in six months of 2026, but custom accelerators are now growing faster from a smaller base

The AI build-out is, first, a semiconductor purchase. Nvidia's data-centre revenue was $115.2 billion in the fiscal year ended January 2025, $193.7 billion in the year ended January 2026, and $164.2 billion in just the first six months of fiscal 2027, February to July 2026. The latest quarter, to 26 July, was $89.0 billion, up 117% on the year and 18% on the quarter before. Data centres now account for more than nine-tenths of Nvidia's revenue, and the company reported a 75% gross margin in that quarter. The company guided total revenue for the quarter to October 2026 to $108 billion, plus or minus 2%, without any data-centre compute sales to mainland China. Measured by money, there has been no slowdown in 2026: each quarter of the year has been larger than the last, and the sequential increase has stayed close to a fifth.

What Nvidia sells has changed as much as how much. In the quarter to April 2026, data-centre compute was $60.4 billion, up 77%, while networking was $14.8 billion, up 199%; in the quarter to January 2026 networking had already risen 263%. The company is increasingly selling whole racks and the fabric that ties tens of thousands of GPUs together, not individual chips. Its new disclosure also splits data-centre revenue into hyperscale customers ($38 billion in the April quarter) and a second group covering AI clouds, industrial and enterprise buyers ($37 billion). Roughly half of the revenue therefore comes from outside the largest cloud providers, including neoclouds and sovereign buyers. The faster growth rates sit with custom silicon. Broadcom, which designs accelerators (XPUs) for individual cloud customers and supplies AI networking, reported AI semiconductor revenue of $12.2 billion in fiscal 2024 and about $20 billion in fiscal 2025, then $8.4 billion, $10.8 billion and $16.7 billion in the first three quarters of fiscal 2026: $35.9 billion in total, with the latest quarter up 221%. XPUs were 73% of that quarter's AI revenue. Broadcom guides to about $58 billion for fiscal 2026 and has given outlooks of about $115 billion for fiscal 2027 and $230 billion for fiscal 2028; these are company forecasts, not results. TrendForce's January 2026 forecast puts custom-ASIC systems at 27.8% of 2026 AI-server shipments, growing 44.6% against 16.1% for GPU-based systems.

AMD is the merchant alternative. Its Data Center segment, which includes EPYC server CPUs as well as Instinct GPUs, grew from $12.6 billion in 2024 to $16.6 billion in 2025, and reached $21.0 billion in the first nine months of 2026. Third-quarter revenue of $8.5 billion was up 97% and made up 64% of the company's sales, helped by the launch of the MI450 series. The OpenAI agreement for 6 gigawatts of AMD GPUs, with a first gigawatt from the second half of 2026, is the main test of whether a second GPU supplier can be deployed at gigawatt scale. AMD's growth in 2025 was held back partly by export limits on its China-specific MI308; Nvidia took a $4.5 billion charge on H20 inventory for the same reason in 2025.

Market share is the figure most often quoted and least well measured. Published estimates of Nvidia's share of AI-accelerator revenue in 2026 range from about 70–75% (Bloomberg Intelligence, a forecast through 2030) to about 85% (Morgan Stanley, March 2026), with a widely repeated 92% for 2025 traceable only to secondary sites. The spread comes from whether in-house chips such as Google's TPUs are counted, and whether shares are measured by revenue or units. We mark the share CONTESTED.
