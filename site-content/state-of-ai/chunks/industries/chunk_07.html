Comparison

Between 2024 and 2026 the sector ranking barely moved; what changed was the level of practitioner use and the volume of regulated AI products

Comparing 2024, 2025 and 2026 shows three patterns. First, the order of sectors is stable. Information and technology firms led on Ramp in November 2024 (65.9%) and still led in 2026 (about 80%); finance was second throughout; Eurostat's top two sectors were the same in 2024 and 2025. Second, practitioner use in regulated professions converged at high levels: physicians went from 66% to 81%, UK undergraduates from 66% to 95% over three academic years, and legal professionals from 79% to 85% on Clio's measure. Third, the regulated product pipeline grew, with FDA AI device authorizations rising from 235 in 2024 to 335 in 2025 and continuing at a similar pace in the first half of 2026.

Across sectors in 2026 the clearest contrast is between individual and organizational adoption. In healthcare, 81% of physicians use AI but only 43.4% of health-care businesses on Ramp pay for it and 22% of healthcare organizations had implemented domain-specific tools by 2025. In education, 95% of UK students use AI but 38% get tools from their university. In finance and software the gap is smaller, because firms buy tools centrally. Retail, construction and hospitality show low figures on both measures. Outcome evidence follows a different order: healthcare has the most controlled studies, while banking and software, the largest spenders, rely mainly on vendor or company figures.

Several apparent year-on-year jumps are partly definitional and should not be quoted as growth. The Census Bureau's measured rate roughly doubled when it changed its question in November 2025; Pew's teen figure doubled when the question widened from ChatGPT to any chatbot; the federal use-case count rose 69% when research uses were added; and Gartner restated 2025 spending upwards by about $300 billion. Where a series broke, this report shows both readings and does not compute a growth rate.
