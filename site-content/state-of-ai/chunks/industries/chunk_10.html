Fact check

Numbers to stop quoting

Widely repeatedWhat the evidence supports
“The FDA has authorized about 1,250 AI medical devices (1,247).”1,247 was the July 2025 list update. The list reached 1,614 entries in its 4 September 2026 update (decisions through 29 June 2026). · detail
“Gartner forecasts $2.59 trillion of AI spending in 2026, up 47% from about $1.5 trillion in 2025.”Gartner's 16 September 2026 forecast is about $2.67 trillion for 2026 (+49.5%), and it restated 2025 to about $1.79 trillion from the $1.48 trillion it published in September 2025. The figure is a forecast, not an outcome. · detail
“Generative-AI traffic to retail sites is up 1,300%.”+1,300% was the 2024 holiday season (Adobe). The 2025 holiday season was +693.4%, and the latest monthly reading, September 2026, was +71% year on year from a much larger base.
“Teen use of ChatGPT for schoolwork doubled from 26% to 54%.”Pew's 26% (fall 2024) asked about ChatGPT only; the 54% (fall 2025) asks about any chatbot. The question changed, so the rise is not a like-for-like doubling. · detail
“Only about 5-10% of US businesses use AI, according to the Census Bureau.”That was the old BTOS question (AI used to produce goods or services). From November 2025 the Census asks about any business function, and the rate was 19.8% as of 3 May 2026; the two series are not comparable.
“Only about 30% of lawyers use AI.”That figure (about 11% in 2023 to 30% in 2024) comes from a different survey series than Clio's, which found 79% of legal professionals using AI in 2024 and 85% reporting firm-level adoption in 2026. Cite the survey and its definition with the number. · detail
Outlook

In 2027 the test for industry AI shifts from adoption to measured outcomes, and the earliest signals will come from healthcare, finance and representative business surveys

Adoption by practitioners is close to its ceiling in medicine, higher education, law and software, so further growth in those headline figures will tell us little. The more informative indicators for 2027 are representative business adoption, which at about one firm in five in the Census survey still has room to rise, the gap between the most and least digital sectors, and the volume and type of regulated AI products. A narrowing of the sector gap would mean AI tools have found uses in physical and customer-facing work; a stable gap would mean adoption is saturating within knowledge work.

The bigger open question is outcomes. By the end of 2027 there should be more controlled studies of ambient scribes, coding agents and AI tutoring, and the second year of McKinsey-style EBIT questions under higher adoption. If controlled studies keep finding single-digit or low double-digit time savings, and the share of organizations reporting EBIT impact stays below 40%, the case that AI is transforming sector productivity will remain unproven, whatever the adoption rates. If replicated trials show larger effects on output or patient outcomes, that view would change.
