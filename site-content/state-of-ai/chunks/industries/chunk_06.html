5. Retail, marketing, manufacturing and government

Outside the knowledge industries adoption is lower and harder to measure, and the best data track customer behaviour rather than firm deployment

Retail is a large AI spender but a modest adopter by firm count. IDC estimated retail AI spending at about $25 billion in 2024, third among industries. Yet Ramp found 48.8% of retail businesses paying for AI in August 2026, below the 55.2% average, and the Census Bureau found about 14% of retail businesses using AI in May 2026, among the lowest rates of any sector. The difference reflects concentration: a handful of large chains account for most retail AI spending, while hundreds of thousands of small stores do not buy AI tools. Retail's AI story is therefore told mainly through large-retailer disclosures and through measures of shopper behaviour, not through representative adoption surveys. IDC does not publish retail AI spending after 2024.

Shopper behaviour is changing faster than retailer adoption. Adobe Analytics found that traffic from generative-AI sources to US retail websites rose 1,300% in the 2024 holiday season and 693.4% in the 2025 season, with AI-driven revenue per visit more than doubling; by September 2026 the monthly year-on-year increase had slowed to 71% from a much larger base. Salesforce estimated that AI and agents influenced $229 billion of global online holiday sales in 2024, about 19% of orders, and $262 billion in 2025, about 20%. Retailers with their own agents grew sales 6.2% against 3.9% for peers. 'Influenced' is Salesforce's attribution model, not a causal estimate, and the growth rates come from a small base, so these figures show direction rather than size of effect.

Marketing is where AI use inside firms is best measured over time. The CMO Survey, run by Duke's Fuqua School of Business with Deloitte and the American Marketing Association, found generative AI used in 7.0% of marketing activities in spring 2024, 15.1% in 2025 and 22.4% in 2026; marketers expect more than half within three years, a forecast rather than an outcome. Menlo Ventures put creator-tool spending at $360 million in 2025. McKinsey's surveys name technology, media and telecom as the sectors furthest along with AI agents. Manufacturing has no comparable three-year series. Ramp found 61.1% of manufacturers paying for AI in August 2026, and Rockwell Automation's 2026 survey of about 1,500 manufacturers estimated that 34% of operations are AI-augmented, but neither measures what happens on the factory floor.

Government adoption is measured by inventories, not surveys. US federal agencies reported 2,133 AI use cases across 41 agencies in 2024 and 3,611 across 56 agencies in the 2025 inventory released in April 2026, an increase of about 69%. Much of that rise is a reporting change: research and development uses were added, NASA's count went from 18 to 425, and nine agencies reported for the first time. High-impact use cases rose from 351 to 445 but fell as a share, from 16.5% to 12.3%, under a new definition. Federal AI contract obligations totalled $5.6 billion across fiscal years 2022 to 2024, 72% of it at the Department of Defense (GAO; site page 374). No comparable series exists for state, local or non-US governments.

Counting use cases is not counting use

Government AI inventories and corporate 'use case' tallies count projects an organization chooses to report, not how much work AI does. A single chatbot pilot and a fraud-scoring system that processes millions of transactions each count as one. Counts also move with reporting rules: the 2025 US federal inventory rose 69% partly because research uses were added and nine agencies reported for the first time. Use-case counts are useful for tracking breadth and for spotting high-impact systems that need oversight, but they are not a measure of adoption depth, spending or outcome, and they should not be compared across organizations that define a use case differently.

6. Spending and outcomes

Spending is concentrating in banking, software and healthcare, but sector-level returns remain mostly unmeasured

Total AI spending is measured well; spending by industry is not. Gartner put worldwide AI spending at $987.9 billion in 2024 in its September 2025 edition and now puts 2025 at about $1.79 trillion, restated from the $1.48 trillion it published a year earlier. Its September 2026 forecast for 2026 is about $2.67 trillion, up 49.5%, with about 56% going to infrastructure. These totals include data-centre hardware, AI-enabled devices and services, and they are not split by buying industry. They are also forecasts for a year in progress, so they should not be compared with the measured adoption figures elsewhere in this report. Gartner's January and May 2026 editions put 2026 at $2.52 trillion and $2.59 trillion; each revision has been upwards, driven mostly by infrastructure.

IDC's industry guide is the only public source that ranks industries by total AI spending, and its public figures stop at 2024. Banking led at about $31.3 billion, retail followed at about $25 billion, and software and information services completed the top three, which together accounted for about $89.6 billion, roughly 38% of global AI spending tracked by IDC. An earlier IDC edition put 2023 banking spending at $20.6 billion, but editions differ in scope, so the two years are not compared here. Manufacturing and healthcare are not separately disclosed in public IDC materials. The industry ranking by total spend is therefore a 2024 snapshot, not a trend. Until IDC or another tracker publishes 2025 and 2026 industry totals, any claim that one sector's AI spending is growing faster than another's rests on survey data or company disclosures.

For generative-AI software bought for a specific industry, Menlo Ventures' annual enterprise survey gives a two-year view. Healthcare spending rose from about $500 million in 2024 to $1.5 billion in 2025, 43% of a $3.5 billion vertical-AI total, and legal from $350 million to $650 million. Menlo's separate healthcare report put 2025 healthcare AI spending at $1.4 billion. Across departments rather than industries, coding took about 55% of departmental generative-AI spending in 2025. These are survey-based estimates from about 500 US enterprise buyers, and Menlo restated its earlier years to exclude inference, so growth multiples should be read as approximate. The vertical total itself, $3.5 billion, is small next to the roughly $37 billion Menlo estimates enterprises spent on generative AI overall in 2025, most of it on horizontal tools and model access.

Measured returns lag both spending and adoption. McKinsey's 2026 survey found 89% of organizations using AI in at least one function but only 37% reporting any EBIT impact, and about 6% qualifying as high performers. At sector level, the controlled evidence comes from a short list of studies: the MASAI mammography trial (29% more cancers detected), the JAMA scribe study (about 16 minutes a day of documentation saved), the METR developer trial (19% slower) and the Stanford legal-tool test (17-33% hallucination rates). Each covers one task in one setting. None measures industry-wide productivity, and the sectors spending most, banking and software, publish the fewest independent outcome measures. Until more sectors publish controlled results, adoption and spending figures should be read as measures of activity, not of value.

Why AI spending figures disagree

Gartner, IDC and Menlo Ventures measure different things. Gartner's worldwide total ($2.67 trillion forecast for 2026) includes AI-optimized servers, data centres, AI-enabled phones and PCs, and services, and is not split by buying industry. IDC tracks vendor revenue for AI software, hardware and services by the industry that buys them, which is why banking appears as a $31.3 billion line. Menlo surveys enterprise buyers about generative-AI software only, so its healthcare figure is $1.5 billion. Each is internally consistent; none can be added to or ranked against another. Gartner also revises its past years, as with 2025, restated from $1.48 trillion to about $1.79 trillion.
