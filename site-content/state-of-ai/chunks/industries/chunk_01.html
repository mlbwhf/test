At a glance
  • Ramp's AI Index showed 55.9% of US businesses on its platform paying for AI tools in September 2026; in August, technology firms were at 80.9% and accommodation and food services at 33.1%, a gap of about 2.4 to 1.
  • The US Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey found 19.8% of US employer businesses using AI as of 3 May 2026, with the information sector at 39.7% and finance and insurance at 33.9%.
  • The American Medical Association found that 81% of US physicians used AI in their practice in a survey of 1,692 physicians fielded from 15 January to 2 February 2026, up from 66% in November 2024.
  • The FDA's AI-Enabled Medical Device List reached 1,614 entries in its 4 September 2026 update, after a record 335 AI device authorizations with 2025 decision dates (other counts of 2025 range from 295 to 333).
  • HEPI found that 95% of UK full-time undergraduates used AI in December 2025, and Clio's 2026 Legal Trends Report found that 85% of legal professionals reported some level of AI adoption at their firms.
  • IDC estimated that banks spent about $31.3 billion on AI in 2024, more than any other industry, while Menlo Ventures found healthcare the largest buyer of vertical generative-AI software at $1.5 billion in 2025.
  • Controlled studies show smaller gains than adoption rates suggest: a JAMA multisite study published in April 2026 found ambient AI scribes cut documentation time by about 16 minutes a day, and METR's July 2025 trial found experienced developers 19% slower with early-2025 AI tools.

In this report
  1. 1. Adoption across sectors — Every comparable survey ranks information, finance and professional services first and hospitality, construction and retail last, with a gap of more than two to one
  2. 2. Healthcare — Healthcare moved from pilots to practice: 81% of US physicians use AI and the FDA authorized a record number of AI devices in 2025, but measured gains remain narrow
  3. 3. Financial services and software — Finance and software lead on adoption and spending, but the best controlled evidence on productivity is mixed
  4. 4. Education and legal — In education and law, individual use is near-universal while institutions, assessment rules and verification lag behind
  5. 5. Retail, marketing, manufacturing and government — Outside the knowledge industries adoption is lower and harder to measure, and the best data track customer behaviour rather than firm deployment
  6. 6. Spending and outcomes — Spending is concentrating in banking, software and healthcare, but sector-level returns remain mostly unmeasured
