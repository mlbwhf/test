Definitions
Ramp AI Index
The monthly share of US businesses on Ramp's corporate card and bill-pay platform that pay for at least one AI subscription or contract, reported overall and by industry. It measures paid adoption among Ramp's customers, not use across the whole economy.
Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS)
A US Census Bureau survey of employer businesses fielded every two weeks. Since the 17 November 2025 collection it asks whether a business used AI in any of 15 business functions; before that it asked about AI used to produce goods or services.
FDA AI-Enabled Medical Device List
The FDA's public list of medical devices authorized for US marketing that it identifies as using AI, dated by decision. It is updated periodically, back-filled and, by the FDA's own description, not comprehensive.
510(k) clearance
The FDA pathway for devices shown to be substantially equivalent to a device already on the market. Most AI devices are cleared this way, which does not require new clinical outcome trials.
Domain-specific AI tool
In Menlo Ventures' healthcare survey, AI software built for a healthcare task (such as ambient documentation or coding) rather than a general-purpose chatbot.
Vertical AI
AI software sold for one industry, such as healthcare or legal. Menlo Ventures estimates vertical generative-AI spending separately from horizontal tools and coding.
Ambient AI scribe
Software that listens to a clinical consultation and drafts the visit note for the clinician to review and sign.
AI use case inventory
The annual list of AI applications that US federal agencies report to the Office of Management and Budget. It counts reported projects, not usage volume or spending.
AI-influenced sales
Salesforce's estimate of online orders in which shoppers interacted with AI or agents during the purchase journey. It is an attribution model, not a measure of sales caused by AI.

Frequently asked

Which industry uses AI the most in 2026?

Information and technology businesses lead on every comparable survey. Ramp's AI Index showed 80.9% of technology firms on its platform paying for AI in August 2026, against 55.2% for all businesses, and the US Census Bureau found 39.7% of information-sector businesses using AI as of 3 May 2026, against a national rate of 19.8%. Finance and insurance is second on both (73.7% on Ramp; 33.9% on Census).

How many AI medical devices has the FDA authorized?

The FDA's AI-Enabled Medical Device List reached 1,614 entries in its 4 September 2026 update, covering decisions through 29 June 2026, up from 1,016 in December 2024. The September 2026 snapshot attributes a record 335 authorizations to 2025 and 235 to 2024; other counts of 2025 range from 295 to 333. Roughly three-quarters of all listed devices are in radiology.

What share of doctors use AI?

The American Medical Association found that 81% of US physicians used AI in their practice in a survey of 1,692 physicians fielded from 15 January to 2 February 2026, up from 66% in November 2024 and 38% in 2023. The average physician reported 2.3 use cases, most commonly summarizing research and drafting documentation.

Which industry spends the most on AI?

IDC ranked banking first, at about $31.3 billion of AI spending in 2024, with retail at about $25 billion and software and information services in the top three; together the three reached about $89.6 billion. For vertical generative-AI software, Menlo Ventures found healthcare largest at $1.5 billion in 2025, 43% of the $3.5 billion vertical total, ahead of legal at $650 million. No public source updates IDC's industry split for 2025 or 2026.

Is AI improving results in these industries?

Controlled evidence is limited and mixed. A JAMA multisite study published in April 2026 found ambient AI scribes cut clinicians' documentation time by about 16 minutes a day (about 10%); METR's July 2025 randomized trial found experienced open-source developers took 19% longer with early-2025 AI tools; and McKinsey's 2026 survey found only 37% of organizations reporting any EBIT impact from AI. Most sector-level gains published so far are vendor-reported.

Methodology & sources

Figures are compiled from company disclosures, regulators, research firms and reputable reporting, and cross-checked against Report AI's own index pages. Each value carries its as-of date, source and a confidence rating: HIGH where the primary source was read, MEDIUM where it rests on consistent reputable secondary reporting (most figures, because many primary pages cannot be opened from our research environment), DERIVED where it is our own arithmetic, and CONTESTED where credible sources disagree. A year means the calendar year the figure describes; 2026 values are the latest available and marked “to date”. Weekly and monthly user counts, and forecasts and outcomes, are never compared as if they were the same measure. Exhibits plot only dated, sourced readings; lines are not interpolated between them, and values with different definitions are never placed on one axis. Known gaps in this edition: Manufacturing has no three-year series: only Ramp's 61.1% of manufacturing firms paying for AI (Aug 2026) and Rockwell's estimate that 34% of operations are AI-augmented (May 2026). Eurostat's 2025 manufacturing share could not be verified (17.3% circulates but conflicts with Eurostat's statement that all sectors outside the top two were below 16% in 2024).; IDC's industry split is paywalled: no public 2025 or 2026 banking, retail or healthcare spending figure; Gartner does not split by industry.; Government: the 2026 federal AI use-case inventory is not yet published; no comparable state/local or non-US government adoption series was found. The 2025 federal count (3,611) is inflated by a reporting change.; Census BTOS sector rates were found only for information (39.7%), finance and insurance (33.9%) and retail (about 14%) as of May 2026; the 24.6% national reading for October 2026 is unverified.; Ramp's information-sector value for December 2025 was not found; Ramp expanded its sample in June 2026 and revises history, so earlier readings may move.; No AMA physician survey was fielded in 2025; no 2026 Menlo healthcare or enterprise vertical figures yet (expected December 2026); Eurostat 2026 data due December 2026.; FDA counts by year differ by snapshot and method (2025: 295-335; Q1 2026: 81 vs 92); FDA's own page could not be read, so all FDA figures are from third-party tabulations of the list.; Media and marketing has only the CMO Survey series; no newsroom or publisher AI adoption series was found.; Primary sites for Eurostat, the Census Bureau and FRED were unreachable from this environment; their figures are rated MEDIUM from consistent secondary reporting.; Clio's 2026 Legal Trends Report publication date is unconfirmed, and its 2026 wording ('some level of AI adoption at their firms') may differ from earlier years' individual-use question.; Site consistency: pages 863 and 938 still show 1,247 FDA devices; page 376 shows Gartner's superseded $2.59T and '2025 ~$1.5T'; page 865 labels Pew's 13% as 2023 (survey year) while this report files school surveys by academic year..
