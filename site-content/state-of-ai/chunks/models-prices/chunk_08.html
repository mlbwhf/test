Comparison

Across 2024, 2025 and 2026, capability rose to the ceiling of every established test while the price of the old flagships fell by two-thirds or more, and the gaps between labs, countries and licences narrowed to a few percent.

Set side by side, the three years tell a consistent story with one turn in it. 2024 was the year previews broke the old benchmarks and inference for last year's capability became nearly free. 2025 was the year reasoning models delivered on those previews: an officially certified IMO gold, the first SWE-bench Verified score above 80%, and DeepSeek-R1 matching o1 at a twentieth of its price. 2026 is the year the measuring instruments ran out: SWE-bench Verified, GPQA Diamond and the IMO no longer separate the leading models, and the gaps between US and Chinese and between closed and open models are a few percent on Arena.

The turn is in prices and disclosure. Flagship list prices fell steeply from 2024 to 2025 and then stopped falling at the top: Opus-tier output went from $75 to $25 to $20 per million tokens, but Anthropic and OpenAI both added a premium tier above their old flagships in 2026, and Google scheduled a price rise. At the same time the inputs to frontier models became less visible. Training compute and cost can be estimated for 2024 and 2025 models but not for 2026 ones, and transparency scores fell. The result is a market where what models can do is measured more precisely than ever, and what they cost to build is measured less precisely than at any point since 2022.
