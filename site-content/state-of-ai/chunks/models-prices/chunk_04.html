Pacing pledgePolicy & events2026-09

The September 2026 commitment by Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and xAI to pace the frontier did not slow releases in any way visible from outside. Within days, OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol and Luna on 22 September at half the API price of their predecessors, and Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 the same day at a lower price than Opus 4.8. Pacing was defined as narrowing the gap between what models can do and what evaluators can verify, not as shipping less, so this is not necessarily a breach. It does mean the commitment has little observable surface: the release timeline in Exhibit 3 shows no gap after 12 September, and Google's Gemini 4 Argon followed on 13 October.

Chinese and open-weight releases are now part of the same calendar rather than a lagging one. DeepSeek-V3 and R1 bracketed the turn of 2024–25; DeepSeek V4-Pro followed in April 2026 under an MIT licence, and Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B in August 2026 under Apache 2.0, small enough to run on one consumer graphics card. In the US, Meta released Llama 5 in April 2026 and Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter open-weight model, on 15 October. The practical consequence for buyers is that the frontier moves every few weeks, so any ranking older than a month should be treated as historical. Moonshot's Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open model, scored 57.1 on the Artificial Analysis index in September 2026, within two points of the leader, without a large US-style launch event.

A release is not a product

Three dates matter for any model: when it is announced, when anyone outside the lab can use it, and whether it stays available. They increasingly differ. OpenAI's o3 posted record scores in December 2024 but shipped in April 2025. Google's Gemini 4 Argon was limited to cyber defenders for two weeks before general availability. Claude Fable 5 was available for less than a week. In this report a model's year is the year it became generally available, and scores from previews are labelled as such.
