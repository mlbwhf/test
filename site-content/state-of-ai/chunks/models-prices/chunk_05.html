3. Competition

The US–China and closed–open gaps narrowed to near parity in early 2025, then reopened slightly in 2026, and their size depends on which ruler is used.

Stanford HAI's AI Index tracks two gaps on the Arena leaderboard, where people compare anonymous model answers side by side. In January 2024 the top US model led the best Chinese model by 9.26%; by February 2025, after DeepSeek-R1, the lead was 1.70%; in March 2026 it was 2.7%. Over the same dates, the top closed-weight model's lead over the best open-weight model went from 8.04% to 1.70% and then to 3.3%, having touched 0.5% in August 2024. Both series show the same shape: rapid convergence through 2024 and early 2025, followed by a modest reopening in 2026 as the closed US labs shipped a new generation. Stanford also notes that Anthropic, xAI, Google, OpenAI, Alibaba and DeepSeek sit within 25 Elo points of one another, so the top of the board is effectively a cluster.

Other instruments give different sizes for the same gaps. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, a composite of reasoning, knowledge, mathematics and coding tests, Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max scored 58 and Moonshot's Kimi K3 57.1 in early September 2026, against 58.9 for OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol. The open-weight gap on the same index was about 13 points in spring 2025 and about 6 in September 2026, but Artificial Analysis's stricter version 5, built largely on private test sets, showed 12 points in October 2026, and about 26 if only models with published weights count. Epoch AI's capabilities index measures the gap in time instead: open-weight models have lagged the closed frontier by about four months on average since January 2026, against about three months from 2023 to late 2025.

Volume still favours the US. Stanford HAI, using Epoch AI's dataset, counted 40 notable models from US institutions in 2024 against 15 from China and 3 from Europe. For 2025 the AI Index 2026 gives 50 for the US and 30 for China in its research chapter, while Stanford's web page gives 59 and 35; on either count, China's output rose from about 38% of the US figure to about 60%. Europe's count fell from 21 in 2023 to 3 in 2024. The number of notable models is a measure of breadth, not quality, and it is sensitive to how Epoch classifies a release as notable. Counts for 2026 will not be available until the AI Index 2027, expected in spring 2027.

The strategic difference between the two countries is now openness rather than capability. China's leading labs publish weights: Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4-Pro and the smaller Qwen models are downloadable, and in April 2026 all ten of the top open-weight models on the Artificial Analysis index came from Chinese labs. Alibaba's models passed 3 billion downloads in August 2026. The leading US labs mostly do not publish weights, although Meta's Muse Spark Open and Nvidia's Nemotron models are exceptions. Stanford HAI also reports that the average Foundation Model Transparency Index score fell from 58 to 40 in 2025, a sign that the closed frontier is disclosing less about how its models are built just as open alternatives get closer.

Why the open-weight gap depends on the ruler

Arena measures human preference in head-to-head chats; Artificial Analysis averages scores on fixed test sets; Epoch converts capability into months of lag. Each answers a different question, and each changes over time: Artificial Analysis's October 2026 version 5 moved most questions to private sets, which cut open models' scores more than closed ones. Definitions matter too. Counting a model as open before its weights are published turns a 26-point gap into a 12-point one. Quote the instrument, the version and the date with any gap figure.
