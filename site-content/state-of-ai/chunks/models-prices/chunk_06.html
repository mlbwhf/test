4. List prices

Frontier list prices fell sharply in 2025 and then stabilised, while a new premium tier appeared above the old flagships.

Measured by the list price of each lab's top model, the cost of frontier AI fell steeply between 2024 and 2025. Anthropic's Opus tier went from $15 per million input tokens and $75 per million output tokens for Claude 3 Opus (March 2024) to $5 and $25 for Claude Opus 4.5 (November 2025), and to $4 and $20 for Claude Opus 5.5 (September 2026), a price Report AI verified on Anthropic's pricing page on 1 October 2026. OpenAI's newest flagship launched at $15 and $60 in December 2024 (o1), at $1.25 and $10 in August 2025 (GPT-5) and at $2 and $10 in September 2026 (GPT-6 Sol). Between those launches, prices moved up as well as down: GPT-5.6 Sol listed at $5 and $30 in July 2026 before GPT-6 Sol halved it.

What changed in 2026 is the shape of the price ladder, not the level of the old flagships. Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.1 lists at $10 and $50, a tier above Opus that did not exist in 2025, and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra is priced at about five times GPT-6 Sol. Google's Gemini 4 Argon became generally available on 13 October 2026 at an introductory $2 and $10 and will rise to $4 and $20 when that period ends on 30 November, a scheduled price increase. At the bottom of the ladder the movement is the opposite. Anthropic's Claude Haiku 5.5, launched on 7 October 2026, lists at $0.10 and $0.50 for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, a tenth of Haiku 4.5's $1 and $5.

The open-weight price floor sits far below both. DeepSeek listed V3 at $0.27 and $1.10 in December 2024 and R1 at $0.55 and $2.19 in January 2025, about 96% below o1's $15 and $60 for comparable reasoning results. DeepSeek V4-Pro, released in April 2026 under an MIT licence, costs about $0.87 per million output tokens and reports 80.6% on SWE-bench Verified, against Claude Opus 4.8's vendor-reported 88.6% at $25. Getting those eight extra points cost about 29 times as much per output token (DERIVED: $25 ÷ $0.87). Qwen3.8-27B, which scores 52 on the Artificial Analysis index against 58.9 for the leader, has no per-token price at all for anyone who runs it on their own hardware.

Capability per dollar is improving faster than any single list price suggests. Stanford HAI measured the price of GPT-3.5-level performance falling from $20 per million tokens in November 2022 to $0.07 in October 2024, and Epoch AI found price declines at fixed capability with a median of about 50 times a year across benchmarks in its 2025 analysis. Those curves describe last year's capability becoming cheap, not the newest model getting cheaper. Two further cautions apply. Reasoning models spend many more tokens per task, so a lower price per token can still mean a higher bill per task. And list prices exclude batch discounts, caching and negotiated enterprise rates. The market-wide price that buyers actually pay, and token volumes, are covered in our separate State of AI Tokens report.

List price versus what buyers pay

A list price is the published rate for one model. What buyers pay on average is lower and depends on the mix of models they use, batch discounts (Anthropic halves prices for batch jobs), cached input (as low as $0.10 per million tokens) and enterprise contracts. Silicon Data's usage-weighted index, which captures that mix, read $0.97 per million tokens on 1 September 2026, far below any frontier list price. Both figures are correct; they measure different things. This report uses list prices to compare models; our Tokens report covers market prices and volumes.
