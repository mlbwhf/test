Models & Prices

In 2024 preview models broke the established benchmarks and the price of last year's capability collapsed, while the US still produced most notable models.

  1. Stanford HAI's AI Index 2025 found that the inference price of GPT-3.5-level performance fell from $20 per million tokens in November 2022 to $0.07 in October 2024, a more than 280-fold decline.
  2. The ARC Prize Foundation reported on 20 December 2024 that OpenAI's o3 preview scored 75.7% on the ARC-AGI-1 semi-private evaluation within the $10,000 compute limit.
  3. OpenAI reported in December 2024 that its o3 preview resolved 71.7% of SWE-bench Verified coding tasks, a figure Stanford HAI uses as the 2024 high point.
  4. Stanford HAI counted 40 notable AI models from US institutions in 2024, against 15 from China and 3 from Europe.
  5. On the Chatbot Arena leaderboard in January 2024, the top closed-weight model led the best open-weight model by 8.04% and the top US model led the best Chinese model by 9.26%, according to Stanford HAI's AI Index 2025.

2024 opened with the closed US labs clearly ahead. In January the top US model led the best Chinese model by 9.26% on the Arena leaderboard, and the top closed-weight model led the best open-weight model by 8.04% (Stanford HAI). The year's releases came about every six weeks: Claude 3 in March, GPT-4o in May, Claude 3.5 Sonnet in June, Meta's open-weight Llama 3.1 405B in July and OpenAI's o1 reasoning model in preview in September. MMLU, the reference test since 2020, stopped separating models when o1 reported 92.3%. Gemini 1.5 Pro offered a 2-million-token context window, while most flagships worked with 128,000 to 200,000 tokens.

The capability records of the year came from a model that was not yet on sale. In December OpenAI's o3 preview reported 71.7% on SWE-bench Verified and 87.7% on GPQA Diamond, and the ARC Prize Foundation verified 75.7% on ARC-AGI-1 within its $10,000 compute limit. Google DeepMind's AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 reached silver-medal standard at the International Mathematical Olympiad with 28 of 42 points, on problems translated into formal language by people. On 26 December DeepSeek released DeepSeek-V3 with open weights, and with it much of the US–China gap closed within weeks.

Prices for established capability fell further than in any earlier year. Stanford HAI measured GPT-3.5-level inference at $0.07 per million tokens in October 2024, down from $20 in November 2022. Frontier prices did not follow: o1 launched in December at $15 and $60 per million input and output tokens, and Claude 3 Opus listed at $15 and $75. The US produced 40 notable models to China's 15 and Europe's 3, and the AI Index's widely cited estimate puts the cost of training Llama 3.1 405B, about 3.8×10^25 FLOP, at roughly $170 million.
