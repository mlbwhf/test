In 2024 AI became the largest destination for venture money while the labs' revenue stayed in single-digit billions

  1. Crunchbase counted more than $100 billion of venture funding to AI companies in 2024, about a third of the roughly $314 billion invested in startups worldwide that year.
  2. OpenAI raised $6.6 billion at a $157 billion valuation in October 2024, and Databricks' $10 billion round in December 2024 was the largest venture raise of the year, according to Crunchbase.
  3. Nvidia reported data-centre revenue of $115.2 billion for its fiscal year ended January 2025, roughly calendar 2024, up 142% on the year before.
  4. OpenAI generated about $3.7 billion of revenue in 2024 and ended the year at an annualised run-rate of about $5.5 billion, according to figures the company confirmed in June 2025.
  5. Anthropic's S-1, as reported by Reuters in 2026, shows revenue of roughly $400 million and an operating loss of $2.98 billion for 2024.

In 2024 AI became the main destination for venture capital. Crunchbase counted more than $100 billion of funding to AI companies, up more than 80% on 2023 and about a third of the roughly $314 billion invested in startups worldwide; PitchBook put AI at 46.4% of US deal value. The largest rounds went to a few companies: Databricks raised $10 billion at $62 billion in December, OpenAI $6.6 billion at $157 billion in October, and xAI $6 billion twice, ending the year valued at $45–50 billion. Anthropic, priced at about $18 billion early in the year, took a further $4 billion from Amazon without a disclosed valuation. Global venture funding as a whole was nearly flat, at about $314 billion against $304 billion in 2023, so AI's gain came largely at the expense of other sectors. Stanford HAI counted corporate AI investment of $252.3 billion, a broader measure.

The labs' revenue was small next to the money committed to them. OpenAI earned about $3.7 billion, reportedly lost about $5 billion, and ended the year at an annualised run-rate of about $5.5 billion. Anthropic's later S-1 shows revenue of roughly $400 million and a $2.98 billion operating loss. Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta spent about $246 billion on capital expenditure, about 60 times the two labs' combined revenue by Report AI's calculation. In June, Sequoia's David Cahn set out 'AI's $600B question': the annual revenue the industry would need to justify its infrastructure spending. OpenAI's run-rate had roughly doubled during the year, and Anthropic's reached about $1 billion around its end. Neither company published audited accounts, so the 2024 figures rest on reported projections and on the S-1 Anthropic filed two years later.

The supplier was the one company whose AI income could be measured. Nvidia's data-centre revenue reached $115.2 billion in the fiscal year to January 2025, up 142%. Exits were scarce. No AI company of scale went public, and consolidation ran through acquisitions and licensing-and-hiring deals; Mergermarket counted $49.9 billion of M&A involving AI startups across 454 deals, both records. The financing model of the following two years was already in place: private rounds for the labs, cash flow for the hyperscalers, and almost no public-market pricing of AI companies. Nvidia's sales were the main channel through which AI money reached public markets, since the labs themselves were private. Report AI found no large AI IPO in 2024 and could not establish the year's largest AI acquisition, a gap recorded in the data file.
