Infrastructure & Energy

In 2024 AI infrastructure was supply-constrained by chips and packaging, and power became a planning problem rather than an operating one

  1. Nvidia reported data-centre revenue of $115.2 billion for its fiscal year ended January 2025, which approximates calendar 2024, up 142% on the prior year.
  2. The International Energy Agency estimates that data centres worldwide used about 415 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2024, roughly 1.5% of global electricity consumption.
  3. Microsoft agreed on 20 September 2024 to buy the full output of Constellation's 835-megawatt Three Mile Island Unit 1 reactor for 20 years, the largest tech-company nuclear deal announced that year.
  4. CBRE reported that vacancy across North America's primary data-centre markets fell to a record-low 2.8% in the first half of 2024, with a record 3,872 megawatts under construction.
  5. By October 2024 Nvidia H100 GPUs were being rented on resale markets for about $2 per GPU-hour, down from around $8 during the 2023 shortage, according to an analysis published by Latent Space.

Nvidia's data-centre revenue reached $115.2 billion in the fiscal year that ended in January 2025, up 142%, and its total revenue $130.5 billion. AMD's Data Center segment grew 94% to $12.6 billion, with Instinct accelerators above $5 billion, and Broadcom's AI revenue rose 220% to $12.2 billion. TrendForce estimates AI-server shipments grew 46%. The limiting factor was not demand but packaging: TSMC ended the year with roughly 35,000 wafers a month of CoWoS capacity. In the rental market, the 2023 shortage unwound. Prices for H100s that had reached about $8 an hour fell to about $2 on resale markets by October, as early reserved contracts were resold and fewer new model developers were training from scratch. The year's growth was concentrated in a single supplier and a few cloud buyers.

Electricity use was still modest in absolute terms. The IEA estimates that data centres worldwide used about 415 terawatt-hours in 2024, roughly 1.5% of global electricity, and EPRI later put US data centres at 177–192 terawatt-hours, or 4–5% of national use. What changed was the outlook. In December the Department of Energy published Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory's estimate of 176 terawatt-hours for 2023 alongside scenarios of 325–580 terawatt-hours by 2028, and the widely quoted per-query figure, about 2.9 watt-hours for a ChatGPT request, framed the public debate. That figure was a top-down estimate for an earlier model generation and was later shown to be about ten times higher than measurements of typical prompts.

Hyperscalers began contracting firm power directly. In September Microsoft agreed to buy the full output of the 835-megawatt Three Mile Island Unit 1 reactor for 20 years, a restart of a shut reactor contracted to a single buyer, and in October Google agreed to buy power from up to 500 megawatts of Kairos small reactors. Grid markets registered the coming load: PJM's July auction cleared at $269.92 per megawatt-day for 2025/26, with two data-centre-heavy zones above $440. In North America's primary markets, CBRE reported record-low vacancy of 2.8% and a record 3,872 megawatts under construction in the first half, nearly 80% of it preleased.
