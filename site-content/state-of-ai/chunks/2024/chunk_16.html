Industries

In 2024 AI spread fastest among individual professionals in data-heavy industries, while firm-level adoption and measured outcomes stayed thin

  1. The FDA's AI-Enabled Medical Device List counted 1,016 authorized AI devices in its 20 December 2024 update, and the September 2026 snapshot of the list attributes 235 authorizations to calendar year 2024.
  2. The American Medical Association found that 66% of US physicians used AI in their practice in its November 2024 survey of 1,183 physicians, up from 38% in 2023.
  3. IDC estimated that banks spent about $31.3 billion on AI in 2024, more than any other industry, with retail at about $25 billion.
  4. Ramp's AI Index showed 36.4% of US businesses on its platform paying for AI tools in November 2024, with finance and insurance at 51.2% and information-sector firms at 65.9%.
  5. Clio's 2024 Legal Trends Report found that 79% of legal professionals used AI, up from 19% in 2023.

In 2024 AI adoption by industry followed the digital intensity of the work. Ramp's index showed 36.4% of US businesses on its platform paying for AI tools in November 2024, with information-sector firms at 65.9% and finance and insurance at 51.2%. Eurostat found 13.5% of EU enterprises using AI, with the information and communication sector at 48.72% and professional, scientific and technical services at 30.53%; every other sector was below 16%. The Census Bureau's survey, on its original narrower question, put US business use at roughly 5% in mid-2024. Professionals moved faster than their employers: the AMA found 66% of US physicians using AI in November 2024, up from 38% in 2023, and Clio found 79% of legal professionals using AI, up from 19%.

Regulated AI products accumulated steadily. The FDA's AI device list reached 1,016 entries in its December 2024 update, and the September 2026 snapshot of the list attributes 235 authorizations to 2024, slightly above 2023's 226. About three-quarters of listed devices were in radiology, so most clinical AI in use was imaging software. Menlo Ventures estimated that only about 3% of US healthcare organizations had implemented domain-specific AI tools, and healthcare was the largest vertical buyer of generative-AI software at about $500 million. Stack Overflow found 62% of developers using AI tools, with 43% trusting their accuracy.

Spending was concentrated in a few industries. IDC estimated banking AI spending at about $31.3 billion and retail at about $25 billion, with software and information services completing a top three worth about $89.6 billion. Gartner put worldwide AI spending at $987.9 billion. Consumer behaviour began to shift in retail: Adobe recorded a 1,300% rise in generative-AI referral traffic to US retail sites in the holiday season, from a small base, and Salesforce estimated that AI influenced $229 billion of global online holiday sales. Measured outcomes were few, and the most cited one came from legal research, where Stanford researchers found leading tools hallucinating on 17-33% of queries.
