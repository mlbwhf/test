2. Valuations

The leading labs are valued at 15 to 25 times their annualised revenue, and every 2026 round priced higher than the one before it

Private valuations of AI labs rose faster than any other measure in this report. OpenAI was valued at $157 billion in October 2024, $300 billion in March 2025, $500 billion in an October 2025 employee share sale, $852 billion after its March 2026 round and a reported $1.25 trillion on 6 November 2026. Anthropic's priced rounds went from about $18 billion in early 2024 to $183 billion in September 2025, $380 billion in February 2026 and $965 billion in May 2026. xAI was valued at $45–50 billion in December 2024 and at $250 billion when it merged into SpaceX in February 2026. Mistral, Europe's largest lab, doubled to more than €21 billion in September 2026. DeepSeek, which took no outside money until 2026, was valued at about $86 billion in October (CONTESTED).

Set against revenue, the multiples are high but no longer in the hundreds. On its $1.25 trillion valuation and a run-rate of almost $50 billion, OpenAI is priced at about 25 times annualised revenue. Anthropic's $965 billion Series H in May 2026 came when its run-rate was about $47 billion, about 21 times, and its July run-rate of more than $65 billion brings that to under 15 times. Cursor was bought for $60 billion on reported annualised revenue of $2.6–4 billion, 15 to 23 times. For comparison, the same ratio for OpenAI in October 2024 was about 29 times ($157 billion on $5.5 billion). On this measure revenue has kept pace with valuations, though only for the leading labs. Mistral and DeepSeek do not disclose revenue, so no multiple can be computed for them.

Two caveats limit what these multiples show. First, the revenue figures are not like for like: Anthropic counts some revenue from cloud partners gross, while OpenAI reports net. Earlier reports that put OpenAI's run-rate near $70 billion counted partner revenue gross; on its own basis OpenAI reports about $50 billion. Second, the valuations differ in kind. Some come from priced primary rounds, some from employee secondary sales, one from a strategic investment that implied a price without setting one, and one from an all-stock merger between two companies controlled by the same founder. A $250 billion valuation for xAI inside SpaceX is not the same kind of number as a $380 billion priced Series G. Our tables therefore keep the type of valuation beside each figure.

The November 2026 OpenAI round is the first sign of resistance to these prices. Bloomberg and Reuters reported that OpenAI closed about $11 billion at $1.25 trillion, below an earlier ask of $1.4–1.5 trillion, and that the round may reach $20 billion rather than the $30 billion first sought. Outlets do not say whether $1.25 trillion is a pre-money or post-money figure. Even so, it is about 47% above the $852 billion of March. It is the first round in this series that priced below what the company sought, which makes it a data point to watch rather than a turn. A lower price on later tranches of the same round, or a final close well below $20 billion, would be a clearer signal.

Priced, implied and secondary valuations

A priced round sets a valuation because new money buys new shares at a stated price: Anthropic's $30 billion Series G at $380 billion is an example. An implied valuation is inferred from the terms of a strategic investment that does not set a price; Microsoft and Nvidia's November 2025 investment in Anthropic implied about $350 billion. A secondary sale prices existing shares sold by employees or early investors and raises no money for the company, as in OpenAI's $500 billion sale in October 2025. A merger valuation is set by negotiation between the two parties. Our valuation series uses priced rounds where they exist and labels every other kind.
