Comparison

Between 2024 and 2026 every measure of money flowing into AI grew between three- and fifty-fold, and only the gap between capex and lab revenue shrank

Comparing the three years shows how far the market moved. Venture funding to AI rose from about $100 billion in 2024 to $202.3 billion in 2025 and at least $488 billion in the first nine months of 2026 (Crunchbase basis; 2026 DERIVED), and AI's share went from about a third to about half to about 72%. The largest single round grew from Databricks' $10 billion to OpenAI's $40 billion and then its $122 billion. OpenAI's valuation rose about eightfold, from $157 billion to a reported $1.25 trillion, and Anthropic's priced valuation about fiftyfold, from about $18 billion to $965 billion. Nvidia's data-centre revenue went from $115.2 billion to $193.7 billion, and reached $164.2 billion in the first half of fiscal 2027 alone.

Revenue is the measure that grew fastest from the smallest base. Anthropic went from about $0.4 billion of revenue in 2024 to $16.3 billion in the first half of 2026, and OpenAI from about $3.7 billion to about $12.4 billion in the same half-year. Because revenue grew faster than capex, big-tech capex per dollar of lab revenue fell from about 60 to about 13. The financing side moved the other way. Bond issuance by the largest builders went from an average of about $28 billion a year to $194 billion in the first half of 2026, and in 2026 equity raising and off-balance-sheet vehicles became standard funding tools. The industry's income grew, but so did its reliance on outside capital.
