6. Exits

2026 reopened the exit window, and the year's largest IPO and largest startup acquisition were both AI deals

For two years AI wealth was almost entirely private. No AI company of scale went public in 2024. CoreWeave's March 2025 IPO raised $1.5 billion at $40 a share, below a $47–55 target range, and finished its first day flat. Consolidation ran through acquisitions and licensing-and-hiring deals instead: Mergermarket counted $49.9 billion of M&A involving AI startups across 454 deals in 2024, both records, and Nvidia's roughly $20 billion licensing deal with Groq in December 2025 was the largest AI transaction of that year, though not a standard acquisition. Investors could own the labs only through private rounds or through their cloud partners. Cognition's purchase of Windsurf in July 2025 showed another route, in which AI-native companies bought one another for products and customers.

That changed in the first half of 2026. Cerebras raised $5.55 billion at $185 a share in May, above its range, and rose 68% on its first day. SpaceX, which had combined with xAI in February at a $250 billion valuation for xAI, priced a $75 billion IPO at $135 a share on 11 June, valuing it at $1.77 trillion. Five days later it agreed to buy Anysphere, Cursor's maker, for $60 billion in stock, which Crunchbase counts as about half of the $119.8 billion spent on venture-backed acquisitions in 2026 to date. Other AI deals followed, including Nvidia–Hugging Face (about $12.9 billion), Schneider–PTC (about $22.6 billion), AMD–World Labs ($8.2 billion) and Salesforce–Braze ($5.66 billion). The public market was now pricing AI companies directly, not only through their suppliers and cloud partners.

Anthropic is the test of how far the public market will follow private valuations. It filed a confidential S-1 on 1 June 2026 and a public one on 26 October, naming Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley among 22 banks, Nasdaq as the exchange and ANT as the ticker. The filing sets no price range. Reported valuation talk has fallen from about $2 trillion in pre-IPO markets in September to about $1.5 trillion, still well above the $965 billion of May. OpenAI's chief executive ruled out a 2026 IPO in September, but Amazon's $35 billion contingent commitment is tied to a listing by 2028. Anthropic's filing describes it as a controlled company under Nasdaq rules, with a trust and its co-founders holding voting control.

Outside the US, the year's most important financing was DeepSeek's first outside money. The company had taken no external funding in 2024 or 2025. In mid-2026 it raised about $7.4 billion at a post-money valuation of about $52–59 billion, with Tencent, CATL and China's national AI fund among the investors, according to Caixin and Bloomberg. Founder Liang Wenfeng reportedly put in about 20 billion yuan. In October 2026 a second round of about $15–16 billion valued it at between $74 billion pre-money and $90 billion; the amount and valuation are CONTESTED. Even at the top of that range, DeepSeek is valued at less than a tenth of Anthropic. China's national AI fund holding equity directly also makes it a different kind of investment from the US rounds above.
