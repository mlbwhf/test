3. Revenue and losses

Lab revenue has grown faster than at any software business on record, and Anthropic reached an operating profit in 2026, but losses still roughly match revenue

The revenue base under these valuations is real and growing very fast. OpenAI's recognised revenue went from about $3.7 billion in 2024 to about $13.07 billion in 2025, according to leaked audited accounts reported by the Financial Times, and to about $12.4 billion in the first half of 2026 alone. Anthropic's S-1 shows roughly $400 million in 2024, about $4.6 billion in 2025 and $16.3 billion in the first half of 2026, when second-quarter revenue was $11.6 billion. Run-rates show the same direction: OpenAI's rose from about $5.5 billion in December 2024 to almost $50 billion in September 2026, and Anthropic's from about $1 billion around the turn of 2025 to more than $65 billion in July 2026. Anthropic's first-half 2026 revenue alone was more than three times its revenue for all of 2025.

Losses grew too, and the headline net figures exaggerate them. OpenAI's 2025 net loss of about $38.5 billion was mostly a non-cash charge of about $41.55 billion tied to its restructuring. Its operating loss was $20.92 billion, and it rose to about $21.6 billion in the first half of 2026 alone, on quarterly figures relayed from investor materials. Anthropic's first-half 2026 net loss of about $80 billion was mainly a $72.4 billion fair-value charge. Its operating result was a $693 million loss for the half, including a $974 million operating profit in the second quarter, the first profitable quarter in any period its filing reports. Anthropic's operating loss had been $2.98 billion in 2024 and $8.06 billion in 2025. Net losses of this kind say little about cash.

Cash is a different measure again, and the least well documented. OpenAI burned about $3.7 billion of cash in the first quarter of 2026, more than half of that quarter's $5.7 billion revenue, according to The Information. Estimates of its 2025 cash burn range from about $8 billion, after stripping out non-cash items and Microsoft compute credits, upward. Projections for 2026 range from $14 billion to $27 billion, and higher on a GAAP basis. Anthropic ended 2025 with $20.28 billion of cash and short-term investments. Its S-1 also lists about $265.6 billion of fixed compute commitments. Those commitments, not the current losses, are the larger claim on future revenue. Neither company publishes regular cash-flow statements outside these filings and leaks, so cash burn is the least reliable figure in this report.

The question in this chapter is whether revenue will outgrow costs before the funding runs out. Anthropic's second quarter suggests that one lab can reach operating profit at about $45 billion of annualised revenue while it is still expanding compute. OpenAI's widening operating loss suggests the opposite for the other. Neither result settles the question. Anthropic's profit is one quarter. Both companies have signed compute commitments that rise steeply from 2027. Neither discloses the margin on inference at scale. The figures to track are Anthropic's third-quarter revenue, which an outside forecast puts near $16.8 billion, and whether OpenAI's quarterly operating loss stops growing. Two more quarters of results, starting with Anthropic's first report if it lists, should settle more than any forecast can.
