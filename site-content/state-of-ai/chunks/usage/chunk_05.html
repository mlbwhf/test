4. Organisations and workers

Nine in ten organisations use AI, but only three in ten US employees use it at work a few times a week or more

Organisational adoption has reached its ceiling on the broadest measure. McKinsey's State of AI surveys found that 78% of organisations used AI in at least one business function in mid-2024, 88% in mid-2025 and 89% in the survey fielded from 4 May to 8 June 2026, among 1,719 respondents in 97 countries. The one-point change between 2025 and 2026 is within what a change of sample could produce. The measure is generous by design: an organisation counts if any one function uses AI regularly, so a single customer-service chatbot qualifies. It answers whether organisations have started, and nearly all have. It does not answer how much work AI does inside them, which the same surveys suggest is still limited: only 37% of respondents in 2026 reported any EBIT impact from AI, against 39% in 2025.

Surveys of individuals show a slower and more uneven climb (Exhibit 6). Gallup found that the share of US employees using AI at work at least a few times a year rose from 27% in the second quarter of 2024 to 46% in the fourth quarter of 2025 and 52% in May 2026. Frequent use, a few times a week or more, rose from about 12% in 2024 to 23% in the third quarter of 2025 and 30% in May 2026, and daily use reached 15%. The St. Louis Fed's Real-Time Population Survey, run by Bick, Blandin and Deming, found that 33.3% of US adults aged 18 to 64 used generative AI for work in August 2024, 37.4% in August 2025 and 45% in May 2026. The two series agree on the shape: about half of US workers use AI at all, and fewer than a third use it routinely.

Set side by side, enterprise and consumer adoption tell the same story from opposite ends. Organisations adopted quickly and broadly because adoption is cheap to claim: one licence or pilot qualifies. Individuals adopted outside work faster than inside it. In the St. Louis Fed survey, 62% of US adults aged 18 to 64 used generative AI for any purpose in May 2026, against 45% for work. Pew found that 49% of US adults used AI chatbots in February 2026 and 44% had used ChatGPT. The populations differ, organisations, employees and all adults, so the gaps between the bars in Exhibit 6 are illustrative rather than ratios. But the order is the same in every source: organisational adoption first, private use second and routine use at work last.

Microsoft's 2024 Work Trend Index, which found that 75% of knowledge workers in 31 countries used generative AI at work, is often quoted alongside these figures and should not be. It surveyed knowledge workers, a group far more exposed to AI than the general workforce, and 78% of its AI users said they brought their own tools rather than using ones their employer provided. The 2024 figure is closer to a ceiling for desk-based professionals than an estimate for workers as a whole, and Microsoft has not published a comparable figure since. The general-population surveys are the better guide to how far AI has spread through work. They put it at roughly half of US workers in 2026, with routine use concentrated in a minority and daily use at about one worker in seven.

Why the adoption surveys disagree

McKinsey asks executives whether their organisation uses AI regularly in any business function. Gallup asks employees how often they personally use AI in their role. The St. Louis Fed asks a representative sample of US adults aged 18 to 64 whether they used generative AI, at work or outside it. Microsoft's Work Trend Index surveyed knowledge workers only. Each question captures a different population and threshold, so their levels differ by design. The St. Louis Fed also changed its question order in August 2025 and restated its August 2024 adult figure from 39.4% to 44.6%; both numbers are kept, marked RESTATED. Compare each series only with itself across years.
