Frequently asked

How many people use ChatGPT in 2026?

OpenAI said ChatGPT had more than 1 billion weekly active users on 31 August 2026, up from more than 900 million on 27 February 2026 and 800 million in October 2025. OpenAI also reported more than 50 million paying subscribers in February 2026. A figure of 1.2 billion weekly users cited at OpenAI's developer conference on 29 September 2026 may include other OpenAI products, and a figure of about 1 billion monthly app users (mid-2026) is a different metric.

Which AI assistant has the most users in 2026?

It depends on the metric, and the metrics should not be mixed. On weekly users, ChatGPT leads with more than 1 billion (OpenAI, 31 August 2026). On monthly users, Alphabet said the Gemini app passed 1 billion on 28 October 2026. Meta AI was put at about 1 billion monthly users in May 2025 but Meta has not given a 2026 total. In China, ByteDance's Doubao leads with 419 million monthly users in September 2026, according to QuestMobile.

What percentage of companies use AI in 2026?

McKinsey's 2026 State of AI survey, fielded from 4 May to 8 June 2026 among 1,719 respondents, found that 89% of organisations regularly use AI in at least one business function, against 88% in 2025 and 78% in 2024. Only 37% reported any EBIT impact from AI, so adoption is near-universal but measurable value is not.

What share of US workers use AI at work in 2026?

Gallup found that 52% of US employees used AI at work at least a few times a year in May 2026, 30% used it a few times a week or more, and 15% used it daily. The St. Louis Fed's Real-Time Population Survey found that 45% of US adults aged 18 to 64 used generative AI for work in May 2026. The two surveys use different questions and bases, so their figures differ.

What do people actually use ChatGPT for?

Mostly everyday, non-work tasks. OpenAI's NBER study of 1.5 million consumer conversations found that 73% of messages were unrelated to work in June 2025, up from 53% in June 2024. Practical guidance, seeking information and writing made up roughly three-quarters of messages; writing was 40% of work-related messages, mostly editing users' own text, and programming was only about 4.2% of all messages.

Methodology & sources

Figures are compiled from company disclosures, regulators, research firms and reputable reporting, and cross-checked against Report AI's own index pages. Each value carries its as-of date, source and a confidence rating: HIGH where the primary source was read, MEDIUM where it rests on consistent reputable secondary reporting (most figures, because many primary pages cannot be opened from our research environment), DERIVED where it is our own arithmetic, and CONTESTED where credible sources disagree. A year means the calendar year the figure describes; 2026 values are the latest available and marked “to date”. Weekly and monthly user counts, and forecasts and outcomes, are never compared as if they were the same measure. Exhibits plot only dated, sourced readings; lines are not interpolated between them, and values with different definitions are never placed on one axis. Known gaps in this edition: No primary source could be opened from this environment (mckinsey.com, frbsf.org and others failed to resolve); every value is MEDIUM or CONTESTED from consistent secondary reporting.
