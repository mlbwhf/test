1. Consumers

ChatGPT passed 1 billion weekly users in 2026, but its growth slowed from about 50 million a month in 2025 to under 20 million

ChatGPT remains the only AI assistant with a company-disclosed weekly user count, and the series is unusually complete. OpenAI reported 200 million weekly active users in August 2024, 300 million in December 2024, 400 million in February 2025, about 700 million in July 2025, 800 million in October 2025, more than 900 million on 27 February 2026 and more than 1 billion on 31 August 2026. That is a fivefold increase in exactly two years. The weekly measure matters: it counts people who came back within seven days, a stricter test of habit than the monthly figures most rivals publish, and it is the only audience figure in this report that the company itself has defined and repeated over time. Exhibit 1 plots every disclosed reading; no points are interpolated, and the 2026 milestone date should be read with the caveat in the exhibit notes.

The pace, however, has changed. Between February and October 2025 ChatGPT added about 400 million weekly users in eight months, or roughly 50 million a month. Between October 2025 and February 2026 it added about 100 million in four months, and between February and August 2026 another 100 million in six months, about 17 million a month, or 11% growth over the half-year (all DERIVED from OpenAI's milestones). Three explanations fit the data and cannot yet be separated. The easiest users to reach may already be counted; Google, Meta and Chinese apps are now absorbing first-time users through products people already have; and OpenAI announces round numbers when it crosses them, so the true curve between milestones is unknown. A further disclosure is needed before calling a plateau.

Paying users have grown at almost exactly the same rate as weekly users. ChatGPT had about 15.5 million paying subscribers at the end of 2024, according to figures OpenAI gave shareholders as reported by The Information; about 35 million Plus and Pro subscribers in July 2025; and more than 50 million paying subscribers in February 2026, according to OpenAI. Set against the weekly user count at the nearest date, that is 5.2%, 5.0% and 5.6% (DERIVED). Conversion has stayed at roughly one paying user in twenty while the base has more than tripled. Subscription scope differs between the three readings, and the July 2025 figure covers Plus and Pro only, so the ratio is approximate (Exhibit 2). No reading suggests conversion is rising or falling sharply, which means revenue from consumers has tracked audience growth rather than outrun it.

Two widely circulated figures should not be read as outcomes. A total of 122 million paying ChatGPT subscribers in 2026 matches an OpenAI internal forecast for consumer subscribers driven by the $8-a-month Go tier; it has not been disclosed as a result. OpenAI's figure of 1.2 billion weekly users, given at its developer conference on 29 September 2026, may cover OpenAI products beyond ChatGPT, and its date is disputed between 29 September and 3 November. Neither is used in this report. A third figure, about 1 billion monthly app users reported in mid-2026, is genuine but measures something different and appears only in the monthly comparison in Chapter 2. The disclosed picture is enough on its own: by August 2026 ChatGPT had passed the 1 billion weekly-user mark with a paying base of at least 50 million behind it.

2. Platforms

Gemini and Meta AI reached ChatGPT's order of magnitude through products people already used, on monthly measures that cannot be ranked against weekly ones

Two other assistants now report audiences in the hundreds of millions to a billion, but on a different measure. Alphabet said the Gemini app had 750 million monthly users at the end of 2025 and more than 1 billion on 28 October 2026, up from 350 million in March 2025 according to a slide shown at the US antitrust remedies trial. Meta said Meta AI had about 500 million monthly users in September 2024 and about 1 billion in May 2025. Both are monthly counts, which are always larger than weekly counts for the same product. Both products were also switched on inside services that already had billions of accounts: Android, Google Search and Workspace for Gemini; WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook for Meta AI. Their figures therefore measure reach as much as deliberate choice, and should be read that way.

Placed like for like, the 2026 picture is narrower than the headlines suggest (Exhibit 3). On monthly users, the measure most products report, ChatGPT's app was reported at about 1 billion in mid-2026 and the Gemini app at more than 1 billion in October 2026. China's largest assistant, Doubao, had 419 million in September 2026 according to QuestMobile, Alibaba's Qwen 158 million and DeepSeek 122.65 million. These are the only 2026 monthly figures with a named source and a date. Meta has given no Meta AI total since May 2025, and the two figures on Report AI's own pages, 1.2 billion and about 600 million, disagree. Microsoft reported 150 million monthly users across its Copilot apps for the quarter to September 2025, then switched to relative growth rates in 2026.

Anthropic's Claude is the clearest case of a product whose user count cannot be stated. Anthropic does not publish one. Third-party estimates for the same months of 2026 range from 56 million monthly app users to about 245 million monthly users across web and app, a spread of 4.4 times, and a further estimate of 271 million also circulates. Anthropic's S-1 filing of 26 October 2026 reports business metrics, including more than 11,900 customers paying at least $100,000 a year, rather than consumer users. The honest statement is a range with its methods attached. The same caution applies, less dramatically, to every figure in this chapter: even the company disclosures are self-defined and unaudited, and companies choose when, and whether, to announce them.

Microsoft's numbers show the other route to scale: selling seats to employers. Microsoft 365 Copilot had 15 million paid seats in the quarter to December 2025 and more than 30 million by July 2026, according to Microsoft's results as reported by CIO Dive and UC Today. OpenAI reported more than 1 million business customers in November 2025 and more than 2 million by the end of July 2026. These are counts of seats and organisations, not people, and they should never be added to consumer audiences. What they show is that the consumer race and the workplace race are being run with different products and different metrics. Most published league tables mix the two, along with downloads and web visits, which is the main reason they disagree with each other by factors of two to four.

How we count users

A weekly active user has used a product at least once in seven days; a monthly active user at least once in 30 days. The monthly count for the same product is always higher, often much higher, so weekly and monthly figures are never placed in one column here. Company disclosures from OpenAI, Alphabet, Meta and Microsoft are self-defined and unaudited, and companies choose when to announce. Third-party panels such as QuestMobile, Sensor Tower and Similarweb infer use from a sample of devices or browsers and carry an error band. Downloads, registered accounts and web visits are not users at all. Where credible sources disagree by a wide margin, we publish the range and mark it CONTESTED rather than choose a number.

3. China

China's AI-native apps reached 546 million monthly users in 2026, in a market measured by panels, led by Doubao and impossible to sum

China runs a separate consumer AI market, closed to the leading US assistants and measured almost entirely by one third-party tracker. QuestMobile counted 546 million monthly users of AI-native apps in September 2026, up 82.1% year on year, after 440 million in the first quarter, 461 million in April and 499 million in May (Exhibit 4). The national figure is deduplicated: a person who uses three assistants is counted once. QuestMobile tracks two other categories separately, AI features inside existing apps and assistants built into phones, whose audiences are larger still and overlap with the AI-native count, so the three cannot be added together. Unlike the US figures, none of these numbers comes from the companies themselves. They are device-panel estimates with a known error band, which is why this report rates them MEDIUM.

ByteDance's Doubao has pulled clear of the field. It had about 56 million monthly users in November 2024, according to QuestMobile figures cited in secondary reports, 226 million in December 2025 and 419.08 million in September 2026, with 181 million daily users on 30 September 2026. That is roughly a 7.5-fold rise in under two years (DERIVED). Doubao's advantage is distribution as much as product: it sits inside ByteDance's Douyin ecosystem, much as Meta AI sits inside WhatsApp. Alibaba's Qwen climbed fastest in 2026, from about 25 million monthly users in December 2025 to 167 million in May 2026, before easing to 158 million in September (Exhibit 5). By September 2026 Doubao's monthly audience was equivalent to 77% of the deduplicated national total (DERIVED: 419 / 546), though the two are not strictly a share because of overlap.

DeepSeek shows that a burst of attention does not guarantee retention. Its consumer app, launched on 11 January 2025, took about 14 days to reach 1 million users and then reportedly added around 100 million in a week at the end of January 2025. By December 2025 QuestMobile put it at 135 million monthly users, about 130 million in May 2026 and 122.65 million in September 2026. It is the only major assistant in this report whose tracked audience fell during 2026. DeepSeek's models remain widely used through APIs and open weights outside China, which an app panel does not capture, so the app figure understates DeepSeek's overall reach. It does not, however, contradict the decline of DeepSeek's own app as a consumer destination inside China.

The per-app figures cannot be summed. In September 2026 Doubao, Qwen and DeepSeek alone total 699.73 million monthly users, against a deduplicated national total of 546 million (DERIVED), so at least 154 million people are counted more than once. Any chart that adds Chinese app audiences to make a market total double-counts by a quarter or more. Nor should China's figures be stacked against US ones. ChatGPT's weekly count is a company disclosure; QuestMobile's monthly counts are panel estimates; and nobody deduplicates across the two markets, so a person using ChatGPT at work and Doubao at home is two users in every comparison published. The defensible statements are within-market: Doubao leads China, the market is still growing fast, and DeepSeek's app has lost ground.

The 3.8 billion mistake

In 2026 at least one English-language headline reported that Doubao had 3.8 billion users. The Chinese source said 3.8 yi; a yi is 100 million, so the figure was about 380 million. The tenfold error is recorded in Report AI's fact-check register. Chinese large numbers are written in units of 10,000 (wan) and 100 million (yi), and any figure that converts to more than a few hundred million users for a single Chinese app should be checked against the unit before it is repeated. The latest QuestMobile figure for Doubao is 419 million monthly users, in September 2026.

4. Organisations and workers

Nine in ten organisations use AI, but only three in ten US employees use it at work a few times a week or more

Organisational adoption has reached its ceiling on the broadest measure. McKinsey's State of AI surveys found that 78% of organisations used AI in at least one business function in mid-2024, 88% in mid-2025 and 89% in the survey fielded from 4 May to 8 June 2026, among 1,719 respondents in 97 countries. The one-point change between 2025 and 2026 is within what a change of sample could produce. The measure is generous by design: an organisation counts if any one function uses AI regularly, so a single customer-service chatbot qualifies. It answers whether organisations have started, and nearly all have. It does not answer how much work AI does inside them, which the same surveys suggest is still limited: only 37% of respondents in 2026 reported any EBIT impact from AI, against 39% in 2025.

Surveys of individuals show a slower and more uneven climb (Exhibit 6). Gallup found that the share of US employees using AI at work at least a few times a year rose from 27% in the second quarter of 2024 to 46% in the fourth quarter of 2025 and 52% in May 2026. Frequent use, a few times a week or more, rose from about 12% in 2024 to 23% in the third quarter of 2025 and 30% in May 2026, and daily use reached 15%. The St. Louis Fed's Real-Time Population Survey, run by Bick, Blandin and Deming, found that 33.3% of US adults aged 18 to 64 used generative AI for work in August 2024, 37.4% in August 2025 and 45% in May 2026. The two series agree on the shape: about half of US workers use AI at all, and fewer than a third use it routinely.

Set side by side, enterprise and consumer adoption tell the same story from opposite ends. Organisations adopted quickly and broadly because adoption is cheap to claim: one licence or pilot qualifies. Individuals adopted outside work faster than inside it. In the St. Louis Fed survey, 62% of US adults aged 18 to 64 used generative AI for any purpose in May 2026, against 45% for work. Pew found that 49% of US adults used AI chatbots in February 2026 and 44% had used ChatGPT. The populations differ, organisations, employees and all adults, so the gaps between the bars in Exhibit 6 are illustrative rather than ratios. But the order is the same in every source: organisational adoption first, private use second and routine use at work last.

Microsoft's 2024 Work Trend Index, which found that 75% of knowledge workers in 31 countries used generative AI at work, is often quoted alongside these figures and should not be. It surveyed knowledge workers, a group far more exposed to AI than the general workforce, and 78% of its AI users said they brought their own tools rather than using ones their employer provided. The 2024 figure is closer to a ceiling for desk-based professionals than an estimate for workers as a whole, and Microsoft has not published a comparable figure since. The general-population surveys are the better guide to how far AI has spread through work. They put it at roughly half of US workers in 2026, with routine use concentrated in a minority and daily use at about one worker in seven.

Why the adoption surveys disagree

McKinsey asks executives whether their organisation uses AI regularly in any business function. Gallup asks employees how often they personally use AI in their role. The St. Louis Fed asks a representative sample of US adults aged 18 to 64 whether they used generative AI, at work or outside it. Microsoft's Work Trend Index surveyed knowledge workers only. Each question captures a different population and threshold, so their levels differ by design. The St. Louis Fed also changed its question order in August 2025 and restated its August 2024 adult figure from 39.4% to 44.6%; both numbers are kept, marked RESTATED. Compare each series only with itself across years.

5. Purpose

Most ChatGPT use is personal: the non-work share of consumer messages rose from 53% to 73% in a year, and programming is about 4%

The most detailed evidence on what people do with an AI assistant comes from OpenAI's own study of 1.5 million consumer conversations, published as an NBER working paper on 15 September 2025 by researchers from OpenAI, Harvard and Duke. It found that the share of consumer ChatGPT messages unrelated to work rose from 53% in June 2024 to 73% in June 2025 (Exhibit 7). Work use still grew in absolute terms, because the user base more than doubled over the period, but personal use grew much faster. The study covers consumer plans only, so use through ChatGPT Enterprise and business plans, which is more work-oriented, is excluded. Its findings describe the general public's use of a general-purpose assistant, which is exactly the population behind the weekly user numbers in Chapter 1.

Three tasks dominate. Practical guidance, such as how-to advice, tutoring and ideas, seeking information and writing together accounted for roughly three-quarters of messages in June 2025; Report AI's reading of the paper puts the figure at about 78%. Writing was the most common work task, at 40% of work-related messages, and about two-thirds of writing requests asked ChatGPT to edit, critique, summarise or translate text the user had supplied rather than to write from scratch. Programming was about 4.2% of all messages. On the study's intent split, as cited on Report AI's country page, 49% of messages were asking for information or advice, 40% were doing, meaning producing an output, and 11% were expressing, such as reflection or companionship. Most use, in other words, is asking and doing.

These findings cut against two common assumptions. The first is that AI assistants are primarily productivity tools. By mid-2025 most consumer use was personal, and the growth of the user base came disproportionately from people using ChatGPT for everyday questions and for improving their own writing. The second is that coding leads AI use. Coding leads enterprise spending on generative AI applications, according to Menlo Ventures, but among general ChatGPT users it is a small share of messages; developers do most of their AI-assisted coding in dedicated tools that this study does not observe. Both statements can be true at once. Confusing them, by quoting enterprise spending shares as if they described what the public does, is one of the more common errors in published AI statistics.

The study has limits that matter for 2026. It is one company's data on one product, classified by automated methods the authors describe in the paper, and it ends in June 2025, before agent-style features became widespread. Report AI's country page cites a later OpenAI series in which non-work use rose from 59.8% to 68.9% of messages by March 2026. That series could not be traced to a published source and conflicts with the 73% the NBER paper reports for June 2025, so it is marked CONTESTED and not plotted. No comparable study exists for Gemini, Meta AI, Claude or the Chinese assistants. What people use those products for in 2026 is, on the public evidence, unknown, and any claim about it should be treated as an estimate.

6. Search

Billions now see AI answers in search, but AI platforms send about one website visit in 300

Search is where most people meet AI without choosing it. Google said AI Overviews reached more than 1 billion monthly users in October 2024, five months after their US launch, and more than 2 billion in July 2025. Secondary reports of Google's I/O conference put the figure at about 2.5 billion in May 2026, and said AI Mode, Google's conversational search tab, had passed 1 billion monthly users. These are counts of people shown an AI summary in the course of a normal search, not people who sought out an assistant, and they are the largest AI audience figures in this report for that reason. They are not comparable with ChatGPT's weekly users or with Gemini's monthly app users, and they are kept in a separate exhibit (Exhibit 8) rather than ranked against assistants.

The traffic those answers pass on is small. SE Ranking estimates that AI platforms accounted for about 0.02% of all website traffic in 2024, 0.24% in 2025 and 0.32% in 2026, roughly one visit in 310 (DERIVED: 1 / 0.0032). Similarweb counted an average of 770.7 million AI referral visits a month between June 2025 and May 2026, up 117.4% year on year. Both describe rapid growth from a very low base. ChatGPT generated most of it, about 80% of AI referrals in 2025 and around 75% in 2026 on SE Ranking's measure. Different panels produce different levels, and another benchmark puts the 2026 share at 0.15% to 0.25%, but the direction is the same in all of them: steep growth, still well under 1% of traffic.

When AI answers appear, people click less. Pew Research Center's analysis of 68,879 real searches, published in July 2025, found that users clicked a traditional result 8% of the time when an AI summary appeared and 15% of the time when none did. Meanwhile ChatGPT's share of chatbot web visits fell from between 76% and 86.7% in May 2025, Similarweb readings that use different comparison sets, to 53.9% in May 2026, as Gemini rose to 27.9%. ChatGPT did not shrink; rivals grew faster. The result is a search market in which AI is absorbing a growing share of questions, spread across more products, while passing very little of that attention on to the websites its answers are drawn from, a pattern every panel in this chapter confirms.

For publishers and brands the arithmetic is uncomfortable. On the evidence above, AI search reaches billions of people a month and sends a fraction of a percent of web traffic. If AI referrals kept growing at the 2025 to 2026 rate they would eventually matter, but they would need to roughly triple again to reach 1% of all traffic (DERIVED: 1 / 0.32). The more immediate effect is the one Pew measured: fewer clicks on the searches where AI answers appear. Use of AI in search is therefore best measured three ways, by exposure, by lost clicks and by referrals, and no single headline captures it. Report AI tracks all three, and treats the exposure figures as the least informative about whether people value what they are shown.

Exposure is not use

Being shown an AI Overview, having Meta AI inside WhatsApp, or having an assistant pre-installed on a phone makes someone a counted user on some measures. That is exposure. Choosing to open ChatGPT, Gemini or Doubao and returning within a week is use. Both matter, because exposure shapes what information people see, but they answer different questions. Mixing them is the most common reason published AI rankings disagree. This report keeps exposure figures, such as AI Overviews and AI Mode, in their own exhibit and never ranks them against assistant audiences.

Comparison

Between 2024 and 2026 almost every usage measure rose, but the leaders by habit, by reach and by organisational adoption are different products measured on different populations

Across the three years, every series in this report rose except one. ChatGPT's weekly users went from 300 million in December 2024 to 800 million in October 2025 and more than 1 billion in August 2026. Organisational adoption in McKinsey's surveys went from 78% to 88% to 89%. Gallup's measure of US employees using AI at work went from 27% to 46% to 52%, and the St. Louis Fed's measure of adults aged 18 to 64 using generative AI went from 44.6% to 54.6% to 62%. AI's share of website traffic went from 0.02% to 0.24% to 0.32%. The exception is DeepSeek's Chinese app audience, which fell from 135 million monthly users in December 2025 to 122.65 million in September 2026, according to QuestMobile.

The shape of growth changed from year to year. 2024 was the year of breadth inside organisations: McKinsey's adoption measure jumped from 55% in 2023 to 78%. 2025 was the year of consumer scale: ChatGPT's weekly users doubled in eight months and the personal share of its use rose to 73%. 2026 so far is a year of convergence and deceleration: ChatGPT's growth slowed to about 11% in six months, organisational adoption barely moved, and rivals, Gemini outside China and Doubao and Qwen inside it, reached comparable monthly scale. Worker use rose steadily throughout, by roughly 12 percentage points a year on Gallup's any-use measure (DERIVED: 27% in Q2 2024 to 46% in Q4 2025, then 52% in May 2026).

Across platforms, Exhibit 9 sets out every disclosed figure by metric, so that only like is compared with like. On weekly users only ChatGPT reports, so it has no comparator. On monthly users, ChatGPT's app and the Gemini app were both at about 1 billion in 2026, Doubao at 419 million and DeepSeek at 122.65 million, with Meta AI and Claude unresolved. The table also shows how thin the public record is: 10 of its 24 platform-year cells are empty or contested. That is itself a finding. Outside OpenAI's weekly series, Alphabet's and Meta's occasional monthly statements and QuestMobile's panel, the AI user numbers in wide circulation are not reliable enough to rank, and Exhibit 10 shows how unevenly the milestones were disclosed.

Outlook

In 2027 the question is depth, not breadth: watch routine use at work, paid conversion and whether ChatGPT's growth resumes

The breadth phase of AI adoption is largely over in the places with good data. Nine in ten organisations report using AI, about half of US workers and three in five US adults of working age use it at least occasionally, and the largest assistants have reached a billion people a week or a month on their own measures. The measures that will move in 2027 are measures of depth: how often people use AI at work, whether a larger share pay for it, and whether organisations can show results. On current evidence each of those is moving slowly. Frequent use at work stood at 30% of US employees in May 2026, ChatGPT's paid conversion near 5%, and EBIT impact was reported by 37% of organisations.

The watch list below sets out the indicators Report AI will track, with the levels that would change this report's conclusions. Two disclosures would matter most. A first official consumer user count from Anthropic, or a renewed Meta AI figure, would allow a genuine cross-platform ranking for the first time. And a repeat of OpenAI's NBER usage study for 2026 would show whether the shift to personal use has continued, reversed, or been overtaken by agent-style work use that the June 2025 data predates. Without those disclosures, the 2027 edition of this report will still be ranking reach rather than use, and the honest answer to the question of which AI is most used will remain a set of ranges.

Definitions
Weekly active users (WAU)
People who used a product at least once in the previous seven days, as defined by the company reporting it. Always smaller than the monthly count for the same product.
Monthly active users (MAU)
People who used a product at least once in the previous 30 days (or calendar month). Not comparable with weekly figures.
Deduplicated
Counting each person once even if they use several products. QuestMobile's national AI-native app total is deduplicated; its per-app figures are not, so they cannot be summed.
Device-panel estimate
A usage figure inferred by a third party (such as QuestMobile, Sensor Tower or Similarweb) from a sample of devices or browsers and extrapolated; it carries an error band and is not a company disclosure.
AI-native app
QuestMobile's category for standalone AI apps such as Doubao, Qwen and DeepSeek, as distinct from AI features inside existing apps or assistants built into phones.
Regular use (McKinsey)
An organisation counts as using AI if respondents say it regularly uses AI in at least one business function.
Frequent use (Gallup)
Employees who say they use AI in their role a few times a week or more; 'any use' means at least a few times a year.
AI Overview
The AI-generated summary Google shows above some search results. Its user count measures exposure inside Google Search, not chosen use of an assistant.
AI referral traffic
Visits to websites that arrive from links in AI assistants or AI search, measured as a share of all website traffic by panels such as SE Ranking and Similarweb.
Paid conversion
Paying subscribers divided by active users at the nearest date; a Report AI DERIVED ratio, approximate where subscriber scope differs.

Frequently asked

How many people use ChatGPT in 2026?

OpenAI said ChatGPT had more than 1 billion weekly active users on 31 August 2026, up from more than 900 million on 27 February 2026 and 800 million in October 2025. OpenAI also reported more than 50 million paying subscribers in February 2026. A figure of 1.2 billion weekly users cited at OpenAI's developer conference on 29 September 2026 may include other OpenAI products, and a figure of about 1 billion monthly app users (mid-2026) is a different metric.

Which AI assistant has the most users in 2026?

It depends on the metric, and the metrics should not be mixed. On weekly users, ChatGPT leads with more than 1 billion (OpenAI, 31 August 2026). On monthly users, Alphabet said the Gemini app passed 1 billion on 28 October 2026. Meta AI was put at about 1 billion monthly users in May 2025 but Meta has not given a 2026 total. In China, ByteDance's Doubao leads with 419 million monthly users in September 2026, according to QuestMobile.

What percentage of companies use AI in 2026?

McKinsey's 2026 State of AI survey, fielded from 4 May to 8 June 2026 among 1,719 respondents, found that 89% of organisations regularly use AI in at least one business function, against 88% in 2025 and 78% in 2024. Only 37% reported any EBIT impact from AI, so adoption is near-universal but measurable value is not.

What share of US workers use AI at work in 2026?

Gallup found that 52% of US employees used AI at work at least a few times a year in May 2026, 30% used it a few times a week or more, and 15% used it daily. The St. Louis Fed's Real-Time Population Survey found that 45% of US adults aged 18 to 64 used generative AI for work in May 2026. The two surveys use different questions and bases, so their figures differ.

What do people actually use ChatGPT for?

Mostly everyday, non-work tasks. OpenAI's NBER study of 1.5 million consumer conversations found that 73% of messages were unrelated to work in June 2025, up from 53% in June 2024. Practical guidance, seeking information and writing made up roughly three-quarters of messages; writing was 40% of work-related messages, mostly editing users' own text, and programming was only about 4.2% of all messages.

Methodology & sources

Figures are compiled from company disclosures, regulators, research firms and reputable reporting, and cross-checked against Report AI's own index pages. Each value carries its as-of date, source and a confidence rating: HIGH where the primary source was read, MEDIUM where it rests on consistent reputable secondary reporting (most figures, because many primary pages cannot be opened from our research environment), DERIVED where it is our own arithmetic, and CONTESTED where credible sources disagree. A year means the calendar year the figure describes; 2026 values are the latest available and marked “to date”. Weekly and monthly user counts, and forecasts and outcomes, are never compared as if they were the same measure. Exhibits plot only dated, sourced readings; lines are not interpolated between them, and values with different definitions are never placed on one axis. Known gaps in this edition: No primary source could be opened from this environment (mckinsey.com, frbsf.org and others failed to resolve); every value is MEDIUM or CONTESTED from consistent secondary reporting.
