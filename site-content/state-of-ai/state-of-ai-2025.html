The year in one page

In 2025 AI became near-universal inside organisations and enormous outside them. OpenAI said ChatGPT reached 800 million weekly active users in October 2025, double the 400 million it reported in February. McKinsey's 2025 survey found 88% of organisations using AI in at least one business function, up from 78%. What people used it for shifted too: OpenAI's study of 1.5 million conversations found that 73% of ChatGPT consumer messages in June 2025 were unrelated to work, up from 53% a year earlier.

Spending grew faster than any revenue line could justify on its own. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spent about $413 billion on capital expenditure in calendar 2025, roughly 69% more than in 2024, and Nvidia's data-centre revenue reached $193.7 billion for its fiscal year ended January 2026. Crunchbase counted $202.3 billion of venture funding to AI companies, close to half of all start-up investment; OpenAI's $40 billion round at a $300 billion valuation in March was the year's largest. US private AI investment reached $285.9 billion, more than 23 times China's, according to Stanford HAI.

Usage of the models, measured in tokens, rose by orders of magnitude. Google said it was processing more than 1.3 quadrillion tokens a month in October 2025, up from 480 trillion in May, and OpenAI said its API handled more than 6 billion tokens a minute. Capability kept pace: Claude Opus 4.5 became the first model reported above 80% on SWE-bench Verified in November, and Gemini Deep Think earned an officially certified gold-medal score at the International Mathematical Olympiad in July. The IEA estimates that data centres used about 485 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2025.

The costs became measurable. Challenger counted 54,836 US job cuts attributed to AI, more than four times the 2024 count. The AI Incident Database recorded 362 incidents, up 55%, and the FBI's first AI-specific tally counted 22,364 complaints and $893 million in losses. Rules started to bite: the EU AI Act's bans applied from 2 February and its general-purpose AI obligations from 2 August, US states enacted between 145 and 227 AI bills depending on the tracker, and by Report AI's count eight AI-focused US executive orders were signed.

Usage

2025 was the year consumer AI doubled in scale and its use turned personal

  1. OpenAI said ChatGPT reached 800 million weekly active users in October 2025, double the 400 million it reported in February 2025.
  2. McKinsey's 2025 State of AI survey, fielded in June and July 2025, found that 88% of organisations used AI in at least one business function, up from 78% a year earlier.
  3. OpenAI's NBER study of 1.5 million ChatGPT conversations found that 73% of consumer messages were unrelated to work in June 2025, up from 53% in June 2024.
  4. The St. Louis Fed's Real-Time Population Survey found that 54.6% of US adults aged 18 to 64 used generative AI in August 2025, up from 44.6% a year earlier.
  5. Meta said its Meta AI assistant had about 1 billion monthly active users across its apps in May 2025, double the roughly 500 million it reported in September 2024.

ChatGPT's weekly active users doubled in 2025, from 400 million in February to about 700 million in July and 800 million in October, according to OpenAI. Paying subscribers rose to about 35 million Plus and Pro users by July 2025, according to The Information. OpenAI's study of 1.5 million consumer conversations, published through NBER in September 2025, found that 73% of messages in June 2025 were unrelated to work, up from 53% a year earlier, and that practical guidance, seeking information and writing made up roughly three-quarters of use. Programming was about 4.2% of messages. The growth in 2025 came mainly from people using ChatGPT for everyday questions and for editing their own writing, not from a shift towards professional use.

Rivals scaled through products people already had. Meta said Meta AI reached about 1 billion monthly users in May 2025. Google's Gemini app had 350 million monthly users in March 2025 and 750 million by the end of the year, and AI Overviews passed 2 billion monthly users in July 2025. Microsoft reported 150 million monthly users across its Copilot apps in the quarter to September 2025. In China, DeepSeek's app launched on 11 January 2025 and reportedly gained about 100 million users in a week at the end of that month, but by December 2025 QuestMobile put it at 135 million monthly users, behind ByteDance's Doubao at 226 million. QuestMobile counted 287 million monthly users of Chinese AI-native apps in September 2025.

Organisational adoption neared its ceiling. McKinsey's survey fielded in June and July 2025 found that 88% of organisations used AI in at least one business function, but only 39% attributed any EBIT impact to it, and 23% were scaling AI agents. Individual use kept rising. The St. Louis Fed's survey found that 54.6% of US adults aged 18 to 64 used generative AI in August 2025 and 37.4% used it for work. Gallup found that 46% of US employees used AI at work at least a few times a year by the fourth quarter, with 23% using it a few times a week or more in the third. Pew found that 34% of US adults had used ChatGPT, and 58% of those under 30. OpenAI reported more than 1 million business customers in November 2025.

Spending

In 2025 most AI spending measures roughly doubled, and enterprise money shifted between model providers

  1. Gartner's September 2026 forecast puts worldwide AI spending in 2025 at about $1.79 trillion, against the $1.48 trillion Gartner projected for the same year in September 2025.
  2. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spent about $413 billion on capital expenditure in calendar 2025, roughly 69% more than in 2024, by Report AI's sum of their reported figures.
  3. IDC counted $318.7 billion of worldwide AI infrastructure spending in 2025, up 107% from 2024, according to its April 2026 tracker release.
  4. Menlo Ventures estimated in December 2025 that US enterprises spent $37 billion on generative AI in 2025, 3.2 times its restated 2024 figure.
  5. The IEA estimates that global data-centre electricity use reached about 485 TWh in 2025, with electricity for AI-focused data centres up about 50% in the year.

Big-four capex rose to about $413 billion in calendar 2025, roughly 69% more than in 2024: Amazon about $131.8 billion, Microsoft about $118.0 billion, Alphabet $91.4 billion and Meta $72.2 billion. IDC counted $318.7 billion of AI infrastructure spending, up 107%, including about $90 billion in the fourth quarter alone. Gartner's estimate of worldwide AI spending for the year rose with each edition, from a $1.48 trillion forecast in September 2025 to about $1.79 trillion in its September 2026 table, partly because it added categories. Oracle's capex for its fiscal year to May 2026 reached about $55.7 billion. Alphabet's capex rose about 74% and Meta's about 84%, by our calculation from the reported figures, and Microsoft's fiscal year to June 2025 alone came to $88.7 billion. Each company ended the year guiding to more for 2026.

Enterprises spent about $37 billion on generative AI, according to Menlo Ventures, 3.2 times its restated 2024 figure, with coding tools the largest application at about $4 billion. API spending reached about $8.4 billion annualised by mid-year, and Anthropic overtook OpenAI, taking 40% of enterprise API spending against 27%. Gartner's later figures put spending on standalone AI agents and assistants at $16.5 billion in 2025 and on all software with agentic features at $86.4 billion. Menlo also estimated a $3.5 billion vertical generative-AI segment, led by healthcare at $1.5 billion. In June 2025 Gartner forecast that over 40% of agentic AI projects would be cancelled by the end of 2027, and McKinsey's November 2025 survey found 23% of organisations scaling an agentic system.

Data-centre electricity use rose to about 485 TWh, according to the IEA, with AI-focused sites up about 50%. Governments made large announcements but smaller commitments: the EU's €200 billion InvestAI was a mobilisation headline over a >€2.7 billion AI Factories programme, the $500 billion Stargate venture was private, China registered a ¥60.1 billion AI fund, the UK set a £2 billion envelope to 2030 and Saudi Arabia launched HUMAIN. The UK's envelope included about £1 billion for sovereign compute. In November 2025 the US authorised advanced chip exports to HUMAIN and G42, each cleared to buy the equivalent of up to 35,000 Nvidia GB300 chips. Data-centre debt issuance nearly doubled to about $182 billion in 2025, according to Morgan Stanley, showing how much of the build-out was already financed by borrowing.

Finance

In 2025 AI took about half of all venture money and valuations ran ahead of revenue, as the build-out began to borrow

  1. Crunchbase counted $202.3 billion of venture funding to AI companies in 2025, close to half of the $425 billion invested globally; the OECD, using a broader definition, put AI's share at 61%, or $258.7 billion of $427.1 billion.
  2. OpenAI's $40 billion round led by SoftBank in March 2025, at a $300 billion valuation, was the largest venture round of 2025, and an employee share sale in October 2025 valued the company at $500 billion.
  3. OpenAI booked about $13.07 billion of revenue and a $20.92 billion operating loss in 2025, according to leaked audited accounts reported by the Financial Times.
  4. Nvidia reported data-centre revenue of $193.7 billion for its fiscal year ended January 2026, roughly calendar 2025, up 68% on the year before.
  5. Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Oracle sold about $108 billion of bonds in 2025, according to a Reuters analysis of LSEG data, against an average of about $28 billion a year for the five hyperscalers in 2020–2024 (Bank of America).

In 2025 AI took about half of global venture funding. Crunchbase counted $202.3 billion of the $425 billion invested, the OECD $258.7 billion of $427.1 billion (61%) on its broader definition, and PitchBook 65.4% of US deal value. OpenAI's $40 billion round at $300 billion in March, led by SoftBank, was the largest of the year. Anthropic raised $13 billion at $183 billion in September, and Microsoft and Nvidia's November investment implied about $350 billion. Mistral reached €11.7 billion. Five companies each raised more than $5 billion, together about a fifth of all venture funding. Global venture funding rose about 30% to $425 billion on Crunchbase's count, and AI accounted for most of the increase. Rounds of $100 million or more made up 77% of AI funding, $163 billion across 233 companies.

Valuations rose faster than revenue, though revenue grew quickly. OpenAI's October employee share sale valued it at $500 billion. Its revenue was about $13.07 billion, with a $20.92 billion operating loss and a net loss of about $38.5 billion, mostly a non-cash restructuring charge. Anthropic's revenue was about $4.6 billion, with an $8.06 billion operating loss. By year end the run-rates were about $20 billion and $9 billion, and Cursor passed $1 billion ARR on a $29.3 billion valuation. Nvidia's data-centre revenue reached $193.7 billion in the fiscal year to January 2026, while big-four capex rose to about $410 billion. That was about 23 times the two leading labs' combined revenue, down from about 60 times in 2024: the labs' revenue had grown faster than the build-out, but from a far smaller base.

The financing model began to change. Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Oracle sold about $108 billion of bonds, against an average of about $28 billion a year before 2025. Meta's roughly $27 billion Hyperion joint venture with Blue Owl became the largest private-credit deal on record, and Morgan Stanley put data-centre debt issuance at about $182 billion. Nvidia's September letter of intent to invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI, which would buy Nvidia systems, made circular financing the year's main bubble argument; it was still unsigned in December. Public investors stayed cautious: CoreWeave's March IPO raised $1.5 billion at $40, below its target range. In December Nvidia paid about $20 billion in a licensing deal with Groq, the year's largest AI transaction, though not a standard acquisition.

In 2025, AI-attributed US job cuts quadrupled to 54,836 and the first payroll evidence of an early-career squeeze appeared

  1. Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 54,836 US job cuts attributed to AI in 2025, 4.5% of the 1,206,374 cuts announced that year and more than four times the 2024 count.
  2. The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025, published in January 2025, forecast that 170 million jobs will be created and 92 million displaced worldwide by 2030, a net gain of 78 million driven by all structural trends, not AI alone.
  3. A November 2025 revision of Stanford Digital Economy Lab research, using ADP payroll data, found a 16% relative decline since late 2022 in employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed US occupations.
  4. PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer, published on 15 June 2026 and based on the previous year's job advertisements, put the average wage premium for jobs requiring AI skills at 62%.
  5. Indeed Hiring Lab data show that 4.44% of US job postings mentioned AI-related terms on 31 December 2025, while US software-development postings remained 32.7% below their February 2020 level.

AI became a recognised reason for US layoffs in 2025. Challenger counted 54,836 AI-attributed job cuts, more than four times the 2024 figure, though still 4.5% of the 1,206,374 cuts announced in a year when total cuts rose 58% to their highest level since 2020. Early reversals followed. Klarna, which had said in February 2024 that its AI assistant did the work of about 700 agents, acknowledged in May 2025 that cost had been 'too predominant' a factor, began rehiring human agents for complex cases, and kept the AI for routine work, which by November 2025 did the work of 853 employees. In October 2025 alone, Challenger counted 153,074 announced US cuts, with cost-cutting and AI among the reasons it highlighted.

The first payroll evidence of an AI effect on young workers appeared. Stanford Digital Economy Lab researchers, using ADP data, reported in August 2025 a 13% relative decline since late 2022 in employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations, revised to 16% in November 2025. Older workers in the same occupations held steady. US software-development postings on Indeed fell to a low of 62.5 in March 2025 and ended the year at 67.3, 32.7% below February 2020. Graduate hiring was flat: NACE's spring 2025 update projected 0.6% more hiring for the class of 2025. The Stanford authors cautioned that the pattern could partly reflect higher interest rates and post-pandemic over-hiring rather than AI alone.

Use and demand rose while the productivity evidence stayed mixed. Gallup found 46% of US employees used AI at work at least a few times a year by the fourth quarter and about 12% used it daily. The share of US Indeed postings mentioning AI reached 4.44% at year-end, and PwC's 2026 Barometer, which analyses the previous year's advertisements, put the AI-skill wage premium at 62%. In January the World Economic Forum forecast 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced worldwide by 2030. METR's July 2025 trial found experienced developers took 19% longer with early-2025 AI tools, and the St. Louis Fed's series put time saved at about 2% of US work hours by the fourth quarter.

Models & Prices

In 2025 reasoning models turned 2024's previews into products, open weights matched the frontier for a moment, and training costs reached an estimated half-billion dollars.

  1. Anthropic reported that Claude Opus 4.5, released on 24 November 2025, scored 80.9% on SWE-bench Verified, the first model reported above 80%.
  2. Google DeepMind's Gemini Deep Think scored 35 of 42 points at the July 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad, the first AI result officially certified at gold-medal standard.
  3. Epoch AI estimated that training xAI's Grok 4, released in July 2025, used about 5×10^26 FLOP and cost about $490 million, the largest known training run.
  4. DeepSeek priced its open-weight R1 reasoning model at $0.55 per million input tokens and $2.19 per million output tokens on 20 January 2025, against $15 and $60 for OpenAI's o1.
  5. Stanford HAI's 2026 AI Index reports that the median price per million tokens across AI models fell from $2.10 in 2024 to $0.85 in 2025.

The year began with DeepSeek-R1 on 20 January, an open-weight reasoning model priced at $0.55 and $2.19 per million input and output tokens, about 96% below o1. By February the top US model's Arena lead over China had fallen to 1.70%, and the closed-weight lead over open weights was also 1.70%. Humanity's Last Exam launched in January with the best models near 9%; OpenAI's Deep Research, using web tools, reached 26.6% in February. ARC-AGI-2 launched in March to replace a test that was close to solved. Releases quickened, with Gemini 2.5 Pro, o3, Llama 4, Claude 4, Grok 4 and, on 7 August, GPT-5 at $1.25 and $10.

Reasoning results moved from previews to certified results. In July Gemini Deep Think scored 35 of 42 at the International Mathematical Olympiad, the first AI result officially certified at gold-medal standard; OpenAI reported the same score for an experimental model, self-graded. In November Gemini 3 Pro and Claude Opus 4.5 arrived within a week of each other, and Opus 4.5 became the first model reported above 80% on SWE-bench Verified, at 80.9%, while cutting Anthropic's flagship price to $5 and $25. By December Gemini 3 Deep Think reported 93.8% on GPQA Diamond and 41.0% on Humanity's Last Exam without tools, and Poetiq's system reached a verified 54% on ARC-AGI-2.

Behind the releases, the cost of training climbed. Epoch AI estimated that Grok 4 used about 5×10^26 FLOP at a median cost of about $490 million, the largest known run, after Grok 3 became the first model above 10^26 FLOP in February. Stanford HAI's 2026 report found the median price per million tokens across models fell from $2.10 in 2024 to $0.85 in 2025. The US produced 50 notable models to China's 30, by the AI Index's chapter count, with a later Stanford page giving 59 and 35. Google reported that a median Gemini text prompt used 0.24 watt-hours.

Tokens

In 2025 token volumes rose by one to two orders of magnitude, and longer prompts and reasoning made the average request more expensive despite cheaper tokens

  1. Google said in October 2025 that it was processing more than 1.3 quadrillion tokens a month across its products, up from 480 trillion in May 2025.
  2. OpenAI said on 6 October 2025 that its API was processing more than 6 billion tokens a minute, up from about 300 million in 2023.
  3. DeepSeek's September 2025 list price of $0.28 per million input tokens and $0.42 per million output tokens bought about 3.2 million tokens of GPT-4-level output per dollar on a 3:1 blend, against about 27,000 for GPT-4 in March 2023 (Report AI calculation).
  4. Menlo Ventures estimated enterprise generative-AI spending at $37 billion in 2025, 3.2 times the 2024 figure; its November 2026 report restates 2025 as $33 billion.
  5. MIT's Project NANDA reported in August 2025 that 95% of the enterprise generative-AI pilots it studied showed no measurable profit-and-loss impact, while Wharton's October 2025 survey found 74% of US enterprise leaders reporting positive ROI.

In 2025 the number of tokens per task, not the price per token, drove costs. Epoch AI measured the price of a fixed capability falling by a median of about 50 times a year, and DeepSeek's September price cut put GPT-4-level output at about 3.2 million tokens per dollar. OpenAI's GPT-5 bought about 291,000 tokens per dollar at list price, and Anthropic's Opus 4.5 cut the Opus tier's price to $5 and $25 in November. But reasoning models generated long hidden chains of output, and prompts grew: on OpenRouter the average prompt rose from about 1,500 to 6,000 tokens. At flagship list prices the average request cost about $0.0115 in November 2025, more than double a year earlier. Artificial Analysis spent about $2,767 running OpenAI's o1 through a seven-benchmark suite against $109 for GPT-4o, an early sign that reasoning would raise the cost of a task.

Disclosed volumes multiplied. Google processed 480 trillion tokens a month in May and 1.3 quadrillion in October. OpenAI said in October that its API handled more than 6 billion tokens a minute, about 8.6 trillion a day. Microsoft processed more than 100 trillion tokens in the first quarter, and over 500 trillion through Foundry in its fiscal year to June. Doubao reached about 50 trillion tokens a day in December, and China's national figure reached about 100 trillion a day by year end, 21.1 quadrillion for the year. OpenRouter passed 1 trillion tokens a day, and agents grew to about 8% of its traffic by October. Only Google and ByteDance published comparable all-product figures; together they reached about 2.8 quadrillion tokens a month late in the year, roughly twenty times the equivalent 2024 figure.

Revenue rose far more slowly than volume: combined OpenAI and Anthropic run-rate revenue reached about $29 billion by year end, about 4.5 times a year earlier. Menlo Ventures estimated enterprise generative-AI spending at $37 billion, later restated to $33 billion, and model-API spending at $8.4 billion a year by mid-2025. Customers spent about $1.00 per million tokens routed through OpenRouter, and ByteDance's Volcano Engine earned roughly $0.024 per million tokens on our estimate. The evidence on returns divided sharply. MIT's NANDA study found 95% of pilots had no measurable profit-and-loss impact within about six months, and IBM found 25% of AI initiatives met expected ROI, while Wharton found 74% of leaders reporting a positive return and Snowflake reported $1.41 back per dollar. These studies asked different questions of different samples, so their results do not cancel out, but together they show that the return on all this activity was not settled.

  1. The FDA authorized 335 AI-enabled medical devices with 2025 decision dates, according to the September 2026 snapshot of its AI device list, 42.6% more than in 2024; other counts of the same year range from 295 to 333.

  4. Eurostat reported that 19.95% of EU enterprises with 10 or more employees used AI in 2025, up from 13.5% in 2024, led by the information and communication sector at 62.52%.
  5. Salesforce estimated that AI and agents influenced $262 billion of global online holiday sales between 1 November and 31 December 2025, about 20% of the total, up from $229 billion in 2024.

In 2025 headline adoption figures for large organizations stopped rising much. McKinsey found 88% of organizations using AI in at least one function, against 78% in 2024. Representative surveys showed more room: Eurostat's EU enterprise rate rose to 19.95%, with the information and communication sector at 62.52%, and Ramp's paid-adoption index ended the year at 46.6%. The Census Bureau changed its AI question in November 2025, and the measured US rate moved from about 10% on the old question to 17.3% on the new one. Within finance, Ramp's measure reached 58.1% in December 2025.

Healthcare recorded the largest changes. Risk

In 2025 the measures rose together and AI moved from assisting attacks to operating one.

  1. The AI Incident Database recorded 362 AI incidents that occurred in 2025, up 55% from 233 in 2024, according to Stanford HAI's AI Index 2026.
  2. The FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report, published in April 2026, counted 22,364 US complaints with an AI-related descriptor and $893 million in losses — the first year the report gave AI its own section.
  3. NewsGuard's audit found that leading AI chatbots repeated false news claims 35% of the time in August 2025, almost double the 18% of a year earlier.
  4. Anthropic reported on 13 November 2025 that a Chinese state-linked group used its Claude Code agent to run a cyber-espionage campaign against about 30 organisations, with the AI performing an estimated 80–90% of the tactical work.
  5. The Internet Watch Foundation found 3,443 AI-generated child sexual abuse videos in 2025, up from 13 in 2024.

2025 produced the first official floor under AI-enabled crime. The FBI's report for the year, published in April 2026, counted 22,364 complaints with an AI descriptor and $893 million in losses, about 4% of the $20.9 billion in all reported losses, which rose 26%. Vendor data pointed to better attacks rather than more: Sumsub found the identity-fraud rate fell to 2.2% while the share using AI-driven, multi-step techniques rose from 10% to 28%. Arup's $25 million remained the largest known single loss. KnowBe4's widely quoted finding that 82.6% of phishing emails showed AI involvement counted any machine assistance, not authorship.

Incidents and misinformation indicators climbed in step. The AI Incident Database recorded 362 incidents, up 55%. NewsGuard's August audit found chatbots repeating false claims 35% of the time, almost double the year before, as assistants stopped declining news questions. NewsGuard tracked 1,271 AI news sites in May, and the count was growing fast. The share of new web articles written mainly by AI, by contrast, levelled off near half, at 50.9% in the fourth quarter on Graphite's later measure. The Internet Watch Foundation found 3,443 AI-generated child abuse videos, up from 13.

Two shifts in kind mattered more than the percentages. In November Anthropic disclosed that a state-linked group had used its Claude Code agent to run an espionage campaign against about 30 organisations, with the model doing an estimated 80–90% of the tactical work. And chatbot harm moved to general-purpose assistants: OpenAI disclosed in October that about 1.2 million of its then roughly 800 million weekly users discussed suicide each week, and eight lawsuits over ChatGPT were filed between August and November, beginning with Raine v. OpenAI. Earlier, in May, a federal judge in Garcia v. Character Technologies had declined at the pleading stage to treat chatbot output as protected speech, opening the product-liability route those suits relied on.

Regulation

In 2025 the EU's first AI obligations took effect and Washington shifted to executive orders aimed at state AI laws

  1. The EU AI Act's bans on prohibited practices applied from 2 February 2025 and its obligations for general-purpose AI models from 2 August 2025, according to the European Commission's timeline.
  2. MultiState counted 1,208 AI-related bills introduced in US state legislatures in 2025 and 145 enacted, while NCSL's count for the same year is 1,262 introduced and 227 enacted.
  3. The US Senate voted 99–1 in July 2025 to strip a proposed 10-year moratorium on state AI regulation from the federal budget bill.
  4. President Trump signed Executive Order 14365 on 11 December 2025, directing the Attorney General to set up an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws.
  5. China's mandatory labelling rules for AI-generated content took effect on 1 September 2025, and 748 generative-AI services had completed CAC filing by the end of 2025, 446 of them during the year.

In 2025 the EU AI Act began to apply. Bans on prohibited practices and the AI-literacy duty took effect on 2 February 2025, and obligations for general-purpose AI models on 2 August 2025. That month 26 providers signed the GPAI Code of Practice; Meta declined. No AI Act fine followed, and Finland became the first member state with full enforcement powers only in December. On 19 November 2025 the Commission proposed the Digital Omnibus, which would move stand-alone high-risk obligations from August 2026 to December 2027. In Asia, Japan's non-punitive AI Promotion Act took full effect on 1 September 2025. At the end of 2025 the legal high-risk date was still August 2026, because the Omnibus was only a proposal. But companies and national authorities now expected a delay, and many paused compliance work while the amendment went through the institutions.

US federal policy reversed direction. EO 14110 was rescinded on 20 January 2025 and replaced by EO 14179, and by our count eight AI-focused executive orders were signed during the year, including three issued with the AI Action Plan on 23 July. Congress passed the TAKE IT DOWN Act in May, making publication of nonconsensual intimate deepfakes a federal crime. In July the Senate voted 99–1 to strip a 10-year moratorium on state AI laws from the budget bill. On 11 December EO 14365 ordered a Justice Department task force to challenge state AI laws and tied federal broadband funds to them. The order named Colorado's AI Act as an example of an onerous law. By the end of 2025 the federal government had adopted a policy of opposing state AI regulation, but there was still no federal statute to replace the state laws it opposed.

The states legislated more than in 2024. MultiState counted 1,208 AI bills introduced and 145 enacted, and NCSL later put the year at 1,262 and 227. California enacted SB 53, the first US frontier-model transparency law, and Texas enacted TRAIGA, both effective in January 2026. Colorado delayed its AI Act to June 2026. Election-deepfake laws reached 25 states by mid-year. China made labelling of AI-generated content mandatory from 1 September 2025 and ended the year with 748 filed generative-AI services, 446 of them added in 2025. In Italy, the Garante fined Replika's developer €5 million. That fine, like others that year, was imposed under data-protection law rather than any AI-specific statute, because the AI Act's penalties for general-purpose models did not apply until August 2026.

How much did big tech spend on capital expenditure in 2025?

Methodology & sources

Figures are compiled from company disclosures, regulators, research firms and reputable reporting, and cross-checked against Report AI's own index pages. Each value carries its as-of date, source and a confidence rating: HIGH where the primary source was read, MEDIUM where it rests on consistent reputable secondary reporting (most figures, because many primary pages cannot be opened from our research environment), DERIVED where it is our own arithmetic, and CONTESTED where credible sources disagree. A year means the calendar year the figure describes; 2026 values are the latest available and marked “to date”. Weekly and monthly user counts, and forecasts and outcomes, are never compared as if they were the same measure. These reports compile and verify secondary data with original analysis; they are not original surveys. See our editorial standards, corrections log and fact-check register.
