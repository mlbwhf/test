1. Price per token

A dollar buys about 188 times more GPT-4-level output than in 2023, but at OpenAI's frontier it buys only about 9% more than in 2024

There is no single price of a token, and most confusion about AI costs comes from comparing different prices. We track three. The first holds capability fixed: what is the cheapest way to buy output as good as GPT-4 was when it launched in March 2023? The second follows the frontier: what does the newest flagship model cost at list price? The third, covered in chapter 2, is the average price the market actually pays, weighted by usage. All three are expressed per million tokens and converted to tokens per dollar using a blend of three input tokens to one output token, which is close to the mix of a typical chat or coding request. The blend matters because output tokens cost four to eight times more than input tokens on most price lists.

At the fixed GPT-4 bar the gain is large and mostly in the past. GPT-4 listed at $30 per million input tokens and $60 per million output tokens in March 2023, a blended $37.50, or about 26,667 tokens per dollar. By December 2024 DeepSeek-V3, which reports a higher MMLU score than GPT-4, listed at $0.27 and $1.10: about 2.1 million tokens per dollar, 78 times more. DeepSeek's September 2025 price raised that to about 3.2 million, and by October 2026 small models from OpenAI and Anthropic at $0.10 and $0.50 reached 5 million. That is 188 times the 2023 figure, but only 2.4 times the 2024 figure. Our series uses named models with a published MMLU above GPT-4's, so it is a conservative floor; smaller models that pass the bar on other tests are cheaper still.

The frontier tells a different story. OpenAI's flagship bought about 229,000 tokens per dollar in 2024 (GPT-4o at $2.50 and $10), about 291,000 in 2025 (GPT-5 at $1.25 and $10) and about 250,000 in October 2026 (GPT-6.1 Sol at $2 and $10). Within 2026 the range was wide: GPT-5.6 Sol listed at $5 and $30 in July, only 88,889 tokens per dollar, before OpenAI halved prices in September. Anthropic's Opus tier moved from 33,333 tokens per dollar for Claude 3 Opus in 2024 to 100,000 for Opus 4.5 in late 2025 and 125,000 for Opus 5.5 in 2026, while its top Fable 5.1 tier sits at 50,000. Google went the other way, scheduling Gemini 4 Argon's price to double to $4 and $20 on 30 November 2026.

The pattern is consistent with how a16z and Epoch AI describe the market. A given capability falls in price quickly once several vendors and open-weight models can supply it: a16z put the rate at about ten times a year in 2024, Epoch measured a median of about 50 times a year in early 2025, and Epoch's 2026 work implies about 13 times a year. New frontier capability is priced for what buyers will pay and gets cheaper only when competitors catch up. So a claim that AI is getting cheaper is true at a fixed capability level and largely untrue at the frontier. These are list prices; negotiated enterprise discounts, batch rates and cached-input pricing, which Anthropic lists at $0.10 to $0.25 per million, can lower actual bills considerably.

How we count tokens per dollar

A token is a chunk of text, roughly three-quarters of an English word. Providers charge separately for input tokens (what you send) and output tokens (what the model writes), and output is usually four to eight times dearer. To turn two prices into one we use a blend of three input tokens to one output token: blended price = (3 x input + output) / 4. Tokens per dollar is one million divided by that blended price. A different blend changes the level but rarely the direction. Reasoning models add a third kind of token, hidden 'thinking' output billed at the output rate, which is why a cheap per-token price can still mean an expensive task. Tokenizers also differ: the same paragraph can be 30% more tokens on one model than another.

2. The market price

The price buyers actually paid nearly doubled to $2.07 per million tokens by late May 2026, then fell below $1 as work moved to cheaper models

List prices say what a token could cost. The usage-weighted price says what buyers paid on average, given the models they chose. The only public series is Silicon Data's LLM Token Expenditure Index, ticker SDLLMTK, launched in December 2025 and published daily as a blended dollar rate per million tokens across more than 200 models. Its launch level is reported at about $1.09. It rose to a peak of about $2.07 around 28 May 2026, fell about 20% by early July, stood at $1.16 to $1.18 in early August, reached a record low of $0.97 on 31 August or 1 September, and was below $1 again, at about $0.95 by our calculation, when Axios reported on 22 October. Exact values are hard to pin down: sources give the peak as $2.07, $2.12 or $2.05, and the timing of the break below $1 is reported inconsistently.

The fall was mostly a change in what buyers bought. A usage-weighted index declines when list prices are cut, but also when customers move work from expensive models to cheaper ones, and Silicon Data's own research lead attributed much of the decline to routing away from frontier models toward open-weight ones. Its April 2026 segment readings show why mix matters so much: $4.20 per million tokens for frontier models against $0.85 for open-weight models, a fivefold gap. List-price cuts added to the fall: OpenAI cut prices on two GPT-5.6 models in late July and halved GPT-6 Sol and Luna in September, and Anthropic's Haiku 5.5 launched in October at a tenth of Haiku 4.5's price. The rise to May is less well explained in the sources; heavier use of frontier models for coding agents is the usual explanation.

Cheaper tokens did not make tasks cheaper, because tasks used more tokens. In the a16z and OpenRouter study of 100 trillion tokens, the average prompt grew from about 1,500 tokens to more than 6,000 between November 2024 and November 2025, and the average completion from about 150 to 400. At OpenAI's flagship list prices, that average request cost about $0.0053 in November 2024 and $0.0115 a year later, more than double, while the blended token price fell 21%. Reasoning models widen the gap. In April 2025 Artificial Analysis spent about $2,767 running OpenAI's o1 through a seven-benchmark suite against $109 for GPT-4o, and in October 2026 its index cost $10.04 per task on Claude Opus 5.5 at maximum effort against $1.27 on GPT-6.1 Sol, a 7.9-fold gap between models whose list prices differ by about two times.

For a buyer, the unit that matters is the cost of a completed task, and that depends on three things that move independently: the price per token, the number of tokens the model spends, and how often the work has to be redone. Only the first is published. OpenAI's GDPval study of September 2025 is the closest thing to a task-level comparison with human labour: it found frontier models completed expert tasks about 100 times faster and 100 times cheaper than the experts, but it counted only inference time and API cost, not review, iteration or integration. We have not found a study that prices the same task at 2024 and 2026 token rates alongside a stated wage, so the return on a task cannot yet be tracked year by year.

Three prices, three questions

The fixed-capability price answers 'what does a given level of quality cost now?' It falls fastest. The flagship list price answers 'what does the best model cost?' It moves in steps and has risen at times. The usage-weighted price answers 'what did buyers pay on average?' It moves with both prices and choices, so it can fall even if no price list changes. A widely shared headline in 2026 read a falling usage-weighted index as falling demand. It is not a demand measure: OpenRouter's volume rose 37% in October while the index sat near its low. Before quoting any token price, check which of the three it is.

3. Volume

Disclosed token volumes rose by two orders of magnitude in two years, and ByteDance now reports more tokens a month than Google

Google publishes the longest cross-product series. It processed about 9.7 trillion tokens a month in spring 2024, 480 trillion in May 2025, 980 trillion in July 2025 and 1.3 quadrillion in October 2025, and said at I/O in May 2026 that it had passed 3.2 quadrillion, about seven times a year earlier. Since then it has reported only its first-party API traffic: about 7 billion tokens a minute in the third quarter of 2025, 16 billion in the first quarter of 2026, 22 billion in the second and nearly 32 billion in the third, more than four times a year earlier. At that rate the API alone handles about 1.4 quadrillion tokens a month. All of these figures are self-reported and unaudited, and Google counts tokens across products as different as Search's AI Overviews and the Gemini app.

The fastest-growing large series is Chinese. ByteDance's Volcano Engine said its Doubao models processed more than 4 trillion tokens a day in December 2024, about 50 trillion in December 2025, 120 trillion in March 2026 and more than 180 trillion in June 2026. Converted to a 30-day month, June's figure is 5.4 quadrillion tokens, more than Google's last all-product figure, although Doubao's count includes ByteDance's internal use and its own consumer app. China's National Data Administration put national use at about 100 billion tokens a day in early 2024, about 100 trillion by the end of 2025 and more than 140 trillion in March 2026, with 21.1 quadrillion tokens for 2025 as a whole. The 2024 baseline is disputed: another official account puts the start of 2025 at more than 1 trillion a day.

OpenRouter, which routes developers' requests to more than 300 models, shows where the growth comes from. Its weekly volume rose from about 10 billion tokens in January 2024, on a single-source baseline, to more than 7 trillion in December 2025 and about 194 trillion in the week of 26 October 2026, according to a chart posted by its investor Menlo Ventures. OpenRouter's own October recap reports 790 trillion tokens for the month, 25 times January, of which agents consumed 76%, against about 8% a year earlier. An agent calls a model repeatedly, feeding back its own output and tool results, so tokens per task rise with every step it takes without a person. OpenRouter's traffic is weighted toward developers and open-weight models and is not representative of the whole market.

The largest Western API businesses are missing from these series. OpenAI said its API processed more than 6 billion tokens a minute in October 2025, up from about 300 million in 2023, but gave no figure at DevDay 2026. Microsoft processed more than 100 trillion tokens in the first quarter of 2025 and over 500 trillion through Foundry in fiscal 2025, then switched to counting customers on track to use a trillion tokens each. Anthropic does not publish token volume. So no market total can be built. Our floor adds only the two firms with comparable all-product figures, Google and ByteDance: about 130 trillion tokens a month in 2024, 2.8 quadrillion in late 2025 and 8.6 quadrillion in mid-2026, a 66-fold rise that excludes most of the market.

Why token counts are not comparable

A token is not a fixed unit. Tokenizers split text differently, so the same document can produce noticeably different counts on different models. Platforms also count different things: Google sums tokens across products from Search to Gemini; ByteDance includes its own apps and internal use; OpenRouter counts paid traffic only and excludes free model variants; Microsoft has switched from token totals to counts of large customers. Images, audio and video are converted to tokens at rates each company sets. None of these figures is audited. Compare each platform with itself over time, not with another platform, and treat any cross-platform total as a floor.

4. The Jevons test

Token volume rose about 330-fold and the two leading labs' revenue about 18-fold while the price of GPT-4-level output fell by more than half: cheaper tokens enlarged the bill

In 1865 the economist William Stanley Jevons observed that more efficient steam engines increased Britain's coal consumption rather than reducing it, because cheaper power found new uses. The same question applies to tokens: when the price of a unit falls, does total revenue fall, or does demand grow faster than the price drops? We built three indexes with 2024 set to 100. Price uses the cheapest GPT-4-level blended list price from chapter 1. Volume uses Google's monthly tokens in May of each year, the only cross-product series measured in the same month each year. Revenue uses the combined annualised run-rate revenue of OpenAI and Anthropic, the two labs that hold most enterprise API spend, measured the same way in each year. All three are DERIVED, and each is a proxy for a market nobody measures in full. The question matters because it decides whether falling prices shrink or grow the token business.

The result is a strong Jevons pattern. The price index fell from 100 to 66 in 2025 and 42 in 2026. Volume rose to about 4,950 in 2025 and 32,990 in 2026, roughly 330 times its 2024 level. Revenue rose to 446 in 2025 and 1,769 in 2026: combined OpenAI and Anthropic run-rate revenue went from about $6.5 billion in December 2024 to about $29 billion at the end of 2025 and about $115 billion by the third quarter of 2026, about 18 times. Every fall in price was matched many times over by growth in tokens consumed. Revenue therefore grew faster than the GPT-4-level price fell, but far more slowly than volume. Menlo Ventures' enterprise spending estimates are not used here: its September 2026 figure of an annualised $176.8 billion uses a new definition that is not comparable with its $11.5 billion for 2024 or its $37 billion, restated to $33 billion, for 2025.

Dividing the revenue index by the volume index gives an implied revenue per token, and it fell to about 9.0 in 2025 and 5.4 in 2026: roughly 95% less money per token than in 2024. That is much steeper than the fall in the GPT-4-level list price, and the difference is the mix. Most new tokens are consumed by cheap models, by long prompts that are largely cached input, and by free or bundled consumer products whose tokens earn nothing directly. The usage-weighted price in chapter 2 shows the same mix effect within 2026. For providers this is the central tension of the token business: revenue rises with volume, but each token earns less, so margins depend on serving costs falling at least as fast as prices.

The index has clear limits. The revenue leg covers two labs only, uses unaudited run rates dated two months apart in 2026, and includes consumer subscriptions as well as token sales. Google's volume includes consumer products whose tokens no one pays for directly, so revenue and volume do not cover the same activity. Menlo's spending series cannot fill the gap: its 2024 base excludes inference, its 2025 figure was restated from $37 billion to $33 billion, and its 2026 figure is an annualised run rate on a new definition. A volume index built from Doubao, which rose from 4 trillion to 180 trillion tokens a day between December 2024 and June 2026, would show a similar order of magnitude. The direction is robust; the exact ratios are not, and should be quoted as orders of magnitude rather than as precise figures.

What the Jevons index can and cannot show

The index shows that falling token prices have not reduced providers' total revenue; demand has grown far faster than prices fell. It cannot show whether the extra tokens are worth what is paid for them, because revenue measures what buyers pay, not the value they get. It also cannot separate genuine new demand from tokens generated by design choices: longer prompts, verbose reasoning and agents that retry. Some of the growth may be waste that falling prices made affordable. If prices stopped falling, part of that volume might disappear. Treat the index as evidence about the market's size and direction, not about returns.

5. Return per token

Providers earn roughly $0.70 to $1.00 per million tokens routed, and fewer than a third of buyers report strong returns

Return per token has no standard definition, and nobody publishes it. We built the measures the data allows and labelled each DERIVED. On the provider side, revenue per million tokens divides what customers pay by the tokens they consume. OpenRouter is the one platform where both can be estimated: Sacra put its gross spend at about $100 million a year in May 2025, when a16z said it handled more than 100 trillion tokens a year, which gives about $1.00 per million tokens. In August 2026 Sacra estimated its revenue at $160 million a year on a take rate of about 5%, implying about $3.2 billion of spend, while Menlo put annual volume at 4.5 quadrillion tokens, which gives about $0.71. The 2026 figure depends on the take rate: 4% gives $0.89, 6% gives $0.59.

Other providers allow only rough estimates. ByteDance's Volcano Engine is reported to have earned about RMB 1.5 billion from model services in 2025. Its daily token count rose from about 4 trillion to 50 trillion during the year, about 8.6 quadrillion tokens in total on a linear path between readings, which works out to about RMB 0.17, or roughly $0.024, per million tokens. That figure understates external revenue per token because Doubao's count includes ByteDance's own apps. The US labs disclose revenue but not tokens. Anthropic's S-1 reported $16.3 billion of revenue in the first half of 2026, about 80% of it from consumption-based billing per one reading of the filing, but no token volume. Silicon Data's index, at about $0.95 in October 2026, is in effect the market's average revenue per million tokens across the models it tracks.

On the buyer side, the evidence depends on how the question is asked. Wharton and GBK Collective's annual survey of US enterprise leaders found about 65% reporting a positive return in 2024, 74% in 2025 and 80% in October 2026, but only 28% describe the return as strongly positive. McKinsey's 2026 survey found 37% of organisations reporting any EBIT contribution from AI, essentially unchanged from 2025, with about 6% attributing more than 5% of EBIT to it. MIT's NANDA study reported in August 2025 that 95% of the generative-AI pilots it studied showed no measurable profit-and-loss impact within about six months, and IBM found that 25% of AI initiatives had met their expected ROI. Snowflake's surveys report average returns of $1.41 per dollar in 2025 and $1.49 in 2026.

These studies are CONTESTED rather than contradictory: they ask different people different questions over different periods. Their common finding is that a majority of firms report some return, a minority report a large one, and the share reporting a large return has not grown as fast as spending. None of them divides return by tokens consumed, and no company discloses firm-level return per token, so the buyer's return per token cannot be measured with public data. The cost side is now visible enough to matter: about one in five organisations told McKinsey in 2026 that AI operating costs were constraining their use. That is the first sign that the token bill, rather than access to the models, has started to limit adoption.

How we built the ROI-per-token measures

Revenue per million tokens = customer spend through a platform / tokens consumed, in millions. For OpenRouter, spend = reported revenue / take rate (about 5%). Implied revenue per token index = (OpenAI + Anthropic run-rate revenue index / Google volume index) x 100, with 2024 = 100. Request cost = prompt tokens x input price + completion tokens x output price, at the flagship list price. Each measure uses public inputs, so each inherits their weaknesses: estimated take rates, unaudited token counts and spend figures with changing scope. We did not build a buyer-side return per token, because no published source gives both a firm's measured return and its token consumption. That figure does not exist in public data.

Comparison

From 2024 to 2026 the cost of fixed capability fell, token volume rose by two orders of magnitude and lab revenue grew in between, with growth rates differing tenfold across platforms

Taken year by year, the three lenses move at very different speeds. Tokens per dollar at the GPT-4 level rose from about 2.1 million in 2024 to 3.2 million in 2025 and 5 million in 2026, a gain of about 2.4 times; most of the improvement since 2023 happened before 2024. At OpenAI's flagship, tokens per dollar were 229,000, 291,000 and 250,000, effectively flat. Volume moved fastest: Google's monthly tokens grew about 49 times from May 2024 to May 2025 and about 7 times in the following year, and Doubao's daily tokens grew about 12 times in 2025 and more than 3 times in the first half of 2026. Combined OpenAI and Anthropic run-rate revenue grew about 4.5 times in 2025 and about 4 times again by the third quarter of 2026; Menlo's enterprise spend estimates changed definition in 2026 and give no comparable multiple.

Across platforms, growth rates differ by an order of magnitude, partly because of different starting points. OpenRouter grew fastest, from tens of billions of tokens a week to nearly 200 trillion, because it began small and caught the move to agents. Google's growth slowed in relative terms as its base grew, from about 49 times in its first year to about 7 times in its second, while its API traffic still quadrupled year on year. Chinese volumes grew at least as fast as American ones on every series we found, and ByteDance's single disclosed figure now exceeds Google's. On revenue, the two leading US labs' combined run rate grew about 18 times in under two years, against about 330 times for Google's token volume, so revenue per token fell by about 95% even as total revenue soared.

Outlook

In 2027 the test is whether spending keeps pace with token volume as agents multiply the tokens behind every task

The data to date supports a simple account. Capability gets cheap once several suppliers can provide it, frontier capability stays expensive, and demand for tokens grows much faster than prices fall. If that continues, provider revenue keeps rising while revenue per token keeps falling, and the margin question moves to serving costs: chips, power and how efficiently models use tokens. Agents strengthen both effects. They raise the volume of tokens per task and shift work to cheaper models wherever a cheap model is good enough, which is what the usage-weighted price recorded in 2026.

Three things could change the picture. A sustained rise in frontier prices, of which Google's scheduled Gemini 4 Argon increase is the first test, would show pricing power returning to the top of the market. A slowdown in volume growth below the rate of price decline would turn the Jevons pattern into falling total revenue. And evidence that buyers' strong returns are not broadening, with the share stuck below a third while costs constrain one firm in five, would point to a correction in token demand driven by cost rather than capability. The indicators below are the ones we will track.

Definitions
Token
The unit AI models read and write, roughly three-quarters of an English word on average. Models are priced and usage is measured in tokens, usually per million.
Input and output tokens
Input tokens are what is sent to the model (prompt, documents, conversation history); output tokens are what it generates. Output is typically priced four to eight times higher. Cached input is re-used input billed at a steep discount.
Blended price (3:1)
One price per million tokens combining input and output prices at three input tokens per output token: (3 x input + output) / 4. Report AI uses it for every tokens-per-dollar figure.
Tokens per dollar
1,000,000 divided by the blended price per million tokens: how many tokens $1 buys at list price.
Fixed-capability price
The cheapest price at which a stated level of performance (here, GPT-4 as of March 2023) can be bought. It tracks how fast a given capability gets cheaper.
Usage-weighted price
The average price actually paid per million tokens, weighted by how much each model is used. Silicon Data's LLM Token Expenditure Index (SDLLMTK) is the only public series; it falls when prices are cut or when buyers switch to cheaper models.
Reasoning tokens
Hidden intermediate output that reasoning models generate before answering, billed as output. They can multiply the tokens, and therefore the cost, of a task.
Run rate
Revenue annualised from a recent period, usually the latest month x 12. Not audited and not the same as calendar-year revenue.
Take rate and GMV
For a marketplace such as OpenRouter, GMV is the total customers spend through it and the take rate is the share it keeps. Revenue / take rate = GMV.
Jevons paradox
The observation, first made about coal in 1865, that making a resource cheaper or more efficient to use can increase total consumption of it. Applied here to tokens.

Frequently asked

How many tokens does $1 buy in 2026?

It depends on the model. At GPT-4 (March 2023) quality, OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna and Anthropic's Claude Haiku 5.5 list at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens as of October 2026, about 5 million tokens per dollar on a 3:1 input:output blend. OpenAI's flagship GPT-6.1 Sol, at $2 and $10, buys about 250,000, and Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5, at $4 and $20, about 125,000 (Report AI calculations from list prices).

Are AI token prices still falling in 2026?

The average price paid is falling; frontier list prices mostly are not. Silicon Data's usage-weighted LLM Token Expenditure Index fell from about $2.07 per million tokens in late May 2026 to below $1 by 22 October 2026, according to Axios, largely because buyers switched to cheaper models. OpenAI cut GPT-6 Sol by 50% in September 2026, while Google scheduled Gemini 4 Argon's price to double to $4 and $20 on 30 November 2026.

How many tokens do AI platforms process?

Each platform uses its own unit. Google reported more than 3.2 quadrillion tokens a month across its products in May 2026, and its model APIs about 32 billion tokens a minute in Q3 2026. ByteDance reported more than 180 trillion tokens a day for its Doubao models in June 2026. OpenRouter routed 790 trillion tokens in October 2026. China's National Data Administration put national use at more than 140 trillion tokens a day in March 2026. OpenAI, Microsoft and Anthropic have not published 2026 totals.

If tokens are cheaper, why are AI bills rising?

Each task now uses many more tokens. On OpenRouter the average prompt grew from about 1,500 to 6,000 tokens between November 2024 and November 2025 (a16z / OpenRouter), and reasoning models and agents generate far more output. At OpenAI's flagship list prices, Report AI calculates that the average request cost about $0.0053 in November 2024 and $0.0115 in November 2025, more than double, even though the blended token price fell 21%.

What is the return per AI token?

No company or study publishes it. Report AI's proxies: customers spent about $0.71 per million tokens routed through OpenRouter in August 2026, down from about $1.00 in mid-2025 (derived from Sacra and Menlo Ventures figures), and revenue per token is about 95% lower than in 2024 on our index (combined OpenAI and Anthropic run-rate revenue up about 18 times against Google's token volume up about 330 times). On the buyer side, Wharton's October 2026 survey found 80% of enterprise leaders reporting positive AI returns but only 28% strongly positive, and McKinsey's 2026 survey found 37% reporting any EBIT contribution.

Methodology & sources

Figures are compiled from company disclosures, regulators, research firms and reputable reporting, and cross-checked against Report AI's own index pages. Each value carries its as-of date, source and a confidence rating: HIGH where the primary source was read, MEDIUM where it rests on consistent reputable secondary reporting (most figures, because many primary pages cannot be opened from our research environment), DERIVED where it is our own arithmetic, and CONTESTED where credible sources disagree. A year means the calendar year the figure describes; 2026 values are the latest available and marked “to date”. Weekly and monthly user counts, and forecasts and outcomes, are never compared as if they were the same measure. Exhibits plot only dated, sourced readings; lines are not interpolated between them, and values with different definitions are never placed on one axis.
