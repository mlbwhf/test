The year in one page

2024 was the year generative AI stopped being a novelty and became infrastructure. OpenAI said ChatGPT had 300 million weekly active users in December 2024, up from 200 million in August. McKinsey's 2024 State of AI survey found that 78% of organisations used AI in at least one business function, up from 55% a year earlier, and the American Medical Association found that 66% of US physicians used AI in their practice in November 2024, up from 38% in 2023. Adoption spread faster than any measure of return: the year produced many pilots and few audited results.

The money moved first into hardware. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spent about $245 billion on capital expenditure in calendar 2024, by Report AI's sum of their reported figures, and Nvidia's data-centre revenue reached $115.2 billion for its fiscal year ended January 2025, up 142%. Crunchbase counted more than $100 billion of venture funding to AI companies, about a third of all start-up investment, and Stanford HAI's AI Index put US private AI investment at $109.1 billion, nearly 12 times China's $9.3 billion.

Prices fell faster than almost anyone expected. Stanford HAI found that the price of GPT-3.5-level inference dropped from $20 per million tokens in November 2022 to $0.07 in October 2024, and Google said it was processing about 9.7 trillion tokens a month in spring 2024, a baseline that would grow more than 300-fold in two years. At the frontier, OpenAI's o3 preview scored 75.7% on the ARC-AGI-1 semi-private evaluation in December 2024, the first strong result on a test designed to resist memorisation.

The costs and the rules arrived together. The EU AI Act entered into force on 1 August 2024, with obligations phased in from 2025; US state legislatures introduced 635 AI bills and enacted 99, according to MultiState. The AI Incident Database recorded 233 incidents, and a deepfake video call cost the engineering firm Arup about $25 million. Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 12,742 US job cuts attributed to AI, 1.7% of all announced cuts: real, but small next to what followed.

Usage

2024 was the year AI adoption spread across organisations, while individual use remained a minority habit

  1. OpenAI said ChatGPT had 300 million weekly active users in December 2024, up from 200 million in August 2024.
  2. McKinsey's 2024 State of AI survey found that 78% of organisations used AI in at least one business function, up from 55% in 2023.
  3. McKinsey found that 71% of organisations regularly used generative AI in at least one business function in 2024, more than double the 33% it recorded in 2023.
  4. The St. Louis Fed's Real-Time Population Survey found that 44.6% of US adults aged 18 to 64 had used generative AI in August 2024, and 33.3% had used it for work.
  5. Google said AI Overviews in Search reached more than 1 billion monthly users in October 2024, five months after their US launch.

In 2024 ChatGPT became a mass-market product. OpenAI reported 200 million weekly active users in August 2024 and 300 million in December 2024, and its paying base reached about 15.5 million subscribers by the end of the year, according to figures OpenAI gave shareholders as reported by The Information. Other large technology companies built AI into products people already used rather than launching standalone assistants. Google launched AI Overviews in US search results in May 2024 and said they reached more than 1 billion monthly users by October 2024. Meta said Meta AI, built into WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook, had about 500 million monthly users in September 2024. Those were monthly counts of reach and were not comparable with ChatGPT's weekly count, which measured people who chose to come back within seven days.

Inside organisations, 2024 was the inflection year. McKinsey's survey fielded in July 2024 found that 78% of organisations used AI in at least one business function, up from 55% in 2023, and that 71% regularly used generative AI, up from 33% a year earlier. Microsoft and LinkedIn's 2024 Work Trend Index found that 75% of knowledge workers in 31 countries used generative AI at work, and that 78% of those users brought their own tools. The organisational measures were broad: a single function using AI was enough for an organisation to count as an adopter. They showed that organisations had started using AI almost everywhere. They did not measure how much work AI was doing inside them, which was the question that came to dominate the following year.

Among individuals, AI use was rising fast but was still a minority habit in 2024. The St. Louis Fed's Real-Time Population Survey found that 44.6% of US adults aged 18 to 64 had used generative AI in August 2024, a figure first published as 39.4% and later restated after a change in question order, and that 33.3% had used it for work. Gallup found that 27% of US employees used AI at work at least a few times a year in the second quarter of 2024, and about 12% used it a few times a week or more. Pew found that 23% of US adults had ever used ChatGPT in early 2024, and 43% of adults under 30. OpenAI's later study found that 53% of ChatGPT consumer messages in June 2024 were unrelated to work.

Spending

In 2024 AI spending became an infrastructure story, and big-tech capex rose by about 60%

  1. Gartner estimated worldwide AI spending at about $988 billion in 2024, in the 2024 baseline of its September 2025 forecast, before it widened its AI categories in 2026.
  2. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spent about $245 billion on capital expenditure in calendar 2024, by Report AI's sum of each company's reported figures.
  3. IDC counted $153.9 billion of worldwide spending on AI infrastructure hardware in 2024, according to its AI infrastructure tracker.
  4. The International Energy Agency estimates that data centres worldwide used about 415 TWh of electricity in 2024, roughly 1.5% of global electricity demand.
  5. Menlo Ventures reported in November 2024 that US enterprises spent $13.8 billion on generative AI that year, a figure it restated to $11.5 billion in December 2025 after excluding inference.

Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spent about $245 billion on capital expenditure in calendar 2024, by Report AI's sum of their reported figures, up from about $151 billion in 2023. Amazon spent about $77.7 billion on a net cash basis, Microsoft about $75.6 billion including finance leases, Alphabet $52.5 billion and Meta about $39.2 billion. IDC counted $153.9 billion of worldwide spending on AI-optimised servers and storage. Gartner later estimated total worldwide AI spending for the year at about $988 billion, on a broader definition that includes AI software, services and AI-enabled devices. The gap between the IDC and Gartner figures reflected what each counted, not a disagreement about the market. Sequoia's essay 'AI's $600B Question' (June 2024) framed the gap between this capex and AI revenue, and became the reference point for the debate that followed.

Enterprise budgets for generative AI were still small. Menlo Ventures reported in November 2024 that US enterprises spent $13.8 billion on generative AI in 2024, about six times its 2023 figure; in December 2025 it restated 2024 to $11.5 billion after excluding inference. Model API spending ran at about $3.5 billion a year by November. OpenAI held 34% of enterprise API spending and Anthropic 24%. Banking was the largest industry on IDC's all-AI measure, at about $31.3 billion. Applications accounted for $4.6 billion of Menlo's original total and large language models for $6.5 billion, so most enterprise generative-AI money still went to models. Banking, software and information services and retail together accounted for about $89.6 billion of AI spending on IDC's measure, about 38% of the total.

The physical cost became visible. The IEA estimates that data centres used about 415 TWh of electricity in 2024, about 1.5% of global demand. Public money was modest in comparison: the US requested about $3.1 billion of non-defence federal AI research for fiscal 2024, and China registered a ¥344 billion semiconductor fund in May. Most government commitments that year were one-off funds rather than recurring budgets. Electricity for AI-accelerated servers was growing about 30% a year against about 9% for conventional servers, the IEA estimated. Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory projected that US data-centre electricity could reach 325–580 TWh by 2028, from 176 TWh in 2023. India approved its IndiaAI Mission, about $1.25 billion, in March 2024, and the $52.7 billion US CHIPS and Science Act continued to underwrite the chip supply chain.

Finance

In 2024 AI became the largest destination for venture money while the labs' revenue stayed in single-digit billions

  1. Crunchbase counted more than $100 billion of venture funding to AI companies in 2024, about a third of the roughly $314 billion invested in startups worldwide that year.
  2. OpenAI raised $6.6 billion at a $157 billion valuation in October 2024, and Databricks' $10 billion round in December 2024 was the largest venture raise of the year, according to Crunchbase.
  3. Nvidia reported data-centre revenue of $115.2 billion for its fiscal year ended January 2025, roughly calendar 2024, up 142% on the year before.
  4. OpenAI generated about $3.7 billion of revenue in 2024 and ended the year at an annualised run-rate of about $5.5 billion, according to figures the company confirmed in June 2025.
  5. Anthropic's S-1, as reported by Reuters in 2026, shows revenue of roughly $400 million and an operating loss of $2.98 billion for 2024.

In 2024 AI became the main destination for venture capital. Crunchbase counted more than $100 billion of funding to AI companies, up more than 80% on 2023 and about a third of the roughly $314 billion invested in startups worldwide; PitchBook put AI at 46.4% of US deal value. The largest rounds went to a few companies: Databricks raised $10 billion at $62 billion in December, OpenAI $6.6 billion at $157 billion in October, and xAI $6 billion twice, ending the year valued at $45–50 billion. Anthropic, priced at about $18 billion early in the year, took a further $4 billion from Amazon without a disclosed valuation. Global venture funding as a whole was nearly flat, at about $314 billion against $304 billion in 2023, so AI's gain came largely at the expense of other sectors. Stanford HAI counted corporate AI investment of $252.3 billion, a broader measure.

The labs' revenue was small next to the money committed to them. OpenAI earned about $3.7 billion, reportedly lost about $5 billion, and ended the year at an annualised run-rate of about $5.5 billion. Anthropic's later S-1 shows revenue of roughly $400 million and a $2.98 billion operating loss. Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta spent about $246 billion on capital expenditure, about 60 times the two labs' combined revenue by Report AI's calculation. In June, Sequoia's David Cahn set out 'AI's $600B question': the annual revenue the industry would need to justify its infrastructure spending. OpenAI's run-rate had roughly doubled during the year, and Anthropic's reached about $1 billion around its end. Neither company published audited accounts, so the 2024 figures rest on reported projections and on the S-1 Anthropic filed two years later.

The supplier was the one company whose AI income could be measured. Nvidia's data-centre revenue reached $115.2 billion in the fiscal year to January 2025, up 142%. Exits were scarce. No AI company of scale went public, and consolidation ran through acquisitions and licensing-and-hiring deals; Mergermarket counted $49.9 billion of M&A involving AI startups across 454 deals, both records. The financing model of the following two years was already in place: private rounds for the labs, cash flow for the hyperscalers, and almost no public-market pricing of AI companies. Nvidia's sales were the main channel through which AI money reached public markets, since the labs themselves were private. Report AI found no large AI IPO in 2024 and could not establish the year's largest AI acquisition, a gap recorded in the data file.

Work

In 2024, AI was a marginal stated reason for US layoffs, while demand for AI skills in job postings and the pay premium for them rose

  1. Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 12,742 US job cuts in 2024 that employers attributed to artificial intelligence, about 1.7% of the 761,358 cuts announced that year.
  2. Indeed Hiring Lab data show that 2.71% of US job postings on Indeed mentioned AI-related terms on 31 December 2024, up from 1.94% a year earlier.
  3. Indeed's US software-development job postings index stood 32.5% below its 1 February 2020 level on 31 December 2024, while total US postings were 10.6% above it.
  4. PwC's 2025 Global AI Jobs Barometer found that job advertisements requiring AI skills offered a 56% wage premium over comparable roles in 2024, a figure PwC later restated as 57%.
  5. Pew Research Center found in October 2024 that 16% of US workers said at least some of their work was done with AI.

AI played a small part in US layoffs in 2024. Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 12,742 US job cuts that employers attributed to AI, about 1.7% of the 761,358 cuts announced that year by Report AI's division. Challenger's analysts noted that some AI-related cuts were filed under other headings, so the figure undercounted explicit AI involvement even as it overstated proven causation. The larger change in 2024 was a cooling technology market: Indeed's index of US software-development postings ended the year at 67.5, 32.5% below its February 2020 level, while total US postings were 10.6% above theirs. Over 2023 and 2024 combined, employers attributed about 17,000 US job cuts to AI, by Report AI's subtraction from Challenger's cumulative total.

Demand for AI skills rose through the year. The share of US job postings on Indeed mentioning AI-related terms climbed from 1.94% at the end of 2023 to 2.71% at the end of 2024, and Lightcast data in Stanford's AI Index put the share of US postings requiring AI skills at 1.8% for 2024. PwC's 2025 Global AI Jobs Barometer, which analysed 2024 job advertisements across many countries, found an average advertised wage premium of 56% for jobs requiring AI skills, more than double the 25% found in 2023 advertisements; PwC later restated the 2024 figure as 57%. SignalFire reported that new graduates made up about 7% of hires at large tech firms in 2024, roughly half the pre-pandemic share.

Use of AI at work was still uncommon. Pew Research Center found in October 2024 that 16% of US workers said at least some of their work was done with AI, and Gallup's figures imply that about 4% of US employees used AI daily in the second quarter. Exposure estimates ran far ahead of use: the IMF estimated in January 2024 that about 40% of global employment was exposed to AI, and about 60% in advanced economies. The St. Louis Fed's November 2024 survey put time saved by generative AI at about 1.4% of all US work hours, a measurable but small effect. The gap between what AI could do and what workers actually used it for was the defining feature of the year, and the main reason forecasts of displacement ran ahead of measured effects.

Tokens

In 2024 the price of established capability collapsed while frontier prices stayed high and volumes were still measured in trillions

  1. Google processed about 9.7 trillion tokens a month across its products in spring 2024, the baseline Sundar Pichai cited at Google I/O in May 2025.
  2. Stanford HAI's AI Index 2025 found that the price of GPT-3.5-level inference fell from $20 per million tokens in November 2022 to $0.07 in October 2024, a decline of more than 280 times.
  3. OpenAI's flagship GPT-4o listed at $2.50 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens from August 2024, about 229,000 tokens per dollar on a 3:1 blend (Report AI calculation).
  4. ByteDance's Volcano Engine said its Doubao models were processing more than 4 trillion tokens a day in December 2024, 33 times the level at their May 2024 launch.
  5. Menlo Ventures estimated that US enterprises spent $11.5 billion on generative AI in 2024, with spending on model APIs running at about $3.5 billion a year in November 2024.

2024 was the year a fixed level of AI capability became cheap. Stanford HAI later recorded that GPT-3.5-level inference had fallen to $0.07 per million tokens by October 2024, from $20 in November 2022, and a16z estimated that the cost of a given capability was falling about ten times a year. At the GPT-4 level, DeepSeek-V3 launched in December at $0.27 per million input tokens and $1.10 per million output tokens, about 2.1 million tokens per dollar on a 3:1 blend, roughly 78 times what GPT-4 itself had bought at its March 2023 launch. The frontier moved less. OpenAI's GPT-4o, its flagship from August, bought about 229,000 tokens per dollar, and Anthropic's Claude 3 Opus about 33,000. The gap between the two curves is the main fact of 2024: the steep decline applied to capability that several vendors could already supply, not to the newest models.

Volumes were small by later standards, and few companies published them. Google processed about 9.7 trillion tokens a month in the spring, a figure it disclosed only a year later. ByteDance said its Doubao models reached more than 4 trillion tokens a day in December, 33 times their level at launch in May. Microsoft's company-wide volume was about 20 trillion tokens a quarter early in the year, which we infer from a later statement that it grew fivefold. OpenRouter handled on the order of 10 billion tokens a week in January. China's National Data Administration put national use at about 100 billion tokens a day in early 2024, a baseline another official source disputes. None of these figures is audited, and each company counts differently, so they show the scale and direction of use rather than a market total, which we do not publish for 2024.

Spending was still modest. Menlo Ventures estimated that US enterprises spent $11.5 billion on generative AI in 2024, with spending on model APIs running at about $3.5 billion a year by November. OpenAI and Anthropic had combined run-rate revenue of roughly $6.5 billion in December. Evidence on returns was limited: Wharton's survey implies that about 65% of enterprise leaders reported a positive return, but no study linked return to token use. Reasoning models arrived only in September, with OpenAI's o1, so their effect on the number of tokens per task does not appear in the 2024 data. For most buyers in 2024 the question was still whether to use the models at all, not what each token earned, and no survey we found asked about token spending directly.

Infrastructure & Energy

In 2024 AI infrastructure was supply-constrained by chips and packaging, and power became a planning problem rather than an operating one

  1. Nvidia reported data-centre revenue of $115.2 billion for its fiscal year ended January 2025, which approximates calendar 2024, up 142% on the prior year.
  2. The International Energy Agency estimates that data centres worldwide used about 415 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2024, roughly 1.5% of global electricity consumption.
  3. Microsoft agreed on 20 September 2024 to buy the full output of Constellation's 835-megawatt Three Mile Island Unit 1 reactor for 20 years, the largest tech-company nuclear deal announced that year.
  4. CBRE reported that vacancy across North America's primary data-centre markets fell to a record-low 2.8% in the first half of 2024, with a record 3,872 megawatts under construction.
  5. By October 2024 Nvidia H100 GPUs were being rented on resale markets for about $2 per GPU-hour, down from around $8 during the 2023 shortage, according to an analysis published by Latent Space.

Nvidia's data-centre revenue reached $115.2 billion in the fiscal year that ended in January 2025, up 142%, and its total revenue $130.5 billion. AMD's Data Center segment grew 94% to $12.6 billion, with Instinct accelerators above $5 billion, and Broadcom's AI revenue rose 220% to $12.2 billion. TrendForce estimates AI-server shipments grew 46%. The limiting factor was not demand but packaging: TSMC ended the year with roughly 35,000 wafers a month of CoWoS capacity. In the rental market, the 2023 shortage unwound. Prices for H100s that had reached about $8 an hour fell to about $2 on resale markets by October, as early reserved contracts were resold and fewer new model developers were training from scratch. The year's growth was concentrated in a single supplier and a few cloud buyers.

Electricity use was still modest in absolute terms. The IEA estimates that data centres worldwide used about 415 terawatt-hours in 2024, roughly 1.5% of global electricity, and EPRI later put US data centres at 177–192 terawatt-hours, or 4–5% of national use. What changed was the outlook. In December the Department of Energy published Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory's estimate of 176 terawatt-hours for 2023 alongside scenarios of 325–580 terawatt-hours by 2028, and the widely quoted per-query figure, about 2.9 watt-hours for a ChatGPT request, framed the public debate. That figure was a top-down estimate for an earlier model generation and was later shown to be about ten times higher than measurements of typical prompts.

Hyperscalers began contracting firm power directly. In September Microsoft agreed to buy the full output of the 835-megawatt Three Mile Island Unit 1 reactor for 20 years, a restart of a shut reactor contracted to a single buyer, and in October Google agreed to buy power from up to 500 megawatts of Kairos small reactors. Grid markets registered the coming load: PJM's July auction cleared at $269.92 per megawatt-day for 2025/26, with two data-centre-heavy zones above $440. In North America's primary markets, CBRE reported record-low vacancy of 2.8% and a record 3,872 megawatts under construction in the first half, nearly 80% of it preleased.

Menlo Ventures estimated that only about 3% of US healthcare organizations had implemented domain-specific AI tools, and healthcare was the largest vertical buyer of generative-AI software at about $500 million. Stack Overflow found 62% of developers using AI tools, with 43% trusting their accuracy.

Spending was concentrated in a few industries. IDC estimated banking AI spending at about $31.3 billion and retail at about $25 billion, with software and information services completing a top three worth about $89.6 billion. Gartner put worldwide AI spending at $987.9 billion. Consumer behaviour began to shift in retail: Adobe recorded a 1,300% rise in generative-AI referral traffic to US retail sites in the holiday season, from a small base, and Salesforce estimated that AI influenced $229 billion of global online holiday sales. Measured outcomes were few, and the most cited one came from legal research, where Stanford researchers found leading tools hallucinating on 17-33% of queries.

Risk

In 2024 AI misuse acquired its signature cases, but almost none of it was yet measured.

  1. Hong Kong police said in February 2024 that a finance employee at engineering firm Arup transferred about US$25 million (HK$200 million) after a video call in which every other participant was an AI deepfake, the largest publicly reported deepfake fraud until September 2026.
  2. The AI Incident Database recorded 233 AI incidents that occurred in 2024, according to Stanford HAI's AI Index 2026.
  3. NewsGuard's audit found that leading AI chatbots repeated false news claims 18% of the time in August 2024.
  4. NewsGuard was tracking 1,121 AI-generated news and information sites operating with little or no human oversight on 11 November 2024, up from 49 in May 2023.
  5. The World Economic Forum's Global Risks Report 2024 ranked mis- and disinformation as the most severe global risk over a two-year horizon, the year an AI-cloned Biden robocall told New Hampshire voters to stay home and later drew a $6 million FCC fine.

2024 was the year synthetic impersonation became a business risk. In February Hong Kong police described how a finance employee at Arup had authorised about $25 million in transfers after a video call in which every other participant was a deepfake. The case set the template for corporate defences and the frame for public debate: a convincing fake face. Identity-verification vendors reported steep growth in detected deepfakes, with Entrust logging an attempt every five minutes. The measurement layer was thin. The FBI recorded $16.6 billion in reported internet-crime losses but had no way to separate AI-enabled cases, and most deepfake statistics came from vendors counting what their own tools detected.

Elections supplied the year's political test. With more than two billion people eligible to vote, the World Economic Forum ranked mis- and disinformation the most severe short-term global risk. In January an AI-cloned Biden robocall urged New Hampshire voters to skip the primary; the FCC ruled AI voices in robocalls illegal without consent and fined the consultant $6 million. No election result was credibly attributed to a deepfake. The more durable change was infrastructural: NewsGuard counted 1,121 AI-generated news sites by November, and in August leading chatbots repeated false news claims 18% of the time.

The gravest harms were the least visible in aggregate data. Nudify sites drew an estimated 200 million visits in the first half of the year, and the Internet Watch Foundation found its first AI-generated child abuse videos, 13 in all. The AI Incident Database recorded 233 incidents, up from about 149 in 2023. In October a Florida mother sued Character.AI and Google after her 14-year-old son's suicide, the first widely reported chatbot-harm suit. No AI agent was documented carrying out an intrusion; published threat reports described language models assisting attackers with research and scripting, not operating attacks.

Regulation

In 2024 the EU adopted its AI Act and US states began legislating, but no obligation of the EU AI Act yet applied

  1. The EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) entered into force on 1 August 2024, with fines of up to €35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover for prohibited practices under Article 99.
  2. MultiState counted 635 AI-related bills introduced in US state legislatures in 2024, of which 99 were enacted.
  3. Stanford HAI's AI Index 2025 counted 131 AI-related state laws passed in the United States in 2024, up from 49 in 2023, and 59 AI-related federal agency regulations, up from 25.
  4. Stanford HAI's AI Index 2025 found that mentions of AI in the legislative proceedings of 75 countries rose 21.3% in 2024, to 1,889 from 1,557 in 2023.
  5. China ended 2024 with 302 generative-AI services filed with the Cyberspace Administration of China, according to CAC filing data reported by legal trackers.

The main regulatory event of 2024 was the adoption of a law that did not yet apply. The EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, entered into force on 1 August 2024. It fixed penalties of up to €35 million or 7% of worldwide turnover for prohibited practices and set out a staged timetable. Its first obligations, the bans on unacceptable-risk practices, were not due until February 2025, and high-risk systems were scheduled for August 2026. At the end of 2024 the EU was the only one of the nine jurisdictions we map with a comprehensive AI statute in force, and nothing in it could yet be enforced. The 2024 schedule is also the baseline for later delays: it set 2 August 2026 for stand-alone high-risk systems and 2 August 2027 for those embedded in regulated products. Companies spent the year preparing for those dates, which later moved.

In the United States, AI regulation in 2024 came from the states and from agencies. MultiState counted 635 AI-related bills introduced in state legislatures and 99 enacted, and Stanford HAI's AI Index counted 131 state AI laws passed, up from 49 in 2023. Colorado enacted the first broad state AI Act in May, originally due to take effect in February 2026. California's governor vetoed the frontier-model bill SB 1047 in September. Federal agencies issued 59 AI-related regulations, up from 25, under the Biden-era EO 14110. No AI-focused executive order was signed that year. Twenty-one states had election-deepfake laws going into 2025, up from five before the year began, as legislators reacted to the robocall and to the many national elections held worldwide in 2024. Most of those laws required disclosure labels on political deepfakes rather than banning them.

Elsewhere, governments used existing rules or soft law. China operated three CAC rules, on recommendation algorithms, deep synthesis and generative-AI services, and 302 generative-AI services had been filed with the regulator by the end of the year. The UK kept a principles-based approach with no AI statute. Enforcement came under existing law: the FCC imposed a $6 million forfeiture over an AI-cloned Biden robocall, and at the end of the year Italy's privacy regulator fined OpenAI €15 million, a fine later annulled. Across 75 countries, Stanford HAI counted 1,889 mentions of AI in legislative proceedings in 2024, up 21.3%, and 40 AI-related laws passed nationally. The picture at the end of 2024 was one binding comprehensive regime that had not yet started to apply, a fast-growing US state patchwork, and a Chinese system that had required pre-launch filing of public generative-AI services since 2023.

Frequently asked

How many people used ChatGPT in 2024?

OpenAI said ChatGPT had 300 million weekly active users in December 2024, up from 200 million in August 2024. These are weekly users and should not be compared with monthly-user counts for other assistants.

How much did big tech spend on AI infrastructure in 2024?

Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spent about $245 billion on capital expenditure in calendar 2024, by Report AI's sum of each company's reported figures. Most of it went to data centres and AI chips.

How much venture money went to AI companies in 2024?

Crunchbase counted more than $100 billion of venture funding to AI companies in 2024, about a third of the roughly $314 billion invested in start-ups worldwide that year.

What AI laws took effect in 2024?

The EU AI Act entered into force on 1 August 2024, with its obligations phased in from February 2025. In the US, state legislatures introduced 635 AI-related bills in 2024 and enacted 99, according to MultiState; there was no federal AI statute.

How many jobs did AI cut in 2024?

Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 12,742 US job cuts in 2024 that employers attributed to artificial intelligence, about 1.7% of the 761,358 cuts announced that year.

Methodology & sources

Figures are compiled from company disclosures, regulators, research firms and reputable reporting, and cross-checked against Report AI's own index pages. Each value carries its as-of date, source and a confidence rating: HIGH where the primary source was read, MEDIUM where it rests on consistent reputable secondary reporting (most figures, because many primary pages cannot be opened from our research environment), DERIVED where it is our own arithmetic, and CONTESTED where credible sources disagree. A year means the calendar year the figure describes; 2026 values are the latest available and marked “to date”. Weekly and monthly user counts, and forecasts and outcomes, are never compared as if they were the same measure. These reports compile and verify secondary data with original analysis; they are not original surveys. See our editorial standards, corrections log and fact-check register.
