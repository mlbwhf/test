( 01 ) — Why AI automation: the business need

Copilots speed up tasks. Automation removes the work.

A person plus a copilot is faster at each step — but the workflow, the handoffs, and the waiting are all still there. The step-change comes when the whole workflow runs itself: an agent picks up the request, does the work across systems, and only surfaces the exceptions for a human to judge. That is where cost, cycle time, and error rates fall together.

Task-level AI (people still operate)
✗Faster steps, but the same number of handoffs
✗Work waits in queues between people and systems
✗Throughput capped by headcount & working hours
✗Ungoverned scripts & bots — brittle, unauditable
✗Pilots that never reach production
Workflow-level automation (AI operates, people supervise)
✦End-to-end — request to result, across systems
✦Straight-through — the routine cases just complete
✦Exceptions to humans — judgment where it matters
✦Governed — guardrails, audit trails, security by design
✦Shipped — in production, not stuck in a pilot
( 02 ) — The automation ladder

From manual to agentic.

Not every workflow should be fully autonomous. We place each one at the right rung — and move it up only as trust, data, and guardrails allow.

RUNG 01

Manual

People do the work step by step, moving it between systems by hand.

RUNG 02

Assisted

Copilots and scripts speed up individual steps — the workflow is unchanged.

RUNG 03

Orchestrated

Steps are chained into a flow; the routine path runs straight through, humans handle exceptions.

RUNG 04
FRONTIER

Agentic

AI agents plan and run whole workflows end to end under human oversight, adapting as they go.

The goal isn’t “maximum autonomy” — it’s the right rung per workflow, with a human-in-the-loop where the stakes demand one.
