# Proposal: restructure the statistics into a citable "State of AI" series

Written 11 Nov 2026 in response to the owner's request: publish indexes and statistics reports
that news outlets can use and reference (AI usage, AI spend, State of AI, State of AI
financial, State of AI usage, as a series).

## The problem with the current structure

The site has ~150 pages organised by topic hub (9 index sections). That is good for search but
hard to cite: a journalist cannot say "according to Report AI's X report" because there is no
small set of named, dated, flagship reports. The numbers exist; the citable products do not.

## The proposal: seven named reports, one flagship, same template

Each report is a single permanent URL that always holds the latest edition, with dated
editions archived beneath it (the convention already used for AI Market Pulse).

## What makes each report quotable (the creative part)

1. **"The Five"**: every report opens with five headline numbers, each written as one
   self-contained sentence (figure + unit + date + source) that can be lifted into a news story.
2. **A "Cite this report" box** with the exact citation line, edition date and permanent URL.
3. **One signature chart** per report, published as an image with the Report AI mark, free to
   embed with attribution.
4. **What changed since last edition**: the table journalists actually want ("up from X").
5. **"Numbers to stop quoting"**: 2–3 fact-check entries for that topic, linking to the register.
6. **Data download** (CSV) and Dataset schema, as on the 45 existing pages.
7. **Press kit per edition**: 200-word press note, chart pack, 5–10 day social series.
8. **Edition numbering** ("State of AI Usage, Q4 2026 edition") so citations are unambiguous.

The existing topic pages are not deleted. They become the detail layer each report links
down to, and each links up to its report. The 9 index hubs map onto the 7 reports.

## Naming: one decision needed first

"State of AI" is a crowded name. **"State of AI Report"** is Air Street Capital's well-known
annual, and **"The State of AI"** is McKinsey's annual survey. A bare title "State of AI Report"
would repeat the problem we just fixed with "The AI Index" (Stanford's name): journalists and
AI systems would credit our figures to someone else.

Recommended: keep "State of AI" only with a qualifier and our name, e.g.
**"State of AI Usage 2026 — Report AI"**, and call the flagship **"State of AI in Numbers"**
(matching the tagline "AI, by the numbers"). The alternative is a wholly distinct series name
(for example "The Report AI Barometer: Usage / Spending / Finance …").

## Build order

1. **State of AI Usage** first: the usage figures were refreshed this week (ChatGPT 1B+ weekly,
   Gemini 1B+ monthly, Doubao 419M), and it is the most searched topic.
2. State of AI Spending and State of AI Finance next (the Pulse research covers both).
3. Flagship "State of AI in Numbers 2026" on 15 December, rolling up whatever is live.
4. Work, Models & Prices, Risk, Regulation in Q1 2027.

Site changes when the first report is live: a "Reports" row for the series on the homepage and
Reports hub, and the existing State of AI hub (362, the year-by-year timeline) becomes the
series landing page.

## Limits to state on every report

Most figures are MEDIUM confidence (primary pages are not reachable from the research
environment), and the reports are compiled and verified secondary data plus our analysis, not
original surveys, until the Readiness Assessment produces primary data.
