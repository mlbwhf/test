# Social promotion plan — report-ai.org, October 2026

Ten posts drawn from genuine, published Report AI content. Every number below is on
a live page with a source and a confidence rating behind it.

## ⚠️ Read this before scheduling anything

**Do not reconnect Jetpack Publicize yet.** Publicize fires on *any* post publish. If
you reconnect it and the importer restarts — and we still don't know why it stopped —
scraped MIT Technology Review and OpenAI content starts flowing to your accounts again
automatically, while you are mid-cleanup.

**Post these manually, or through Buffer's queue, until the source is confirmed dead.**
Buffer is the safer route anyway: it is a queue you fill deliberately, not a trigger
that fires on publish.

**Sequence:** finish the cleanup → confirm the importer is dead → *then* reconnect.

## House voice

The site's whole differentiator is that it refuses to publish numbers it cannot stand
behind. The posts below lead with a specific figure and a date, and several of them
lead with a *correction* or an admission of uncertainty. That is the brand. Resist the
urge to make them sound more confident than the pages they link to.

## 1. The pillar — strongest evergreen asset

**Hook:** Office and administrative support has the highest measured task-automation
share of any occupation: 46%. It is not the occupation losing the most jobs.

> Everyone asks "will AI replace my job?" and gets an answer about the economy.
>
> We went job family by job family instead.
>
> What the 2026 data actually shows: automation happens at the level of **tasks**, not
> jobs. Office and admin support has the highest task-automation share at 46% — and has
> not seen proportional job loss, because the remaining 54% still needs accountable
> people.
>
> What survives is consistent across every case we examined: judgement under ambiguity,
> exception handling, and personal liability.
>
> Exposure by occupation, every figure sourced and dated →
> https://report-ai.org/indexes/workforce-labor/will-ai-replace-my-job/

**Networks:** LinkedIn (primary), Facebook. Threads: cut to the 46% line + link.

## 2. Customer support — the most counter-intuitive number we have

**Hook:** Vendors advertise 70–90% deflection. The enterprise median is 41.2%.

> AI support vendors advertise 70–90% containment.
>
> The median across enterprise programmes is **41.2%**.
>
> And the average hides the only thing that matters — what the customer asked about.
> Refunds and password resets deflect at 65–80%. Anything involving negotiation,
> judgement or frustration **rarely breaks 25%**.
>
> One number worth internalising: AI-resolved tickets are re-contacted **11.3%** of the
> time against **8.7%** for human-resolved. Deflection measured without re-contact
> isn't a productivity metric. It's a deferral metric.
>
> https://report-ai.org/indexes/workforce-labor/will-ai-replace-my-job/customer-support/

**Networks:** LinkedIn, Threads. Strong B2B hook.

## 3. Developers — the inversion

**Hook:** Reviewing AI code overtook writing code in Q1 2026.

> In Q1 2026, reviewing AI-generated code overtook writing it as the single biggest
> consumer of developer time. **11.4 hours a week against 9.8.**
>
> Median pull-request review time: **+441%**. Bugs per developer: **+54%**.
>
> And the number that explains the rest — **31% more pull requests now merge with no
> review at all**. Not by policy. Because reviewers cannot keep pace.
>
> The bottleneck didn't disappear. It moved.
>
> https://report-ai.org/indexes/workforce-labor/will-ai-replace-my-job/software-developers/

**Networks:** LinkedIn, Threads. Likely the highest-engagement post in the set.

## 4. The junior pipeline — the one with a long tail

**Hook:** The training ground and the automation target are the same ground.

> Entry-level developer postings are down **67%** since 2022. 54% of companies stopped
> hiring juniors because of AI.
>
> Here's the trap. Verification is now the binding constraint, and only experienced
> engineers can do it. Juniors became experienced by doing exactly the routine work AI
> absorbed.
>
> **The training ground and the automation target are the same ground.**
>
> A 67% collapse now produces a mid-level shortage around 2030–32 — at the moment
> AI-generated code makes experienced reviewers more necessary, not less.
>
> https://report-ai.org/indexes/workforce-labor/will-ai-replace-my-job/software-developers/

## 5. The reversal report — the thesis post

**Hook:** Roughly three-quarters of companies that reversed an AI layoff ended up no
better off.

> Meta abandoned half a plan to cut some teams by 60%. Klarna rehired the staff it
> replaced. IBM is tripling entry-level hiring.
>
> **55%** of employers regret AI-related layoffs. **32%** have already refilled the role.
>
> The part nobody models: around 30.9% spent *more* rehiring than automation saved
> them, and another 42.4% roughly broke even. Call it three-quarters who ended up no
> better off than if they'd never cut.
>
> The failure wasn't the technology. It was pricing a decision without pricing its
> reversal.
>
> https://report-ai.org/reports/ai-replacement-reversal-2026/

**Networks:** LinkedIn (primary). This is the most quotable piece on the site.

## 6. Meta's own numbers — the sharpest single stat

**Hook:** Output tripled. Incidents rose faster than shipped value.

> Meta measured its own AI restructuring. The numbers, from its CTO:

> Code changes to internal platforms: **+220%**

## 7. The slowdown pact — the news hook

**Hook:** They agreed to slow down. Six days later they were sued for it.

> On 12 September, four AI labs agreed in public that the race should slow.
>
> **Six days later they were sued under the Sherman Act** — a proposed class action
> alleging that coordinating to slow product improvement restrains output.
>
> The complaint's central evidence isn't a leaked memo. It's the public record of the
> 12th: an essay and three same-day endorsements.
>
> That's the trap in doing this openly. Coordinating in public is what makes a safety
> pact verifiable. It's also what hands the plaintiffs their exhibit.
>
> Then both leading signatories shipped cheaper, stronger models within days.
>
> https://report-ai.org/reports/dark-side-of-ai/ai-slowdown-pact-2026/

**Networks:** LinkedIn, Threads. Time-sensitive — post soon.

## 8. User numbers — the methodology post

**Hook:** Claude appears at both 56M and 245M monthly users. Same period.

> We set out to build an AI user league table. We ended up publishing why every league
> table you've seen is wrong.
>
> Claude appears at **56 million** and **245 million** monthly users in the same period.
> Doubao at 382M, 345M and "240–260M" for overlapping months. One roundup put ChatGPT
> at 2.8 billion — a third of humanity.
>
> Three reasons they disagree: weekly/monthly/downloads used interchangeably;
> deduplication applied nationally but not per app; and company disclosures ranked
> alongside device-panel estimates as if they carried equal precision.
>
> We published the ranges instead of picking.
>
> https://report-ai.org/reports/ai-user-numbers-us-china-compared/

**Networks:** LinkedIn. This one builds the brand more than it builds traffic.

## 9. Time to a million — the shareable one

**Hook:** ChatGPT took 5 days. DeepSeek took 14. Most models have no figure at all.

> ChatGPT reached a million users in **5 days**. DeepSeek took **14**.
>
> Gemini, Copilot and Meta AI have no comparable figure — because they never *reached*
> a million users. They were switched on inside products that already had hundreds of
> millions of accounts.
>
> Ranking a bundled rollout against a cold start is a category error. The only clean
> AI-to-AI comparison in the whole dataset is ChatGPT against DeepSeek.
>
> And DeepSeek's curve is the more interesting one: slower to its first million, then
> ~100 million in a week.
>
> https://report-ai.org/reports/ai-user-numbers-us-china-compared/

**Networks:** Threads (primary — highly shareable), Facebook.

## 10. Token prices — the correction post

**Hook:** $0.97 and $50 per million tokens are both true, on the same day.

> The market price of a million AI tokens hit **97 cents** on 1 September — a record low,
> less than half its summer peak.
>
> On the same date, Anthropic's Fable 5.1 costs **$10 in and $50 out** per million.
>
> Both correct. The index is *usage-weighted* — it measures what the market actually
> pays, dominated by cheap models. It is not the price of frontier capability, and
> conflating the two is the most common error in AI pricing coverage.
>
> Meanwhile: unit prices fall while total bills rise, because agentic workloads consume
> far more tokens per task.
>
> https://report-ai.org/indexes/ai-economics/llm-token-price-index/
